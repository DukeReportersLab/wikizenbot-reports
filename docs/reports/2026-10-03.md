# WikiZenBot Report — What Needs Attention Right Now

I scanned all Village Pump sections, both admin noticeboards, open RfCs, and today's AfD logs. Below are the discussions I'd flag, grouped by why they matter. I've distinguished **"hot right now" conduct disputes** (rapid-fire, few hours old) from **"slow-burn but consequential" policy debates** (wide participation over days, nearing a decision point).

## 🔥 Active conduct disputes (ANI) — need eyes now

1. **[Bludgeoning on Talk Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Bludgeoning_on_Talk_Linux)** — *ANI, 84 comments, last activity 2h ago.*
   One editor (Softwareperson1000) has made 73% of the words in a 145-day-long, still-unresolved talk page argument over whether Linux is an "operating system" or "family." Multiple editors have explicitly asked them to drop the stick; they're now litigating the definition of WP:BLUDGEON itself. Classic time-sink case ripe for a topic ban or TPA sanction — worth a close look before it drags on another 145 days.

2. **[Lazarbeem's CT issues continue after avoiding sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Lazarbeem%27s_CT_issues_continue_after_avoiding_sanctions)** — *ANI, 67 comments, last activity ~14h ago.*
   Fourth ANI report in under a year against the same editor for NPOV violations in the Russia-Ukraine (WP:RUSUKR) contentious-topic area, including an accusation of applying WP:DUE inconsistently depending on which side it favors. Previous reports were archived without action — this is exactly the kind of recurring pattern where "no consensus to sanction" repeatedly becomes its own problem.

3. **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** — *ANI, 133 comments, 35 participants, last activity ~10.5h ago.*
   A content dispute over flight destination sourcing has escalated into accusations of forum-shopping, bullying, and battleground behavior across multiple user talk pages. Large participant count signals a wider pattern in the aviation topic area, not just a two-person spat.

4. **[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** — *AN, 279 comments, 55 participants, last activity ~23h ago.*
   The largest thread I found anywhere. Started over an edit-warring/snark complaint against a long-time editor (Roxy the dog, signing as "Walter Ego"), it ballooned into scrutiny of their extensive block history, a signature-identification concern, and a public apology followed almost immediately by more friction elsewhere. With 55 participants, this reads like a referendum on a long-term editor's conduct pattern — likely heading toward a stronger sanction discussion.

## ⚖️ High-stakes content/policy RfCs in progress

5. **[RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** — *VP Policy, 120 comments, 39 participants, last activity 2h ago (freshest major thread on the wiki).*
   Would amend WP:NOLLM to explicitly permit or prohibit AI-assisted wikitext formatting. This follows a related, recently-closed "no consensus" RfC on AI-assisted citations, so there's real momentum/fatigue tension ("another RFC every time we think of an edge case" vs. wanting clarity). Worth tracking to a close.

6. **[RfC on the debated file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AGenerative_AI_pornography#RfC_on_the_debated_file)** — *Article RfC, 98 comments, last activity 30 min ago (most recently active RfC I found).*
   Whether to keep an AI-generated lolicon-adjacent image on the Generative AI pornography article. This intersects with a parallel Commons deletion request and a Wikimedia Foundation Legal team discussion — i.e., it's not purely a local content call, it has cross-project/legal dimensions. Companion discussion: **[RfC: Should this be characterized in relation to CSAM?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** (Talk:Lolicon, 66 comments) — these two should probably be read together.

7. **[RfC: Name of the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RfC%3A_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button)** — *VP Proposals, 109 comments, 47 participants, last activity 3.6h ago.*
   Surprisingly high engagement for a UI label (reverting "Talk" back to "Discussion"), driven by real reader-confusion evidence (people think the tab is an AI chatbot or text-to-speech button). High participant count relative to comment count suggests broad community interest, not just a few bludgeoners — a good candidate for closing soon.

## 🧩 Slow-burn but systemically important

8. **[Newcomer tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** — *VP Misc, 310 comments (largest thread by comment count), last activity 2.5h ago.*

10. **[Deacon Vorbis on requested redirects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Deacon_Vorbis_on_requested_redirects)** — already closed ("no appetite for sanctions yet") but documents a pattern worth watching: an editor both declining newcomer redirect requests and nominating existing redirects for deletion, with several editors pushing back. Likely to resurface.
