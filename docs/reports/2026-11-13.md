# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI use, policy, and detection dominate the Village Pump (policy) board right now — seven separate threads there touch on large language models (LLMs), making this the single biggest cluster of activity.

**[Promote the large-language-model guideline to full policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (formal Request for Comment, 438 comments, 100 participants) — This is the most consequential discussion in today's scan. The guideline "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) is already enforced like a policy — editors are blocked and administrators have been removed from their position ("desysopped") for violating it — and this Request for Comment asks whether it should formally be labeled a policy rather than a guideline. With 100 participants weighing in, this is a wide-reaching, high-stakes discussion about how seriously Wikipedia treats its core rule against AI-written content; expect a close call on whether promoting it is "pointless relabeling" or an important clarity measure.

**["Microsoft Edge just turned into a rule-violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump policy page, 70 comments, 22 participants, still active) — An editor discovered that Microsoft's Edge browser now pops up built-in AI rewriting suggestions ("Polish this draft," "Make the tone more empathetic") directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, risking a flood of undisclosed AI-rewritten text from editors who don't even realize they've broken the rule against using AI output in articles. The discussion has turned into a fight over the existing carve-out that allows AI for "spelling and grammar" fixes — several editors now argue that loophole is being routinely abused and should be eliminated entirely.

**[Drafting a dedicated "AI agent" policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump policy page, 48 comments, 18 participants) — Prompted by a recent incident where an autonomous AI agent ("TomWikiAssist") was banned and publicly complained about censorship, an editor is proposing a short, unambiguous policy specifically naming AI agents (as distinct from human editors using AI tools), so that an agent reading Wikipedia's rules has no excuse for violating them. Still in the pre-proposal brainstorming stage but moving toward a future Request for Comment.

**[What to do with articles deleted for AI content when editors ask for them back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** (Village Pump policy page, 14 comments) — A practical enforcement gap: articles deleted through the "proposed deletion" process for being AI-generated (shortcut LLMPROD) are currently restored on request exactly like any other proposed deletion, with no safeguard against simply undeleting the same AI content into the main encyclopedia. Consensus is forming around restoring such pages only to draft space with a mandatory warning banner, rather than letting them straight back into articles.

**[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by a blocked editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard, 21 comments, 15 participants) — An editor who was indefinitely blocked for using AI-generated content and committing copyright violations had also personally approved 29 articles as "Good Articles" (a quality designation requiring independent review). Other editors have since found hallmarks of AI-generated prose (eerily repetitive phrasing, and in one case a flatly invented quotation) in articles that passed this reviewer's checks, including a Lady Gaga song article. The community is now discussing a coordinated reassessment of all of that reviewer's approvals — a clean-up effort with real reputational stakes for the Good Article system.

**[New editor pasting AI-generated articles directly into mainspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Hajiani%2C_Dr.Seyed_Firdawoosbee_Hanifdeen_and_AI_use)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, 9 comments, still active) — A representative, close-to-real-time case: an editor using two apparent accounts has been appending AI-generated paragraphs to existing articles and, more seriously, creating brand-new AI-written articles straight into mainspace, continuing even after repeated warnings. The user's own explanatory reply (in a very different writing register than the "polished" articles) suggests they may not understand what the problem is — a recurring competence-versus-bad-faith dilemma in AI enforcement.

**["JaredMcKenzie's behaviors"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, 29 comments, 10 participants) — An editor already topic-banned once partly for using AI-generated talk-page comments is accused of adding a quotation to the Falun Gong article that was actually fabricated by Google's Gemini chatbot and falsely attributed to a named scholar's book, then denying having added it at all when challenged months later. The report also documents heavy "bludgeoning" (dominating discussions with repeated lengthy replies) in the same topic area — a pattern worth watching since it combines AI fabrication with broader conduct problems.

**Other AI threads worth a shorter look:**
- [Should the "what Wikipedia is not" policy page explicitly mention LLMs?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F) (Village Pump policy, 22 comments) — debating whether AI-generated content needs its own callout in that foundational policy.
- [Are policies about AI use clear enough to new editors?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F) (Village Pump policy, 18 comments) — a fairly introspective discussion about newcomer-facing documentation.
- [Should Wikipedia build its own AI-assisted search feature?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search) (Idea lab, 82 comments, 25 participants) — a proposal to add an AI button to Wikipedia's own search box to compete with chatbots, drawing both enthusiasm and skepticism about cost and hallucination risk.
- ["AI-powered drafting and sealioning"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI-powered_drafting_and_sealioning) and ["Unsourced, neutral point of view (NPOV) edits, LLM usage, biographies of living persons (BLP) violations (part 2)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Unsourced%2C_NPOV_edits%2C_LLM_usage%2C_BLP_violations_%28part_2%29) — two smaller, fresh Incidents noticeboard reports accusing specific editors of using AI to generate both article text and argumentative talk-page walls of text.
- Two Articles for Deletion debates — [Mizanur Rahman Azhari (3rd nomination)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Mizanur_Rahman_Azhari_%283rd_nomination%29) and [Bosniakisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Bosniakisation) — where participants used an AI-detection tool and stylistic tells to argue that article text (and in one case, a deletion nomination itself) was written by a chatbot, showing how AI-detection arguments are now routinely entering ordinary deletion debates.
- Two unban/unblock appeals at the Administrators' noticeboard, [CarnaticHistoryEditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_request_from_CarnaticHistoryEditor) and [Sbb1413](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unban_request_from_Sbb1413), both involve past findings of AI misuse as part of the sanction being appealed.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RfC: Should South Asian cinema become a contentious topic area?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump proposals, 232 comments, 48 participants, still very active) — The largest discussion in today's scan by far. It would place all South Asian film articles under formal "general sanctions," similar to restrictions already in place for caste and Indian military history topics, in response to persistent nationalist/caste-motivated edit warring — and, participants note, a wave of low-quality AI-generated "slop" content in the topic area. With four competing restriction options on the table and steady fresh comments, a decision here could reshape editing rules across an entire subject area.

**[Should a new "page mover" move-protection level be created?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump proposals, 131 comments, 37 participants) — A technical governance proposal to add a protection tier between regular extended-confirmed protection and full administrator-only protection, letting trusted "page mover" volunteers move contentious page titles without waiting on an administrator. Wide, substantive participation suggests this is close to becoming policy, though some object it could backfire by making protections stricter overall.

**[Alleged hounding by Sirfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, 95 comments, 27 participants) — A year-long dispute in which one editor accuses another of systematically tracking and reverting their work on politically sensitive navigation templates (covering subjects like the second Trump administration and the Gaza war). It's one of the most heavily commented behavioral disputes currently open, with real risk of sanctions for one or both parties.

**[RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** (open Request for Comment, 85 comments, 26 participants) — A sensitive naming question about a soldier charged in connection with the 1972 Bloody Sunday killings in Northern Ireland, balancing privacy for biographies of living persons (BLP) against historical documentation. High participation on a topic with lasting real-world sensitivity.

**[RfC on the notability proposal adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RFC_on_new_notability_proposal_adoption)** (Village Pump policy, 68 comments, actively getting new comments within the last hour) — A live debate over adopting a new general notability standard, worth watching simply because it's moving in real time.

**AfD note:** Today's deletion logs are mostly routine (new-page patrol, schools, minor topics) with no unusually large pile-ons — the main exception being the AI-flagged debates mentioned above.

A clear theme across today's venues: enforcement of the rules against AI-generated content has moved from the drafting stage into messy real-world application — contested Good Article reviews, fabricated quotations, browser-level AI nudges, and deletion-restoration loopholes are all now live problems rather than hypotheticals, while the community simultaneously debates whether to harden the underlying rule into a formal policy.
