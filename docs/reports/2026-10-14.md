# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI governance continues to dominate Wikipedia's internal discussion venues, with activity spanning formal policy-making, noticeboard enforcement, and product proposals. A clear pattern: the community keeps tightening the screws on large language model (LLM) use, while individual enforcement cases pile up faster than the "AI noticeboard" can process them.

### Policy and guideline fights

- **[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — The single biggest AI discussion right now: 147 comments, 43 editors, still active. It asks whether the no-large-language-model guideline (shortcut: NOLLM) should be explicitly amended to permit or prohibit using AI to generate or format raw Wikipedia markup ("wikitext") such as citations and tables. Early replies lean toward keeping the status quo (ambiguous) or tightening restrictions; several editors complain this is the second or third RfC rehashing the same underlying dispute.
- **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — A fresher, still-growing thread (last reply 20 hours ago) asking whether the guideline on AI use in talk-page comments (shortcut: LLMTALK) should be tightened to match the stricter article-content rule, given that enforcement practice at the Incidents noticeboard has already moved that way informally.
- **[Permit usage of LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions)** (Village Pump, policy) — A proposal to loosen the rules for AI-written user pages and image captions was shut down within days ("this is going nowhere," per the editor who closed it), with blunt pushback like "stop trying to undermine the clear consensus banning them." A useful temperature check on just how little appetite exists for loosening AI restrictions anywhere on the site.
- **[Onus for maintenance templates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Onus_for_maintenance_templates)** (Village Pump, policy) — A practical dispute with real implications for AI cleanup: when an editor tags an article as containing AI-generated text, who must do the work of identifying exactly which passages are suspect? Eighty-plus comments debate whether AI-cleanup taggers can reasonably decline to pinpoint problems article-by-article given the scale of the problem, versus the principle that unexplained tags should eventually be removable.

### Enforcement cases on the incident noticeboards

- **[Up to 400 recent AfDs may contain AI-generated votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Up_to_400_recent_AfDs_may_contain_AI-generated_%21votes)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — The most urgent item in this report: a single editor is suspected of using AI to generate "delete/keep" opinions across as many as 400 Articles for Deletion (AfD) discussions, posting at a mechanical 3–6 minute cadence for eight straight hours and apparently evaluating sources in languages they don't speak. The thread is brand new (last comment under an hour ago) and directly threatens the integrity of the deletion process; expect rapid follow-up as admins decide whether to mass-revert the votes.
- **[Dtf1978 AI and lying about it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Dtf1978_AI_and_lying_about_it)** (Incidents) — Already resolved: an editor was indefinitely blocked, and had their "extended confirmed" editing permission revoked, after posting obviously AI-generated talk-page messages and then denying AI use even after being warned. A clean example of how quickly enforcement now moves once AI-generated text is identified.
- **["Itsekiri writing" disruptive behavior, edit warring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User_%22Itsekiri_writing%22_disruptive_behavior%2C_removal_of_sourced_content_and_edit_warring)** and **[BrowseTQ Battlegrounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT)** (both Incidents) — Two live disputes illustrating the fuzzy line editors keep probing: one editor insists their AI use was merely "refining" grammar (permitted) rather than generating content (forbidden), while in the other, a promotional draft article is shown to be stitched together from text generated by different chatbots across different years, identifiable by era-specific AI writing tics.
- **[Purvanchal bhojpuriya, Jha2 Chhiyanchali: edit warring, likely AI usage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Purvanchal_bhojpuriya%2C_Jha2_Chhiyanchali%3A_edit_warring%2C_likely_AI_usage)** (Incidents) — A smaller, very fresh report in the same vein, worth watching as it develops.

### Proposals and tools

- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict-of-interest edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Less a dispute than a data point worth flagging for a book on AI's impact: a volunteer who handles requests from editors with a declared conflict of interest ("COI") published monthly statistics showing new requests more than doubling in 2026 versus 2025, which they attribute directly to AI making it easier for paid/COI editors to generate "surficially passable" requests — swamping the volunteer review queue.
- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A currently very active brainstorm (last comment minutes ago) proposing an official AI-powered natural-language search feature for Wikipedia, intended to keep readers on-site rather than losing traffic to general chatbots — with strict guardrails requiring any AI summary to be strictly grounded in, and clearly sourced to, actual Wikipedia text.
- **[Bot proposal: automatic addition of infoboxes for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A bot proposal that would use a large language model (Claude Opus) internally to decide, with high confidence, whether a company article is safe to auto-format — drawing pushback that "throwing a large language model into a contentious topic area seems like a bad idea," even though the bot's actual edits would only reformat existing text.
- **[Should admins be running the AI Noticeboard?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Should_admins_be_running_AI_Noticeboard%3F)** (idea lab) — A governance discussion about the relatively new noticeboard dedicated to AI-content cleanup (shortcut: AINB), with most respondents arguing it needs more volunteer hands rather than administrator oversight specifically.
- **[RfC on the debated file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AGenerative_AI_pornography#RfC_on_the_debated_file)** (Talk:Generative AI pornography) — A 121-comment Request for Comment over whether an AI-generated sexualized image should illustrate the article about generative AI pornography at all, touching on Wikipedia's rules against gratuitous offensive content and overlapping with a parallel debate about similar imagery on the "Lolicon" article.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (Incidents) — A sprawling, still-growing conduct dispute (200+ comments, 42 participants, active within the last hour) in the aviation topic area, escalating from a content disagreement about unsourced flight-destination data into questions about incivility and a long block history. This is a slow-burn, chronic problem rather than a single urgent incident — worth noting as a different kind of "busy" than a fast-moving vote-stacking case.
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — 144 comments and still very active (last comment within the past 8 hours); a naming-convention RfC with broad participation.
- **[RfC: Name of the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RfC%3A_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button)** (Village Pump, proposals) — 124 comments, 48 participants; a seemingly small interface-wording question that has drawn unusually wide participation.
- **[Request for Comment: should "Lolicon" be characterized in relation to child sexual abuse material?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** — A substantial (101-comment), sensitive content-classification dispute running in parallel with the generative-AI-pornography discussion above.
- **[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — By far the largest thread scanned (317 comments, 65 participants), an interpersonal civility dispute between editors on textile-related articles that escalated to the main administrators' noticeboard; size suggests high community interest even though the underlying issue is a fairly ordinary conduct complaint.

**Pattern to note:** across both the AI-specific and general discussions, a recurring theme is process strain — volunteer queues (conflict-of-interest requests, AI-content cleanup, deletion discussions) are being overwhelmed by rising volume, and much of the debate is really about who bears the burden of verification when something might be AI-generated, rather than whether AI content is acceptable at all (on which there is already strong consensus: it mostly isn't).
