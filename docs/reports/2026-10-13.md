# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-Related Discussions

AI policy enforcement and the boundaries of permitted AI use dominate the policy venues right now, with several interlocking threads at Village Pump (policy) all chewing on different corners of the same question: when exactly is AI assistance allowed on Wikipedia?

- **[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — The biggest and most contentious AI thread right now: 147 comments from 43 editors, still active as of 37 hours ago. It's a formal Request for Comment (a structured community vote to establish consensus) asking whether the guideline against using large language models, or LLMs (the technology behind chatbots like ChatGPT), to write articles (shortcut "NOLLM") should be explicitly amended to permit or prohibit using AI specifically to generate or format Wikipedia's underlying markup code ("wikitext"). The discussion got off to a rocky start — an administrator initially stripped the "RfC" label because the process hadn't been followed correctly — and participants are split between leaving the guideline alone, carving out a narrow permission with human-review safeguards, or writing in an explicit ban.

- **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy section) — The single most active AI discussion at this moment, with the last comment posted only an hour ago. It asks whether the guideline on AI use in talk-page comments ("LLMTALK") should flatly ban AI-written comments the same way article content is banned, or whether its vaguer allowance for "AI-refined" comments should stand. Several editors argue the current wording is being exploited as a loophole, while others note that AI-assisted translation has a clear, pre-existing community endorsement that can't simply be erased.

- **[Onus for maintenance templates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Onus_for_maintenance_templates)** (Village Pump, policy section) — 84 comments sparked by a real dispute: an editor tagged an article as containing AI-generated text but refused to say which passages were the problem, telling others to "use a tool and figure it out" (despite policy explicitly saying detection tools shouldn't be relied on this way). The resulting debate — who bears the burden of justifying a maintenance tag? — is really about how workable the AI-cleanup process is in practice.

- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict-of-interest edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Less a dispute than a striking data point: a volunteer who reviews requests from editors with a declared conflict of interest (COI), submitted on article talk pages, shared statistics showing a near-doubling of monthly request volume in 2026 versus 2025, with August and September setting all-time records. They explicitly attribute the surge to AI making it cheap to generate "surficially passable" edit requests, warning this is "not a short-term blip." This is one of the more concrete, number-backed illustrations of AI's effect on Wikipedia's maintenance workload.

- **[RfC on the debated file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AGenerative_AI_pornography#RfC_on_the_debated_file)** (Talk:Generative AI pornography) — A heated, 121-comment formal Request for Comment over whether to keep an AI-generated (Stable Diffusion) screenshot in the article, illustrating how open-source AI image generators lack the content "guardrails" that commercial ones have. The dispute is tangled up with a parallel Request for Comment at Talk:Lolicon over whether similar content should be characterized in relation to material depicting child sexual abuse, and with a live discussion at the Wikimedia Foundation's legal team forum — making it a genuinely high-stakes content and legal question, not just a style dispute.

- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A proposal, still drawing comments as of 5 hours ago, to give Wikipedia's own search box an AI button that would use a large language model restricted to Wikimedia content to answer natural-language queries. Supporters frame it as a way to stop readers drifting to commercial chatbots; the immediate pushback centers on cost, redundancy with existing chatbots, and hallucination risk.

- **[Permit usage of LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions)** (Village Pump, policy section) — Already closed as "going nowhere" after a swift and largely hostile response. Worth noting for what it reveals: proposals to loosen the no-LLM rule even slightly (here, for personal user pages and photo captions) are being shut down almost unanimously, sometimes with blunt language about "techbro talking points."

- **[Bot proposal: automatic addition of infobox for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A bot proposal to auto-add standard infoboxes to roughly 12,000 company articles, using a large language model (Claude Opus 5.5) purely as an internal safety check to flag uncertain cases for human review. Still drawing comments 21 hours in; some editors object on principle to putting an LLM anywhere inside automated editing, even in this narrow "quality control" role.

- **Enforcement snapshot — three quick cases at the Incidents noticeboard:** **[Dtf1978 AI and lying about it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Dtf1978_AI_and_lying_about_it)** (editor indefinitely blocked within about 20 minutes of the report for posting AI-generated talk-page comments and falsely denying it, and separately stripped of extended-confirmed editing rights for gaming the system), **[Pointbreak47 — AI use after repeated warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Pointbreak47_--_AI_use_after_repeated_warnings)** (also indeffed within minutes after a fourth warning), and **[Behaviour needs attention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Behaviour_needs_attention_-_ongoing_at_the_time_of_report_initiation)** (a messier dispute where a new editor's mass-draftification of another user's articles, labeled "machine generated," is itself undercut by that editor's own suspiciously fluent, AI-sounding edit summaries, tangled with sockpuppetry suspicions). Together these show how routine AI-use accusations have become in day-to-day conduct enforcement — these are quick, low-drama cases compared to the slow policy RfCs above.

- **[Should admins be running AI Noticeboard?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Should_admins_be_running_AI_Noticeboard%3F)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — Stalled for 13 days, but a useful meta-discussion on whether the volunteer-run noticeboard that handles AI-content cleanup needs more administrator oversight. Consensus leans toward "no, it just needs more volunteer hands," not more admin tools.

- Briefly: **[Nexos.ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Nexos.ai)** is a quiet, low-comment Articles for Deletion debate about an AI startup that appears to fail the notability guideline for companies — unremarkable on its own, but a reminder of how many new AI-company articles are flowing through deletion review.

## Other Discussions Worth Attention

- **[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — By far the largest thread in this report: 312 comments from 64 participants. What began as a content dispute over fiber-related articles ballooned into complaints about one editor's "snarky" and abrasive conduct, an apology and partial withdrawal, and then a sub-debate about whether that editor's confusing signature itself needs fixing. This is a slow-burning, sprawling dispute (comments spread over days) rather than a fast-moving one — a different kind of "big" than a rapid pile-on.

- **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (Incidents noticeboard) — 197 comments, 41 participants, over unreliable sourcing (an airline-schedule aggregator) and alleged forum-shopping in airport-related articles. Explicitly flagged by participants as part of a recurring pattern of disruption in Wikipedia's aviation topic area.

- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy section) — 103 comments and still getting replies as of this report. A naming-convention Request for Comment that's drawn wide participation and looks to be approaching a decision point.

- **[RfC: Name of the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RfC%3A_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A surprisingly contentious interface-wording question (124 comments, 48 participants) about what to call the tab that leads to an article's talk page — a reminder that even small user-interface changes can generate large debates.

- **[TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A live conflict-of-interest case (last comment just 30 minutes ago) proposing a topic ban, or "TBAN," for an academic editor with industry funding ties who has spent years editing articles on polyphenols and flavonoids related to his own research and sponsors.

A pattern across several venues: AI is no longer a niche topic at Village Pump policy — it's generating more simultaneous, overlapping guideline debates (wikitext formatting, talk-page comments, maintenance tags, user pages) than almost any other subject, while on the noticeboards, AI-use accusations have become a routine, fast-resolving category of conduct complaint rather than a novel one.
