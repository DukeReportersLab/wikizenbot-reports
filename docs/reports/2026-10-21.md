# English Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

Artificial intelligence dominates the discussion boards right now — there's a major policy debate underway, a flurry of individual enforcement cases, and even a proposal to put AI search features directly into Wikipedia.

### The central policy fight: should AI be allowed to generate or format wiki markup?

The **[Request for Comment on permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) is the single biggest AI discussion happening: 152 comments from 46 editors in just over a month, with three competing options — leave the no-large-language-model guideline (nicknamed "NOLLM") unchanged, explicitly *permit* AI-assisted formatting of wikitext (the underlying markup language articles are written in) as long as it's error-free, or explicitly *prohibit* it outright. Early comments already split along a familiar fault line: editors who want crisp, enforceable rules versus those who worry that "every edge case becomes its own RfC." Given the volume of individual AI-misuse cases below, the outcome of this RfC will directly shape how those are handled going forward.

A closely related, smaller thread, **["The status of LLMTALK"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)**, is trying to resolve a separate but overlapping ambiguity: the guideline on AI use in talk-page comments (LLMTALK) seems to allow "AI-refined" comments, while the article-content guideline (NOLLM) is closer to an outright ban. Editors are debating whether "refined" should be narrowed to mean only correction or translation, and whether translated comments (long allowed) create an unavoidable exception to any blanket ban. A third thread, **["Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/large language model (LLM) are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)**, shows the same community groping toward a single clear reference point rather than scattered, inconsistent guidance — a pattern worth watching since it suggests the policy is currently too fragmented to enforce consistently.

### A constructive proposal: AI-powered search

In the idea incubation forum, **["AI-assisted search"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (66 comments) proposes the opposite of a ban: an opt-in AI search button, run on Wikimedia Foundation infrastructure with freely-licensed software, that would answer natural-language queries by pulling together and citing only Wikimedia content — explicitly framed as a response to Wikipedia "losing visits" to general-purpose chatbots. Early responses are skeptical about cost and necessity (why not just tell a chatbot to only use Wikipedia sources?), but it's a notable sign that some editors want Wikipedia to compete with AI tools rather than just defend against them.

### Data point: AI is driving a conflict-of-interest backlog

At the Administrators' noticeboard, **["Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (68 comments) started as a routine call for volunteers but turned into a striking data-driven analysis: one editor tracked monthly conflict-of-interest (COI) edit-request volume back to 2012 and found it's roughly tripled in 2026 alone (737 new requests in August versus a historical monthly average under 200), with the editor attributing the surge specifically to AI-generated requests becoming more convincing and more numerous. This is a concrete, measurable sign of AI's structural effect on Wikipedia's volunteer workload, not just an anecdote.

### A new venue: the AI noticeboard

Quietly announced on the Administrators' noticeboard, **["AI noticeboard overhaul"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#AI_noticeboard_overhaul)** notes that the dedicated Wikipedia:AI noticeboard — a relatively new venue specifically for reporting suspected AI misuse — has been "streamlined." This matters because several of the enforcement cases below are now being filed there instead of at the general incident noticeboard, suggesting the community is starting to triage AI cases through a specialized channel.

### A wave of individual enforcement cases

The incident noticeboard currently has an unusually large cluster of reports alleging AI misuse by individual editors:

- **[Spam and possible AI bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (Administrators' noticeboard, 61 comments, 32 participants) is the most serious: an actual administrator, who discloses being the academic David M. Levinson, added roughly 150 self-citations to his own journals across articles, some batches just minutes apart, raising both conflict-of-interest and possible-automation concerns. Several editors are now calling for him to resign his administrator tools, and a separate **[recall petition against DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ADavidLevinson%7CDavidLevinson%5D%5D)** has already been opened — a real-consequence case worth following to its conclusion.
- **[Creation of articles using AI which are not properly sourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Creation_of_articles_using_AI_which_are_not_properly_sourced%2C_linguistic_CIR%2C_inability_to_recognise_reliable_sources_and_deliberate_avoidance_of_AfC)**: an editor mass-produced AI-templated articles on Indian villages sourced to unreliable census-scraper websites, kept moving them back into the encyclopedia after they'd been sent back to draft space, and was ultimately partial-blocked from articles and page creation during this very discussion — a rare case where the report and the sanction happened within hours of each other.
- **[User:FavCourtier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3AFavCourtier)** (45 comments, 24 participants): large numbers of undisclosed AI-assisted drafts about Holocaust-related topics, followed by a long, emotionally charged reply from the user invoking family hardship and implied threats of government intervention — now also tracked on the new AI noticeboard.
- **[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)**: a 13-year veteran editor accused of pasting raw, unedited chatbot output — including text meant for the user, not the article — directly into election articles.
- Smaller but similar reports: **[Martinpri, blocked for AI, promised to stop, didn't](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Martinpri_-_blocked_for_AI%2C_said_they_wouldn%27t_use_AI_anymore%2C_you%27ll_never_guess_what_happened_next)**, **[Purvanchal bhojpuriya/Jha2 Chhiyanchali: edit warring, likely AI usage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Purvanchal_bhojpuriya%2C_Jha2_Chhiyanchali%3A_edit_warring%2C_likely_AI_usage)**, and **[Tran tuan 2511: LLM use and failure to engage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Tran_tuan_2511%3A_LLM_use_and_NOENGAGE)** (the last referencing the guideline against ignoring other editors' attempts at discussion).

Taken together, this cluster suggests AI-generated or AI-assisted low-quality content has become one of the single most common reasons editors end up at the incident noticeboard this cycle.

### AI inside a bot proposal

In a different register, the **[bot proposal for automatically adding infoboxes to company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (70 comments, 28 participants) would use a large language model (Claude Opus) as a filter to flag any edit the model isn't fully confident about for human review. Several editors are uneasy about putting an AI judgment call inside bot logic for a topic area already flagged as a contentious topic, even though the proposer stresses the bot would add no new information, only reformat what's already in the article.

## Other discussions worth attention

### Closed with a site ban: a month-long dispute resolves
**[Talk: Fiber (Administrators' noticeboard)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** — despite the odd heading, this 321-comment, 66-participant thread was a month-long conduct case against the long-time editor Roxy the dog, triggered by repeated snarky and abrasive talk-page comments. It has just been closed with a community **site ban**, the maximum sanction short of an Arbitration Committee case — a good example of how a seemingly minor civility complaint can escalate into a project-ending outcome when a pattern of behavior accumulates.

### Live and fast-moving right now
**[RfC: How should the Hindu American Foundation be characterized in the opening sentence?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AHindu_American_Foundation#RFC%3A_How_should_the_Hindu_American_Foundation_be_characterized_in_the_opening_sentence_of_the_lead%3F)** — 82 comments and still getting replies within the last 15 minutes. Editors are weighing scholarly sources that call the group "Hindutva" (a Hindu-nationalist political ideology) against others who think that framing is undue; a genuinely live content dispute with real-time back-and-forth, not just a slow accumulation of opinions.

**[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** — 202 comments and still active (last comment 30 minutes ago). A naming-convention dispute that sounds dry but has drawn sustained, high-volume participation, suggesting real disagreement about a rule that would affect thousands of article titles.

### Conduct cases with real sanctions on the table
**[TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** — a researcher with an admitted industry conflict of interest (research funded by a confectionery company) is facing a proposed topic ban from the nutrition-science articles he has single-purpose-edited since 2016 under two different accounts.

**[Swoonfed personal attacks and aspersions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Swoonfed_personal_attacks_and_aspersions)** and **[BrowseTQ Battlegrounding & IDHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT)** (the latter named for "I didn't hear that," the essay about ignoring consensus) are both large, high-participant incident reports with noticeable AI-related mentions woven through the disputes — worth a look if you want to see how accusations of AI use are now surfacing even in otherwise unrelated behavioral disputes.

### Sensitive content-policy debate
**[RfC: Should this be characterized in relation to CSAM?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** on the Lolicon article talk page (109 comments, 24 participants) is a long-running, sensitive dispute over whether to describe a genre of art in relation to child sexual abuse material (CSAM) in Wikipedia's own voice — a debate with real reputational and legal stakes for how the encyclopedia characterizes the topic.

### A structural concern about newcomer retention
**["Newcomer tasks"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** (Village Pump miscellaneous section) is enormous — 356 comments, 31 participants, and still active — with multiple editors independently reporting that the automated "newcomer task" suggestion system is pointing new editors toward articles far beyond their ability to improve, generating unhelpful or nonsensical edits that experienced editors then have to clean up. It's less about AI policy directly and more a cautionary tale about automated/algorithmic systems producing plausible-looking but low-quality suggestions at scale — a theme that echoes the AI-misuse cases above even though this particular tool isn't itself a large language model.
