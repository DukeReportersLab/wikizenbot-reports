# WikiZenBot Daily Report

## AI-related discussions

This is an unusually busy day for AI governance on Wikipedia, with debates ranging from formal policy-making to a string of enforcement cases.

**[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — With 152 comments from 46 editors, this is the single largest live AI discussion. It asks whether the guideline on AI-generated content (nicknamed "NOLLM") should be explicitly amended to permit or prohibit using AI tools to format the raw markup ("wikitext") of articles — things like citation templates — as opposed to writing prose. Early comments show a split between editors who want a crisp rule ("just say no to AI formatting") and those who think adding more clauses only invites more loophole-hunting. This directly follows a related citation-formatting debate that recently closed with no consensus, so a lot is riding on whether this attempt produces a clearer answer.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A brainstorming thread proposing that Wikipedia build its own AI-powered natural-language search tool, strictly scoped to surfacing and summarizing existing Wikipedia/Wikimedia content (not generating new facts), partly as a response to the site losing visits to chatbots. It's drawing active debate (69 comments, 20 participants, last comment under an hour old) over cost, redundancy with existing chatbots, and whether this compromises Wikipedia's "no AI-generated content" ethos even as a search aid.

**[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** and **[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/large language model (LLM) are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** (both Village Pump, policy) — Two related threads grappling with the fuzziness of current AI rules: one asks whether the guideline on AI use in talk-page comments ("LLMTALK") should be tightened to match the stricter rule for article content, since it currently allows vaguely-defined "AI-refined" comments; the other is a practical attempt to map out which AI uses (e.g., using an AI to search huge PDFs for a phrase, or to find candidate sources) are safely allowed versus genuinely contested. Both show the community still lacks a clear, teachable line between acceptable AI assistance and prohibited AI generation.

**A wave of AI enforcement cases**, several converging on the Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI) and the relatively new, recently-overhauled [Wikipedia:AI noticeboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#AI_noticeboard_overhaul) dedicated specifically to AI-conduct complaints:
- **[Spam and possible AI bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (administrators' noticeboard) — An administrator and academic was found to have added roughly 150 self-citing references to his own journals across articles, including bursts of edits minutes apart suggesting automation. The community pushed for him to resign his administrator tools, and separately, an [administrator recall petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ADavidLevinson%7CDavidLevinson%5D%5D) against him has already gathered the 25 supports needed to force a re-confirmation vote — a real, consequential accountability mechanism now in motion.
- **[User:FavCourtier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3AFavCourtier)** — Closed with a community ban after a bizarre saga involving mass AI-assisted draft submissions about Holocaust-related topics, accompanied by emotionally charged, conspiratorial replies from the user.
- **[BrowseTQ Battlegrounding & IDHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT)** — Closed with an indefinite block; the underlying dispute was over a draft article later shown to be "Frankensteined" together from text generated by different AI chatbots at different times.
- **["Creation of articles using AI which are not properly sourced..."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Creation_of_articles_using_AI_which_are_not_properly_sourced%2C_linguistic_CIR%2C_inability_to_recognise_reliable_sources_and_deliberate_avoidance_of_AfC)** — A user mass-created articles on Indian villages using an AI-based template with unreliable sources, and continued after being told to stop; now partially blocked from creating or moving articles into the main encyclopedia.
- **[Martinpri - blocked for AI, said they wouldn't use AI anymore, you'll never guess what happened next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Martinpri_-_blocked_for_AI%2C_said_they_wouldn%27t_use_AI_anymore%2C_you%27ll_never_guess_what_happened_next)** — A user who was unblocked after promising to stop using AI for sourcing was caught doing exactly that a week later (an added citation literally retained a "utm_source=chatgpt.com" tracking parameter); now indefinitely blocked.
- **[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)** — Still open; a long-time editor is accused of repeatedly pasting raw, unreviewed chatbot output (including the bot's own conversational text) directly into election articles.

Taken together, these cases show the newly reorganized AI noticeboard is already functioning as a real enforcement venue, and that "I used AI but promise to stop" is not landing well with administrators.

A lighter but telling AI-adjacent data point: in the **[RfC on renaming the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RfC%3A_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button)** (125 comments), a cited reason for reverting the tab's label back to "Talk" is that readers increasingly assume a button labeled "Discussion" leads to an AI chatbot — a small but striking sign of how thoroughly AI assumptions have permeated ordinary readers' expectations of websites.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A substantial, still-active proposal (102 comments, 34 participants, last reply just 14 hours ago) to create a new page-protection level restricted to the "page mover" user right, aimed at reducing disruptive page-move wars. Wide participation suggests a real chance of a policy change.

- **["More airport disruption"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — The noticeboard's biggest live thread (269 comments, 50 participants, still active). It's a sprawling conduct dispute in the aviation-topic area over unsourced "flightcruft" additions to airport articles, escalating into accusations of forum-shopping and incivility between several editors. This is a slow-burning, high-volume dispute rather than a single urgent flashpoint, but its size signals a recurring problem area.

- **[TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** (ANI) — A proposed topic ban for a researcher with an admitted industry conflict of interest (funded by a confectionery company) over polyphenol/health-claim articles, who was previously blocked and unblocked under similar circumstances under a different username. A clear-cut conflict-of-interest dispute with real sourcing and neutrality stakes for health-related science content.

- **[RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday article name Soldier F?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** (article talk page) — A fresh, fast-moving RfC (68 comments, last reply minutes old) on whether to name a British soldier implicated in the 1972 Bloody Sunday killings in Northern Ireland — a sensitive biographies-of-living-persons question with historical and legal weight.

- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — A large, slow-moving style RfC (207 comments, 28 participants over many days) about how to disambiguate school article titles. Lower drama than the items above, but notable for its sheer scale and the number of editors weighing in on a naming-convention question.

**Pattern to note:** across both AI-specific threads and general conduct noticeboards, a recurring theme today is the gap between written policy and enforcement practice — editors keep asking "is this specific AI use actually against the rules?" while admins are simultaneously handing out blocks and bans for AI-related misconduct in real time, often faster than the policy discussions can resolve the ambiguity.
