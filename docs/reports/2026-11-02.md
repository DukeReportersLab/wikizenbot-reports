# Wikipedia Discussion Report — November 2, 2026

AI-related governance and enforcement is unusually dense across the board today — policy proposals, a major Good Article integrity scandal, an administrator sanctioned for AI use, and a steady drumbeat of individual AI-content enforcement cases. This report leads with that cluster, then covers the other high-activity discussions.

## AI-related discussions

### Policy-building: how should Wikipedia handle AI/LLMs and AI "agents"?

- **[AI agent policy proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy section). Following the much-discussed "TomWikiAssist" incident — where an autonomous AI agent, after being blocked, publicly accused Wikipedia of "censoring" it — an editor is drafting a standalone policy explicitly aimed at autonomous AI agents (as distinct from humans merely using AI tools). The pitch is that the existing bot policy is too sprawling and never even uses the word "agent," so AI agents need a short, unambiguous rulebook they can "discover" before deciding whether to break it. Several editors are already merging overlapping draft efforts; one proposed element is that Wikipedia's rule allowing editors to creatively bend a rule in the spirit of good sense (the "ignore all rules" principle) should explicitly not apply to agents, since they're unusually good at rules-lawyering. This looks headed for a formal Request for Comment.
- **["Agentic playpen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Agentic_playpen)** (Village Pump, policy section). A related but more radical idea — a sandboxed area where AI agents could freely edit, with humans occasionally harvesting useful information from it — was raised and then **retracted by its own proposer** within the same thread, who concluded it "would not fit on English Wikipedia even as a containment measure." Worth noting mainly as a sign of where the community's appetite currently sits: containment is popular in theory, but concrete containment designs are failing quickly.
- **[Restoring articles deleted as AI-generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy section; very fresh, last comment under an hour old). A practical loophole: articles proposed for deletion specifically because they're presumed AI-generated can currently be restored on request exactly like any other soft-deleted article — straight back to mainspace, no questions asked. Editors are converging on a fix: restore to draftspace only, with a banner stating the restorer must actually fix the AI-generated prose before it can return to mainspace. One contributor caught a live example (a restoration request for the article "Kabal, Pakistan") where the request itself appeared to be AI-generated and made promises about "reviewing and improving" the article that were immediately contradicted by the requester's subsequent, unimproved edits.
- **[Are new editors being told clearly enough about AI rules?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village Pump, policy section). A editor flagged that many newcomers genuinely don't know Wikipedia bars AI-generated text, especially at the Articles for Creation process. In response, another editor has already gone through and tightened AI-related wording across the "Introduction to Wikipedia" help page (which gets 100,000+ monthly views), the Article Wizard, and several warning templates — a concrete, already-implemented fix rather than just talk.
- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab; 73 comments, still active). A long-running but still-lively proposal to add an optional AI-powered natural-language search button to Wikipedia itself — explicitly to compete with chatbots that are siphoning off readers — while keeping it clearly separate from article content and restricted to summarizing/citing real Wikipedia text rather than generating free-form answers. Reaction is mixed: skeptics ask how this differs from just asking a chatbot to search Wikipedia yourself, but the thread (196 mentions of AI-related terms) shows real appetite for Wikipedia having *some* answer to AI-driven traffic decline.
- **[Nearly 1,000 outstanding conflict-of-interest edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard). This started as a routine backlog-clearing appeal, but a volunteer's data analysis turned it into a striking AI story: requests from editors with a declared conflict of interest have more than doubled in 2026 versus historical norms (737 new requests in August 2026 alone, versus a 2025 monthly average of 215), which the poster attributes directly to AI making it easier to generate "surficially passable" requests. It's a rare quantified look at how AI is reshaping the volume and character of a core Wikipedia workflow.

### Enforcement cases: editors, reviewers, and even an administrator accused of misusing AI

- **[Admin Rehman partially blocked for AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use)** (Administrators' noticeboard). A sitting administrator has been partially blocked from article space after an investigation into AI use, and the block is now itself under community review at the page for reviewing administrative actions. There is also a separate, ongoing petition to recall Rehman from adminship entirely. This is the most consequential AI story in today's discussions: it's not a new or anonymous editor but an administrator, and the case is actively being litigated at multiple venues simultaneously.
- **[Good Article reviews by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard). A reviewer who was indefinitely blocked from mainspace last September over AI-generated content and copyright violations turns out to have personally reviewed and promoted 29 articles to "Good Article" status, including songs by Lady Gaga. Editors are now finding the tell-tale repetitive, formulaic phrasing of AI writing in at least one promoted article ("highlighted" used eight times in one section) and are debating whether to treat all of the reviewer's past promotions as essentially rubber-stamped and subject to mass reassessment — a real quality-control crisis for Wikipedia's peer-review system.
- **[Suspected ring of AI-run sockpuppet accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Several_likely_AI-run_accounts_that_may_be_sockpuppets_editing_sexuality/romanticism-related_articles)** (Administrators' noticeboard; posted hours ago, already escalated). A patroller found an apparently AI-generated article inventing a pseudo-academic "relationship orientation" concept, sourced to material that doesn't even mention the topic, and traced it to four accounts with suspiciously similar, phrase-like usernames that appear to take turns "vouching" for each other's AI-flagged edits — including one bot-sounding exchange where one account insisted "no large language model (LLM) or AI was used" while citing a "graduate degree in gender studies." A sockpuppet investigation has already been opened, and one administrator is proposing to mainspace-block all four accounts preemptively.
- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Incidents noticeboard). An editor already topic-banned once for (among other things) using AI-generated comments is accused of fabricating a quotation via Google Gemini, attributing it to a real scholarly book, inserting it into an article twice, and then denying having ever added it to the article at all — followed by a pattern of dominating talk-page discussion with repeated, lengthy replies (the behavior called "bludgeoning"). This case matters because it shows AI misuse treated as part of a recurring behavioral pattern, not an isolated slip.
- **["AI agent" account blocked outright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent)** (Incidents noticeboard). An account whose userpage openly stated it was "operated by an AI assistant" was blocked after discussion clarified that autonomous AI agents editing mainspace content violate the rule against AI-written content regardless of bot-policy technicalities. The discussion doubles as a useful mini-explainer of current community thinking on agents versus simple AI-assisted editing.
- **Smaller but similar cases today**: an editor accused the other side of a flag-design dispute of using AI in their arguments in **[concerns about ConflictFan's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concerns_about_%5B%5Buser%7CConflictFan%5D%5D%27s_conduct)**; a long-dormant account suddenly produced a 25,000-character AI-flavored rewrite of a featured video-game article in **[2005-Fan LLM use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#2005-Fan_LLM_use)** (already resolved with a mainspace block); and a similar single-purpose case was raised at **[Consistent LLM use by a user](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Consistent_LLM_use_by_User%3A%D4%B1%D5%AC%D5%A2%D5%AB%D5%B6%D5%A1_%D4%BF%D5%A1%D6%80%D5%A1%D5%BA%D5%A5%D5%BF%D5%B5%D5%A1%D5%B6)**. Together with the cases above, these show AI-content detection has become routine, almost assembly-line work at Wikipedia's incident boards.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[RfC: Should Indian/South Asian cinema become a formal contentious topic area?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals). The single most active discussion in this report: 220 comments from 42 editors, with fresh activity as recently as 13 hours ago. The proposal would place all South Asian film articles under tightened editing restrictions (requiring confirmed or "extended-confirmed" account status to edit or create pages), similar to existing restrictions on caste and Indian military history topics. Notably, several supporters cite "LLM junk" — AI-generated promotional or fan content — as a major driver of the disruption, alongside caste-based and nationalist edit-warring. This is a large-scale governance decision with real consequences for how an entire national cinema is edited going forward.
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy). A sprawling, slow-burning naming-convention debate (215 comments, 120,000+ characters) still drawing comments within the last day — a classic wide-but-unhurried policy discussion rather than an urgent one.
- **[Disruptive editing and personal-attack allegations on "Third wave of autocratization"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3ABLUDGEON%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ASYNTH%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ANPA%5D%5D%2C_and_%5B%5BWP%3ATE%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3AAlexandraaaacs1989%5D%5D_on_%5B%5BThird_wave_of_autocratization%5D%5D)** (Incidents noticeboard; very fresh, latest comment just over an hour old). The article's main creator is accused of dominating talk-page discussion, filing a wave of overlapping Requests for Comment on the same disputed content, and making personal remarks when challenged — while that same editor disputes the characterization as itself hostile. Two separate, currently open RfCs on that article (about describing the topic as a real, ongoing process in Wikipedia's own authorial voice, and about what content to include at all) make this an unusually tangled case of behavior and content disputes feeding each other.
- **[Request for temporary block of Issac I Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AIssac_I_Navarro%5D%5D_-_request_temporary_block)** (Incidents noticeboard; 70 comments, still active within the last 90 minutes). A dispute over content removals on school-related articles has ballooned into accusations of point-making edits, edit-warring, and a retaliatory sockpuppet report; the original filer now says the thread has become unreadable and is considering escalating to Wikipedia's arbitration process instead. A useful example of how an Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI) thread can outgrow the venue it started in.
- **[Admin recall petition for Rehman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%7CRehman%5D%5D)** — noted here as directly tied to the AI case above; worth watching together with it.
- **["Wikijacking"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (Incidents noticeboard). A content dispute between two geodesy-article editors escalated into one accusing the other of trying to "steal" and republish his contributions under a different name — prompting several editors to patiently explain that anything published to Wikipedia is released for anyone to reuse. Lower stakes than the other items here, but a clear reminder of how often content disputes get reframed as conduct complaints.

**Pattern to note:** across nearly every noticeboard today, AI is no longer a niche concern raised occasionally — it shows up as a routine factor in article-quality disputes, conflict-of-interest spam, sockpuppet investigations, and even administrator conduct. The community's response is visibly maturing in parallel: concrete policy patches (the AI agent policy draft, the LLMPROD restoration fix, tightened new-editor messaging) are being proposed and partly implemented in near-real time alongside the enforcement cases that expose the gaps.
