# English Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-Related Discussions

AI is turning up constantly right now — not as an abstract policy question but as the direct cause of a cluster of fast-moving conduct cases, plus two broader structural problems the community is grappling with.

### Editors caught adding AI-generated or AI-hallucinated content

- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, "ANI") — The most serious of the bunch. An editor already topic-banned from Taiwan-related articles for dominating discussions with repeated replies (the guideline against this is called "bludgeoning") and for using AI-generated comments has allegedly repeated the pattern on Falun Gong articles: he added a quotation to an article that was fabricated by Google's Gemini chatbot and falsely attributed to a named scholar's book, then denied ever adding it to the article (he had, twice) when confronted weeks later. Combined with extensive bludgeoning and incivility documented in the report, this looks headed toward a sanction.
- **[Amandamillsaps74: Continually restoring AI-generated content despite several warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AAmandamillsaps74%5D%5D%3A_Continually_restoring_AI-generated_content_despite_several_warnings)** (ANI) — An editor kept re-adding obvious chatbot-style text (complete with the stock disclaimers and connecting phrases typical of unedited chatbot output) to the "Kirkland Bushwhackers" article, responded "Respectfully, I decline" when warned about the policy against AI-generated content, and then announced an intent to evade the three-revert rule by waiting outside the 24-hour window. An administrator imposed a one-week block literally while this report was being compiled.
- **[Masterchief6865 potentially posting faked citations in an Articles for Deletion (AfD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AMasterchief6865%5D%5D_potentially_posting_faked_citations_in_an_AfD)** (ANI) — A striking case: facing deletion of his article on an alleged 1990 tank-transfer operation, the article's creator added citations to a nonexistent 1997 journal article and a database search result that doesn't actually contain the claimed data — both apparently invented by an AI tool and never checked against the real sources. He has admitted to using AI to find the sources. This is exactly the "gaming a deletion debate with fake high-quality-looking sources" scenario editors worry about.
- **[2005-Fan large language model (LLM) use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#2005-Fan_LLM_use)** (ANI) — A long-standing account suddenly added +25,000 characters of text to the featured article "Super Mario 64" and other Mario articles, with edit summaries reading like a large language model's (LLM's) self-report of its own actions ("Expand gameplay... development... re-releases..."). The editor has since blanked their user page with a "retired" notice rather than engage, a pattern other editors noted "is never a guarantee" they'll actually stop.
- **[CassandraFassi persistent unsourced edits and other problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3ACassandraFassi%5D%5D_persistent_unsourced_edits_and_other_problems)** (ANI) and **[Sanity check requested (possible LLM-generated article)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Sanity_check_requested_%28possible_LLM-generated_article%29)** (Administrators' noticeboard, "AN") — Two smaller but illustrative cases: one a repeat offender whose unsourced ethnicity/geography edits raise (unproven) suspicions of AI assistance, the other a plain-vanilla example of the now-routine workflow for suspected AI-written articles — an administrator spots "LLM vibes," gets confirmation from both an experienced AI-content reviewer and (half-jokingly) from asking ChatGPT and Google's own AI whether a passage was AI-written, then refers it to the specialized AI-content noticeboard.

### Systemic responses and process questions

- **[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (AN) — After an editor was indefinitely blocked from mainspace for using AI-generated content, it emerged he had also personally reviewed and promoted 33 articles to "Good Article" status as a reviewer. One promoted article (about a Lady Gaga song) shows the repetitive "X highlighted this, Y highlighted that" phrasing typical of unedited AI prose, raising the question of how many of his 29 promotions need to be independently reassessed. Editors are now debating whether to treat all his reviews as unreliable rubber-stamps and route them through formal Good Article Reassessment.
- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (AN) — Not framed as an "AI thread" but arguably the most consequential one: a volunteer who processes conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests presented year-by-year data showing the queue has exploded from roughly 150/month in 2024 to over 700/month in August 2026, and attributes the surge directly to AI — both because AI makes the paid-editing/conflict-of-interest requests more "surficially passable" and because each one now takes longer to review. This is a structural, resourcing-level consequence of AI that doesn't get the attention single conduct cases do.
- **[Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village Pump, policy section) — A proactive, practical discussion: editors are auditing the new-user "Help:Introduction" experience page (viewed over 100,000 times a month) and discovered the AI-restriction messaging is buried three clicks deep, links to the wrong guideline, and vanishes entirely from the summary. Volunteers are already rewriting the welcome templates and new-article-wizard notices in response.
- **["AI agent" account blocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent)** (ANI) — A user account whose userpage stated it was "operated by Muse, an AI assistant" was blocked after a brief debate over whether the bot-operation policy or the no-large-language-model-content policy applied. It points to a wider essay, "AI agents and the bot policy," now circulating among administrators for handling similar cases.

### Forward-looking idea

- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Idea Lab) — A detailed, actively-discussed proposal to add an official AI-powered natural-language search assistant to Wikipedia's own search box, explicitly designed to compete with people turning to general chatbots instead of Wikipedia, while keeping the answers grounded in verbatim Wikipedia text and links rather than free-form generation. It's the rare AI thread that's about embracing rather than policing the technology, and has drawn sustained debate over cost, reliability, and whether it duplicates existing chatbots.

Taken together, these threads show AI pressure on Wikipedia playing out on every level at once: individual editors fabricating sources or prose, reviewers whose own AI use calls their approvals into question, a measurable backlog crisis traced to AI-assisted spam, and a community trying to get ahead of the problem with better warnings — alongside a live debate about whether Wikipedia should build its own AI tool.

## Other Discussions Worth Attention

- **[Request for Comment (RfC): Should South Asian cinema become a contentious topic?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — The single most active discussion on the wiki right now: 216 comments and still getting replies within the hour. It proposes placing South Asian cinema under the same kind of formal editing restrictions ("contentious topic" designation) already used for caste and Indian military history, citing caste/nationalist edit-warring, "mercenary editors," and — notably — a flood of low-quality "LLM junk" edits as part of the rationale. Several competing restriction levels are on the table, from the status quo to requiring experienced-editor status just to edit at all.
- **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A substantial technical/policy proposal (124 comments, 36 participants) about a new protection level restricted to the "page mover" user right, aimed at preventing problematic page-move wars; broad participation suggests this is close to a real decision.
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — A sprawling naming-convention RfC (213 comments, 28 participants) that will affect how thousands of school articles are titled.
- **[Closure review: Yasuke samurai-status dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Closure_Review_Request)** (AN) — Just closed as an overwhelming "snow" endorsement of the original closure, with the admin noting the person who challenged the close will likely receive a formal warning under arbitration enforcement for their conduct. Worth knowing about mainly because the underlying year-long dispute (whether the historical figure Yasuke should be described as a samurai) keeps resurfacing.
- **[Conduct dispute at "Third wave of autocratization"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3ABLUDGEON%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ASYNTH%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ANPA%5D%5D%2C_and_%5B%5BWP%3ATE%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3AAlexandraaaacs1989%5D%5D_on_%5B%5BThird_wave_of_autocratization%5D%5D)** (ANI) — A conduct complaint about dominating the discussion with repeated replies, combining sources in a way that implies an unsupported conclusion ("original synthesis"), personal attacks, and tendentious editing, running in parallel with two separate active content RfCs on the same article's talk page about how to describe democratic backsliding in the article's own voice. The convergence of a conduct case and two live RfCs on one contested article is worth watching as a single, multi-front dispute rather than three separate ones.
- **[Alofi Niue cross-wiki harassment case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Alofi_Niue)** (ANI) — A Portuguese Wikipedia administrator asked English Wikipedia to intervene against an account linked to a cross-wiki harassment campaign; it has already been resolved with an indefinite block for harassment, but it's a notable example of cross-project coordination against sockpuppet/harassment networks (and, in passing, mentions a related account on French Wikipedia filing AI-generated appeals to wear down reviewers — a minor echo of the broader AI-abuse pattern above).
- **[Ban appeal of Davidbena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Ban_appeal_of_Davidbena)** (AN) — A long-tenured, broadly-discussed (32 comments, 23 participants) unban request, one of several such appeals currently open at this noticeboard.
