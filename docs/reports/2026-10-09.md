# Wikipedia Discussion Report — October 8, 2026

## AI-Related Discussions

AI enforcement, policy-drafting, and tooling dominate the boards today. A clear pattern: editors are increasingly comfortable identifying and acting on suspected AI-generated content, but basic definitional questions (what counts as "refined" vs. "generated," who has to prove it) remain unsettled, and the enforcement workload keeps growing.

- **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — A new, focused discussion asking whether the guideline on using large language models (LLMs) in talk-page comments (shortcut "LLMTALK") should be tightened to match the stricter article-content rule known as "NOLLM" (no large language models). Editors broadly agree repeated LLM use in discussions signals a competence problem, but disagree on where "AI-refined" (allowed) ends and "AI-generated" (forbidden) begins. Modest in size (17 comments) but exactly the kind of definitional gap that keeps spawning enforcement disputes elsewhere.

- **[Permit usage of LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions)** (Village Pump, policy) — Previously featured; now shows a near-unanimous rejection. A proposal to let editors use AI tools to write their own user pages or caption images was met with a wall of "no" votes, several quite heated ("read the room"), reflecting strong community resistance to loosening the AI ban in any direction right now.

- **[LLM Paste Check - live later this week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28technical%29#LLM_Paste_Check_-_live_later_this_week)** (Village Pump, technical) — Previously featured; now has a concrete rollout date. The Wikimedia Foundation is deploying an automated "Edit check" that flags text pasted from AI chatbots (based on length, wording, and chatbot-specific metadata) and shows editors a configurable warning linking to local AI policy. Technical editors are already hand-tuning the wording to align tightly with English Wikipedia's strict anti-AI rules — a rare case of policy enforcement getting built directly into the editing software.

- **[Itsekiriwriting: Continued AI use and disruptive editing after being warned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Itsekiriwriting%3A_Continued_AI_use_and_disruptive_editing_after_being_warned)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, or ANI) — A detailed case study in AI-detection-by-pattern: an editor previously warned off AI use is shown to still be producing text with telltale AI phrasing and AI-style "edit summaries," despite claiming to use AI only for "grammar checks." Still open; illustrates how hard enforcement is when an editor insists their AI-polished prose is "their own work."

- **["AI agent on the loose"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent_on_the_loose)** (ANI) — Already resolved: several accounts self-identifying as autonomous AI agents posted on the Reference Desk; all were indefinitely blocked within hours, with editors now watching for a wave of similar accounts via a saved search. A small but notable first-contact case with actual autonomous (not just AI-assisted) editing.

- **["Critical Hippo": aspersions, disruptive editing, revenge AfDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Critical_Hippo%3A_aspersions%2C_disruptive_editing%2C_revenge_AfDs)** (ANI) — Resolved with a one-week block. An editor whose own AI-generated article was flagged responded by filing retaliatory Articles for Deletion (AfD) nominations against the reviewer's old articles; other editors walked through specific AI "tells" in the disputed text (vague unsourced attribution, generic phrasing) as part of the case.

- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard, or AN) — Not framed as an AI story at first, but becomes one: a volunteer's statistics show conflict-of-interest (COI) edit request volume has roughly doubled in 2026 versus prior years, and a long-time reviewer attributes this directly to paid editors increasingly using AI to mass-produce "surficially passable" requests, outpacing the volunteers who review them.

- **[Appeal of community ban (Saroj)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Appeal_of_community_ban)** (AN) — Now closed/withdrawn (the editor agreed to the "standard offer" of waiting out a ban period instead). Notable for the backlash an editor received for using AI to polish their own unblock appeal — several editors treated that disclosure itself as evidence against the appeal, showing how toxic any admitted AI use has become in community eyes, even for non-article purposes.

- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — Previously featured; still a slow-burn proposal to add an AI-powered natural-language search button to Wikipedia itself (to compete with chatbots sending readers elsewhere), while keeping the classic search untouched. Interesting as a rare "pro-AI-feature" idea surviving in a generally AI-skeptical community.

## Other Discussions Worth Attention

- **["Censorless conduct in discussions"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Censorless_conduct_in_discussions)** (ANI) — Very fresh (last comment under an hour old, 45 comments). A long-simmering dispute over an editor who dominates talk-page discussions with extremely long replies (the guideline against excessive walls of text is called "WP:TEXTWALL") and repeated accusations of bad faith, despite five separate editors giving the same corrective feedback. A clean example of the guideline against dominating a discussion with repeated, lengthy replies (known as WP:BLUDGEON) playing out in real time.

- **[TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** (ANI) — An academic researcher editing articles about his own research area (polyphenols) is facing a proposed topic ban (TBAN) for conflict-of-interest advocacy, despite having disclosed the conflict. Active dispute over where legitimate expert contribution ends and conflict-of-interest advocacy begins.

- **[Dispute over an editor's use of an unreliable music-review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#~2026-41404-95)** (ANI) — A long-running conflict (previously featured, now reignited with 109 comments) between two editors, each accusing the other of harassment and disruptive editing, centered on whether the website Album of the Year can be used as a source in music articles.

- **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals; also an open Request for Comment, or RfC) — A technical proposal to create a new, intermediate page-protection level so trusted "page mover" editors (a user right below administrator) can move controversial pages without needing a full administrator. Attracting broad support (73 comments, 25 participants) and looks likely to pass.
