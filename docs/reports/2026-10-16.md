# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-Related Discussions

AI issues dominate the noticeboards and policy venues right now, touching everything from mass deletion debates to an admin's conduct to the wording of core guidelines.

### Enforcement and detection incidents

**[Up to 400 recent AfDs may contain AI-generated !votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Up_to_400_recent_AfDs_may_contain_AI-generated_%21votes)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, ANI) — This is the most urgent AI thread right now: 83 comments, 39 participants, still moving as of under 4 hours ago. An editor, Kukuryza, is accused of posting delete/keep opinions ("!votes") in Articles for Deletion (AfD) discussions at suspiciously regular 3–6 minute intervals for 8 hours straight, including assessments of sources in languages (Romanian, Hindi) the editor shows no other sign of knowing. If confirmed, this could mean hundreds of deletion discussions were influenced by AI-generated participation rather than genuine human judgement — a potentially serious integrity problem for the deletion process itself.

**[Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (Administrators' noticeboard, AN) — An administrator and university professor is accused of adding roughly 150 citations to his own academic papers and journals across Wikipedia articles, some batches posted just minutes apart, raising both conflict-of-interest and possible-automation concerns. Several editors are now calling for him to voluntarily give up his administrator tools; this is notable because it involves an admin's own credibility, not just a rank-and-file editor's.

**[Purvanchal bhojpuriya, Jha2 Chhiyanchali: edit warring, likely AI usage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Purvanchal_bhojpuriya%2C_Jha2_Chhiyanchali%3A_edit_warring%2C_likely_AI_usage)** (ANI) — A content edit-war over South Asian regional/ethnic topics where one editor eventually admitted to using AI to write replies after persistent questioning, illustrating how AI-detection accusations and ordinary edit-warring are increasingly intertwined.

**[Behavioral issue and unjustified rejection by reviewer Sulfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Behavioral_issue_and_unjustified_rejection_by_reviewer_Sulfurboy_regarding_Draft%3AJos%C3%A9_Guertzenstein)** (ANI, closed) — A new editor complained about a draft reviewer who bluntly accused them of using AI; the complaint backfired ("boomeranged") once participants showed the complaint itself, and the draft article, were almost certainly AI-written (one 16,000-character draft was apparently produced in about 15 minutes). A good illustration of how AI-detection is now a routine, if imperfect, part of new-article review.

**[Dtf1978 AI and lying about it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Dtf1978_AI_and_lying_about_it)** (ANI, closed) — Quickly resolved: an editor who posted obviously AI-generated talk-page messages, then denied using AI even after being warned, was indefinitely blocked within hours. Notable mainly as a clean, fast example of how blatant "AI-generated text plus denial" cases are now handled almost routinely.

### Policy debates on how much AI should be allowed

**[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump policy) — The largest active AI discussion on the site: 148 comments, 44 participants, still active within the last day. This formal Request for Comment (RfC) asks whether the guideline against unsupervised AI writing (known by its shortcut WP:NOLLM) should be explicitly changed to allow or to forbid using AI specifically to format Wikitext (the markup code articles are written in), following an earlier, inconclusive discussion about AI-formatted citations. Opinion is split between those who want a firm "no AI, full stop" rule and those who think narrow, supervised technical uses should be fine.

**[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump policy) — A related, still-active discussion (last comment 16 hours ago) asking whether the guideline on AI use in talk-page comments (shortcut WP:LLMTALK) should be tightened to match the stricter article-content rule, or whether it should keep allowing "AI-refined" comments (such as machine-assisted translations). Several editors note the current wording is vaguer than it should be, inviting exactly the kind of disputes seen in the enforcement cases above.

**[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/large language model (LLM) are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** (Village Pump policy) — A newer, still very fresh thread (last comment under an hour ago) asking for clarity on "grey area" uses like using AI purely to locate sources or search huge documents, rather than to write content. Useful context for how confusing the current patchwork of AI rules has become even for experienced editors.

**[Permit usage of LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions)** (Village Pump policy, archived as going nowhere) — A proposal to loosen the no-AI rule for personal user pages and image captions was shut down quickly and with unusually strong pushback ("read the room"), reflecting a broader community mood that further loosening the AI rules is unwelcome right now.

**[Onus for maintenance templates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Onus_for_maintenance_templates)** (Village Pump policy) — Framed as a generic question about maintenance tags, this thread is really about an "AI-generated content" warning tag placed on the Deep State article, and whether the tagging editor must explain exactly which passages are suspect. It surfaces a practical enforcement gap: how do ordinary editors clean up suspected AI content when the accuser won't specify what's wrong?

**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (AN) — Less a dispute than a data-driven warning: a volunteer who processes conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests presented monthly statistics showing requests have nearly doubled in 2026 compared to recent years, and argues AI is the likely cause — either AI is getting better at producing passable requests, or more paid/COI editors have started using it. This is a striking quantitative signal of AI's growing footprint on the encyclopedia's back-end workload.

### Proposals to use AI as a tool

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump idea lab) — A substantial brainstorm (66 comments, 188 AI mentions) proposing an official AI-powered natural-language search feature for Wikipedia, intended to keep readers from leaving for commercial chatbots. Reactions are mixed between curiosity and skepticism about cost, hallucination risk, and whether it duplicates the failed Abstract Wikipedia project.

**[Bot proposal: automatic addition of infobox for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump proposals) — A bot proposal to add standard infoboxes to ~12,000 company articles would use a large language model (LLM) internally just to flag uncertain cases for human review. Even with that narrow, "AI only for triage" design, one editor warned that "throwing an LLM into a contentious topic area seems a bad idea," showing how reflexive suspicion of AI now extends even to backend tooling with no AI-written text reaching the final article.

**[AI noticeboard overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#AI_noticeboard_overhaul)** (AN) — A brief announcement that the dedicated AI noticeboard (where cases like the AfD-votes incident above get formally reported) has been restructured and streamlined, suggesting the community is investing more infrastructure specifically for AI-related conduct cases.

**[RfC: Name of the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RfC%3A_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button)** (Village Pump proposals) — Mostly an interface-naming debate (124 comments), but worth a mention here because a chunk of the argument for renaming the "Talk" tab back to "Discussion" is that readers increasingly mistake the current "Talk!" label for a link to an AI chatbot — a small but telling sign of how AI has reshaped even basic user expectations of a website.

**[RfC on the debated file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AGenerative_AI_pornography#RfC_on_the_debated_file)** (Talk:Generative AI pornography) — A content RfC (121 comments) over whether a screenshot illustrating "uncensored" open-source AI image generation should remain in the Generative AI pornography article, tangled up with a parallel, larger debate at Talk:Lolicon over whether that topic should be characterized in relation to child sexual abuse material (CSAM). Sensitive but directly about AI-generated imagery policy.

## Other Discussions Worth Attention

**[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (ANI) — The single most active thread on any noticeboard right now: 237 comments, 45 participants, still being posted to within the last 90 minutes. A dispute over unreliable "flightcruft" sourcing on airport articles has snowballed into forum-shopping accusations and broader complaints about the aviation topic area's chronic conduct problems. Worth distinguishing from slower discussions: this is a rapid-fire pile-on, not a considered policy debate.

**[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (AN) — A sprawling (320 comments, 65 participants) civility dispute centered on one long-tenured editor's increasingly hostile talk-page comments across several textile-related articles, culminating in a public apology and partial withdrawal. Less urgent now (last comment 32 hours ago) but remarkable for its sheer size and the scrutiny of the editor's block history and conduct patterns.

**[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump policy) — A large, wide-participation (163 comments, 25 participants) naming-convention RfC, still active, that will affect how thousands of school articles are titled going forward.

**[RfC: Should this be characterized in relation to CSAM?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** (Talk:Lolicon) — A large (101 comments, 21 participants), sensitive RfC on whether the Lolicon article should frame the genre in relation to child sexual abuse material, directly adjacent to the AI-generated imagery debate above and part of a broader, ongoing Wikimedia-wide discussion about AI-generated sexual content involving minors.

### Pattern worth noting

Across venues, AI now shows up in three distinct modes: (1) **accusation and detection** — editors increasingly scrutinize edit timing, phrasing, and sourcing patterns to catch suspected AI-generated votes, drafts, and talk-page comments, sometimes with real due-process friction; (2) **guideline-tightening** — multiple simultaneous RfCs and discussions are trying to close perceived loopholes in the no-AI guideline (WP:NOLLM) and its talk-page counterpart (WP:LLMTALK), with a general community mood resistant to any further loosening; and (3) **AI as infrastructure** — proposals to use AI internally (search, bot triage) are drawing reflexive skepticism even when no AI-written text would reach readers. Together these suggest Wikipedia's AI policy is still being actively, and somewhat anxiously, worked out in real time rather than settled.
