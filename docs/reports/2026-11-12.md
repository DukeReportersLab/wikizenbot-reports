# Wikipedia Discussion Report

The dominant story across venues right now is AI: English Wikipedia is in the middle of a wide-ranging, multi-front argument about how to write, enforce, and even label its policies on large language model (LLM) use — running simultaneously as a 400+ comment policy Request for Comment (RfC), several smaller policy threads, a proposed new "AI agent" policy, and at least seven separate noticeboard cases about editors accused of using AI improperly. Below is a guide to what matters most.

## AI-related discussions

### The big one: should the LLM-writing guideline become binding policy?
**[Promote WP:LLM from guideline to policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Village Pump, Policy — technically a spun-off RfC)
This is by far the largest live discussion anywhere on the project at the moment: 434 comments from around 100 editors, so large it had to be moved off the main Village Pump page onto its own subpage after blowing past the normal 100-comment guideline. The proposal is narrow on its face — simply re-labeling the existing guideline "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) as a formal policy rather than a guideline — but the stakes are real: policies are harder to change and treated as closer to non-negotiable, and supporters argue the community already enforces it like policy (blocking editors and even desysopping administrators over violations). Opponents worry this forecloses future flexibility or worry about how AI-assisted editors would be accommodated. Given the participation and the real downstream enforcement consequences, this is the single most consequential AI discussion to watch.

### Building the rules for autonomous AI agents
**["Wikipedia:AI agent policy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, Policy)
A brand-new, very live thread (last comment just minutes old) proposing a short, explicit policy specifically for AI "agents" — AI systems that act autonomously on Wikipedia rather than just assisting a human typing. It was prompted partly by a real incident (an AI agent called "TomWikiAssist" that was banned and reportedly complained publicly about censorship). The author deliberately wants something simpler and more discoverable than the existing, process-heavy bot policy, and is trying to merge efforts with a parallel draft, "Wikipedia:Agent policy." Worth tracking as this could become the next major rule change after the policy-promotion RfC above.

### Browser AI assistants are quietly creating new loopholes
**["Microsoft Edge just turned into a [[WP:NOLLM]] violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, Policy) — 56 comments, very AI-dense (83 mentions)
An editor noticed the Microsoft Edge browser now offers an in-editor "rewrite this" AI assistant — unprompted — while editing Wikipedia, with options like "polish this draft" or "make the tone more empathetic." The discussion is a genuinely interesting policy puzzle: the existing rule allows LLM help with spelling, grammar, and "simple corrections," but editors are split on whether tools like this (or Grammarly) quietly blow past that exception, since there's no bright line between "fixing grammar" and "rewriting prose." This directly feeds into a related, more abstract thread, **["Should something about LLMs be mentioned at WP:NOT?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)**, debating where (if anywhere) in the rulebook a general "Wikipedia is not written by AI" principle belongs — with disagreement over whether that's even an accurate or useful thing to say, given "Grokipedia" is also an AI-written encyclopedia.

### Making AI rules legible to newcomers
**["Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village Pump, Policy)
A more constructive, less contentious thread: editors agree newcomers often genuinely don't know AI-generated text is restricted, and volunteers have already started adding clearer warnings to the Articles for Creation (AfC) wizard, welcome templates, and the "Help:Introduction" page (viewed over 100,000 times a month). A useful counterpoint to the more adversarial enforcement threads below — this is the policy community trying to prevent problems rather than just punish them after the fact.

### Should Wikipedia build its own AI search tool?
**["AI-assisted search"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, Idea Lab) — 82 comments, very active (last comment under 20 minutes ago), extremely AI-dense (207 mentions)
A proposal that Wikipedia itself offer an AI-powered natural-language search assistant to compete with chatbots that are (the proposer argues) siphoning off readers, while strictly keeping it separate from article-writing and routing all answers back to sourced Wikipedia text rather than free-form generation. It's a brainstorm rather than a formal proposal, but the volume of discussion shows how seriously editors are taking the "Wikipedia is losing readers to AI chatbots" problem, even among people who are otherwise hostile to AI-generated content.

### Enforcement cases: AI misuse by individual editors
A striking pattern this cycle is how many simultaneous noticeboard cases revolve around AI misuse by specific editors — this is clearly the most active enforcement category right now:

- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Incidents noticeboard) — An editor already topic-banned once partly over AI-generated comments is now accused of fabricating a quote with Google's Gemini chatbot, attributing it to a real scholarly book, restoring it after being challenged, and then denying having added it at all. Combined with heavy bludgeoning (dominating discussions with repeated replies) and incivility, this is a serious case with a real sanctions history behind it.
- **[Hajiani, Dr.Seyed Firdawoosbee Hanifdeen and AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Hajiani%2C_Dr.Seyed_Firdawoosbee_Hanifdeen_and_AI_use)** (Incidents noticeboard) — An editor mass-creating obviously AI-generated articles directly in the main encyclopedia (rather than draft space), unresponsive to warnings except once, in a heartfelt human-written message explaining he is an elderly self-taught historian using voice-typing, not realizing the AI-polished versions were the problem.
- **[AI-powered drafting and sealioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI-powered_drafting_and_sealioning)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A smaller but telling case: a user repeatedly posts AI-generated help requests about a declining draft, ignoring responses and re-asking the identical AI-phrased question elsewhere, raising both an AI-use and an undisclosed conflict-of-interest concern.
- **[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Fallout from an earlier AI-related indefinite block: the blocked editor had reviewed 33 Good Article nominations, and editors are now finding likely AI-generated prose (including a hallucinated quote in one related article) that passed review, prompting calls for a coordinated reassessment of all his promotions.
- **[Unblock request from CarnaticHistoryEditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_request_from_CarnaticHistoryEditor)** (Administrators' noticeboard, now closed) — A tidy illustration of how hard this problem is to self-correct: an editor blocked for using AI to write talk-page comments had their unblock request denied because the unblock request itself appeared to be AI-generated.

Taken together, these cases suggest that detecting and adjudicating AI misuse — not just writing policy about it — has become one of the steadiest workloads on the administrative noticeboards.

## Other discussions worth attention

1. **[RfC: Should South Asian cinema become a contentious topic area?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, Proposals) — With 227 comments from 44 editors, this is the single most heavily-discussed thread of the week. It proposes placing all South Asian cinema articles under formal "general sanctions" (restricting editing to more experienced accounts) to curb caste- and nationalist-driven edit warring — and notably, several supporters cite a flood of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") content as part of the problem. A real governance decision with teeth, currently still open.

2. **[Alleged hounding by Sirfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A long-running, 95-comment dispute in which an editor accuses another of following their edits across multiple politically-charged navigation templates (about the Trump administration and Gaza) and repeatedly challenging their content. Both sides have a documented history, including a prior failed complaint against the same user, making this a genuinely contested behavioral dispute rather than a one-sided report.

3. **[RationalWiki is canvassing about its Articles for Deletion (AfD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#RationalWiki_is_canvassing_about_its_AfD)** (Incidents noticeboard) — The Deletion debate over the RationalWiki encyclopedia's own Wikipedia article was flagged on RationalWiki's own community forum, drawing accusations of improper off-wiki vote-rallying (called "canvassing" on Wikipedia) and a side dispute about one participant dominating the discussion with excessive replies.

4. **[RfC: Should templates containing article body content be allowed?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Should_templates_containing_article_body_content_be_allowed%3F)** (Village Pump, Policy) — A fast-moving, currently very active policy RfC (60 comments, last reply under an hour old) on whether templates should be allowed to hold shared article text, a technical-sounding question with real implications for how consistent content is kept across related articles.

5. **[Should a new "language notability" criterion be adopted?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RFC_on_new_notability_proposal_adoption)** (Village Pump, Policy) — A niche but substantive RfC on formally adopting a new notability guideline specifically for languages, debating thorny questions like whether every entry in a linguistic database (Glottolog) should count as evidence a language deserves its own article.
