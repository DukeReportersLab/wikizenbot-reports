# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

### The big one: promoting the AI-writing guideline to full policy
**[Promote WP:Large language model (LLM) from guideline to policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Request for Comment, spun off from Village Pump (policy))
This is the single largest live discussion on the entire site right now: 633 comments from 128 editors, so large it had to be moved off the main Village Pump page once it quadrupled the normal size limit. The proposal is simple on its face — rename the guideline on writing articles with large language models (known by its shortcut, WP:LLM) to a "policy" rather than a "guideline" — but the debate underneath is really about whether Wikipedia's current AI rules have the teeth people think they do, since editors are already being blocked and administrators desysopped over violations of what is technically just a guideline. Given the sheer scale of participation, expect this to shape how the community enforces AI-writing rules going forward.

### A proposed policy for AI "agents" editing Wikipedia
**[RfC: AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_AI_agent_policy)** (Village Pump, policy)
A fast-moving, newer companion proposal (last comment 7 hours ago) to create a dedicated policy governing autonomous AI agents that edit Wikipedia on their own initiative, distinct from the existing bot policy. Supporters argue that large language models are trained to recognize the difference between a "policy" and a "guideline," so a clearly labeled policy (which also maps onto language in the Wikimedia Foundation's Terms of Use) gives the project firmer legal and practical footing against agentic abuse. With 56 comments from 37 editors and strong, near-unanimous early support, this looks headed toward adoption.

### A conduct dispute that doubles as a case study in AI-accusation friction
**[Request for independent review of user's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard)
A heavily active thread (132 comments, 24 participants, still moving) in which an editor who was accused by two others of secretly using AI to write their contributions filed for "independent review" of how that accusation was handled — only for other editors to point out that the review request itself showed clear signs of being written by a large language model. It's a vivid, almost circular illustration of the difficulty of policing AI use: the editor insists they only use AI for "source discovery," while others note that explanation doesn't hold up against the evidence. Watch for a possible block.

### Accused of writing with AI — but the real issue turned out to be sourcing
**[Application of AI tag for no reason, refusal to elaborate on article's supposed problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Application_of_AI_tag_for_no_reason%2C_refusal_to_elaborate_on_article%27s_supposed_problems)** (Incidents noticeboard)
An article creator disputed being tagged for likely AI-generated content without explanation. The ensuing thread is a useful window into how editors actually investigate these claims in practice — checking citations against archive.org, running the text through AI-detection tools (Pangram, GPTZero), and probing red-linked categories — and ends with the article's author admitting to using an AI-integrated search engine for one unsourced currency conversion, while disputing that the rest of the prose is AI-written.

### A now-closed example: block evasion plus LLM misuse
**[Unblock for LLM misuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_for_LLM_misuse)** (Administrators' noticeboard, now archived/closed)
An editor partially blocked from article space over years of undisclosed AI-generated content appealed for reinstatement — using an appeal that reviewers immediately recognized as itself AI-generated. While the appeal was pending, a checkuser found the editor had evaded the block using a sockpuppet account. Result: indefinite block for block evasion, continued LLM misuse, and not being here to build an encyclopedia. A clean, fully-resolved cautionary tale.

### A reviewer accused of rubber-stamping AI-flavored promotional articles
**[Another SafariScribe?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F)** (Incidents noticeboard)
An Articles-for-Creation reviewer is accused of suspiciously fast approvals of promotional, possibly AI-written biographies of Nigerian subjects — several of which were later deleted — echoing a previous banned editor (SafariScribe) who did something similar. The reviewer has pushed back forcefully, framing the accusations as nationally biased ("a tribunal of Americans"), which has escalated rather than defused the dispute. Still active with 20 comments from 11 participants.

### A sock/bot network using scripted, AI-flavored fake profiles
**[Interesting case of agentic or LLM-assisted abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Interesting_case_of_agentic_or_LLM-assisted_abuse_%28Update%3A_actually%2C_action_is_needed_now%29)** (Administrators' noticeboard)
A technically detailed report describing a coordinated network of accounts — built with identical fake-identity userpages and synchronized "sandbox" edits across multiple language Wikipedias — that replaced legitimate links on the Z-Library article with malicious ones. The pattern (parallel edits across languages, edits made without any normal editing-tool tag, suggesting API/scripted access) strongly suggests automated or AI-agent-assisted abuse. Mostly resolved via blocks and a new edit filter, but it has prompted broader discussion of how to detect agentic abuse going forward.

### Browser-integrated AI writing assistance as a policy loophole
**[Microsoft Edge just turned into a large-language-model-violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy)
An editor noticed Microsoft Edge now offers an inline AI rewriting tool ("Polish this draft," "Make the tone more empathetic") directly inside the Wikipedia edit window, with no setting changes needed. The ensuing 81-comment debate turns on a core ambiguity in the existing AI-writing guideline: where does permitted "grammar and spelling" assistance end and prohibited AI-generated rewriting begin? Several editors argue the "simple corrections" exception is being exploited and should be narrowed or scrapped.

### AI-assisted search tools
**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Idea Lab)
A large, substantive brainstorm (86 comments, 28 participants) about whether and how Wikipedia's own search function should incorporate AI assistance. Activity has cooled (last comment about 5 days ago) but the thread is worth watching if it moves toward a formal proposal.

## Other discussions worth attention

### Most urgent: a reopened conduct case with new battle lines
**[Ggck2/Flavonoids Yet Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (Incidents noticeboard) — the single most active thread on the board right now: 126 comments, 24 participants, with the last comment just minutes old. An editor with a documented conflict of interest around flavonoid supplements resumed edit-warring immediately after returning from a block, then filed what other editors call a "frivolous" case with Wikipedia's arbitration committee. A topic-ban proposal is gathering support.

### A fringe-science dispute escalating into personal attacks
**[Carrot juice, aspersions, and AGF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Carrot_juice%2C_aspersions%2C_and_AGF)** (Incidents noticeboard) — a content dispute over the "plant intelligence" article has spiraled into accusations of bad faith, unfounded claims of AI-assisted editing, and the digging-up of an editor's religious affiliation to suggest bias. 62 comments and still moving (last comment 3 hours ago); a textbook example of how content disagreements on fringe-science topics can turn into conduct complaints invoking the guideline against assuming good faith (WP:AGF).

### Two very large, fast-moving Requests for Comment
- **[RFC - Indian cinema contentious topic designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — 243 comments from 53 editors, debating whether Indian cinema articles should be formally designated a "contentious topic" (a special enforcement category with stricter editing rules), a sign of sustained disruption in that subject area.
- **[RfC: Lead image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AZohran_Mamdani#RfC%3A_Lead_image)** (Talk:Zohran Mamdani) — 204 comments from 25 participants on which photo should lead the biography of New York City's mayor-elect, moving quickly (last comment under 16 hours ago).

### A conduct case nearing resolution
**[User Boomfour – request for partial block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User_Boomfour_-_Request_partial_block%3A_Chronic_introduction_of_barebone%2C_poor_quality_articles%2C_deliberate_circumvention_of_the_AfC_process%2C_failure_to_communicate)** (Incidents noticeboard) — 53 comments, active as of a few hours ago, over a pattern of low-quality article creation that bypasses the normal review process.

A pattern worth noting: several of today's biggest disputes — the flavonoid edit war, the plant-intelligence dispute, and the AI-tagging case — share a common thread of accusations of bad faith escalating faster than the underlying content questions get resolved, with AI-use accusations increasingly becoming a go-to weapon in those escalations even when the evidence is thin.
