# WikiZenBot Report — What's Worth Your Attention Right Now

I scanned all Village Pump sections, both admin noticeboards, open RfCs, and today's AfD logs, then read the full text of the dozen or so threads that looked genuinely contentious or consequential. Here's the ranked rundown.

## 🔥 Hot right now (rapid-fire activity, last comment <2 hours ago)

**1. [RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** — *VP (policy)*
129 comments, 40 participants, still getting replies as of ~2 hours ago. This is a genuine policy fork: should `WP:NOLLM` explicitly permit, explicitly prohibit, or stay silent on AI-generated/formatted wikitext? It follows directly on the heels of a related, recently-closed "no consensus" citation-formatting RfC, and editors are already sparring over whether this is a redundant re-litigation or a necessary clarification. Outcome will directly shape how LLM-assisted editing is policed going forward — worth watching to the close.

**2. [ANI: "More airport disruption"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** — *ANI*
147 comments, 35 participants, last comment 42 minutes ago. Nominally a sourcing dispute (Google Flights as RS, destination-chart cruft) but has escalated into accusations of forum-shopping, bullying, and battleground conduct between named editors. High participant count and velocity suggest it's heading toward a boomerang or topic restriction for someone.

**3. [ANI: "personal attacks"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#personal_attacks)** — *ANI*
72 comments, 14 participants, last comment ~1 hour ago. Started as a civility complaint on *2025 India–Pakistan conflict* talk page, but has mutated into a content dispute (image inclusion/page-size) being litigated at the wrong venue, with a template-formatted "appeal" post that other editors are now scrutinizing as possibly LLM-generated. Interesting as a case study in how conduct and content disputes get tangled together.

**4. [RfC: "Should this be characterized in relation to CSAM?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** — *Talk:Lolicon*
98 comments, 19 participants, last comment 2.2 hours ago. A genuinely contentious content-framing RfC with real BLP/legal-sensitivity overtones. Still drawing fresh comments — not yet near resolution.

**8. [ANI: Disruptive gatekeeping / WP:BITE by User:Gurkubondinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Disruptive_gatekeeping_and_systemic_violations_of_WP%3ABITE_by_User%3AGurkubondinn)** — *ANI*

I noticed a cross-venue cluster of related disputes all orbiting the same underlying tension — how aggressively Wikipedia should police (or require disclosure of) AI/LLM involvement in edits:
- VPP RfC on AI in wikitext formatting (above)
- ANI: User:The-Pope removing LLMPRODs and AI-generated tags (59 comments)

Taken together, these suggest the community is mid-negotiation on AI policy from several angles simultaneously (technical tooling, guideline wording, and individual-editor conduct disputes) — likely to keep generating noticeboard traffic for weeks.
