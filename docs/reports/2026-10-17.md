# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI issues dominate this cycle — from a formal policy Request for Comment (RfC) on wikitext formatting, to an admin accused of AI-assisted spam, to a wave of AI-generated deletion-discussion votes and user-page drafts. A clear pattern emerges: volunteers are increasingly overwhelmed by the sheer *volume* of AI-assisted submissions (conflict-of-interest requests, drafts, deletion votes), while policy on where the line sits remains contested and inconsistently enforced.

**[RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — The biggest live AI policy fight right now: 149 comments from 45 editors, still getting replies within the last ten hours. It asks whether the guideline against using AI/large language models (LLMs) to generate article content, known by its shortcut "NOLLM," should be explicitly extended to formatting raw Wikipedia markup text (citations, tables, etc.), explicitly permitted, or left ambiguous. This follows directly from a related, recently-closed RfC on AI-formatted citations that ended in "no consensus," so this is effectively a re-run with sharper options. Given the participation and the fact it touches nearly every editor who uses citation tools, this is the one to watch for an actual policy change.

**[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — A companion debate asking whether the guideline on AI-assisted talk-page comments (shortcut "LLMTALK") actually bans AI-written comments the way the content guideline bans AI-written articles, or merely allows them to be collapsed. Editors are split between tightening the wording to an outright ban (with a carve-out for translation) versus explaining the *rationale* (preserving genuine human communication) rather than drawing a hard line. With 30 comments and fresh activity, this is shaping up to be the next formal RfC.

**["Up to 400 recent AfDs may contain AI-generated !votes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Up_to_400_recent_AfDs_may_contain_AI-generated_%21votes)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, ANI) — Already resolved, but the outcome matters: editor Kukuryza was found to have posted votes in Articles for Deletion (AfD) discussions at suspiciously regular 3–6 minute intervals for eight-hour stretches, evaluating sources in languages they don't read, in a pattern consistent with automated or AI-assisted voting. The community banned the account. With 84 comments and 40 participants, this is a significant enforcement precedent for detecting bulk AI participation in deletion debates, and the ~400-vote scale means cleanup may still be ongoing.

**["Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard, AN) — Started as a routine call for volunteers to clear the conflict-of-interest (COI) edit request backlog, but a reviewer's data makes it genuinely newsworthy: monthly new COI requests have roughly tripled in 2026 compared with recent years (737 in August alone, versus a 2025 monthly average of 215), which the poster attributes directly to AI making it easier to generate "surficially passable" edit requests. This is hard evidence of AI reshaping volunteer workload at scale.

**["Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (AN) — An administrator and university professor is accused of adding roughly 150 self-citing references to his own academic journals across Wikipedia articles in a single week, including bursts of edits one to two minutes apart, raising suspicion of automated or AI-assisted editing on top of the underlying self-promotion/COI problem. With 33 comments and growing, editors are pushing for him to resign his administrator tools voluntarily; this one still has real consequences pending.

**["Onus for maintenance templates"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Onus_for_maintenance_templates)** (Village Pump, policy) — A dispute that started over one editor tagging an article with an "AI-generated" maintenance template without explaining which passages were suspect, and then refusing to clarify when asked. It has broadened into a general policy question: who bears the burden of proof when an "AI-generated" tag is placed, and can editors be required to run external AI-detection tools to figure out the problem themselves? With 84 comments, this could set real precedent for how the hundreds of AI-cleanup taggings happening in practice are supposed to work.

**["Is there any write up of what uses of AI/LLM are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — A smaller but telling thread: an editor asks for a plain-language summary of which AI uses are actually fine, because the patchwork of guidelines (NOLLM, LLMTALK, LLMTRANSLATE) is confusing even to experienced editors. Symptomatic of the broader governance gap visible across all these threads.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — An early-stage proposal (66 comments, 19 participants) for Wikipedia itself to offer an AI-powered natural-language search tool, pitched partly as a defense against readers abandoning Wikipedia for chatbots. Not close to a decision, but worth watching as a sign the community is beginning to discuss offensive (not just defensive) uses of AI.

**["Bot proposal: automatic addition of infobox for company articles"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A bot proposal (70 comments, 28 participants) to add infoboxes to roughly 12,000 company articles, using an LLM (Claude Opus) as a filter to flag uncertain cases for human review. Several editors are uneasy about delegating even this narrow judgment call to an LLM inside a historically contentious area (infobox disputes), making it a useful real-world test case for how much editors trust AI-assisted bots even for "boring" maintenance tasks.

**[RfC on the debated file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AGenerative_AI_pornography#RfC_on_the_debated_file)** (Talk:Generative AI pornography) — A content RfC (121 comments, 16 participants, still active) over whether a screenshot of an open-source AI image generator producing explicit content should illustrate the "Generative AI pornography" article. It's tangled up with a parallel, equally active RfC on [Talk:Lolicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F) over whether that topic should be characterized in relation to child sexual abuse material (CSAM), and a Wikimedia Foundation legal team discussion off-wiki — together these form a genuinely high-stakes cluster of decisions about how Wikipedia depicts AI-generated sexual content involving children or childlike characters.

**Smaller AI enforcement cases worth noting:** A pattern of AI-related conduct disputes is showing up throughout Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents: a reviewer at Articles for Creation detected a draft "Frankensteined" from text written by different AI chatbots across different years in the [BrowseTQ case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT); a complaint against a reviewer for calling out AI use backfired into a boomerang warning after the complaint itself was shown to be AI-written in the [Sulfurboy case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Behavioral_issue_and_unjustified_rejection_by_reviewer_Sulfurboy_regarding_Draft%3AJos%C3%A9_Guertzenstein); and a previously-blocked editor was caught using AI again just a week after promising not to, in [the Martinpri case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Martinpri_-_blocked_for_AI%2C_said_they_wouldn%27t_use_AI_anymore%2C_you%27ll_never_guess_what_happened_next). A short-lived proposal to let editors use LLMs on their own user pages and for image captions was [closed as "going nowhere"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions) after near-unanimous rejection. Together these suggest AI-detection has become a routine, almost reflexive part of noticeboard triage.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — 165 comments and still active within the last five hours. This is a naming-convention question but it would affect the titles of potentially thousands of school articles, so it's drawing wide participation and will need a careful close.

**["TBAN proposal for Ggck2"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** (ANI) — Live right now (last comment 30 minutes before this report). A proposal for a topic ban against an editor with an admitted industry conflict of interest (funded polyphenol/flavonoid research) who was previously indefinitely blocked under a different username for the same conduct, then unblocked, and is now accused of resuming advocacy editing on the same articles. A real-time test of how the community handles repeat conflict-of-interest offenders.

**["More airport disruption"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (ANI) — The largest thread currently open anywhere scanned (241 comments, 45 participants, still active within the last seven hours). It's a sprawling civility and sourcing dispute in the aviation topic area — unreliable "destination chart" sourcing, accusations of forum-shopping, and a related dispute about civility that was already archived from a previous report. Less about one decision and more a sign of chronic battleground behavior in that topic.

**[RfC: Should the "Lolicon" article be characterized in relation to child sexual abuse material?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** — 107 comments and still very active (last comment 11 hours ago). A sensitive content-policy question that, as noted above, is directly entangled with the AI-generated pornography image dispute.

**["Namespace-block block evasion by RossH Music"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Namespace-block_block_evasion_by_RossH_Music)** and the broader **[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** mega-thread (AN, 320 comments, 65 participants) are worth a mention for scale alone, though activity has slowed considerably (over two days since the last comment), suggesting these are winding down rather than approaching a decisive moment.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A permissions/technical proposal with substantial engagement (78 comments, 30 participants) about giving the "page mover" user right the ability to move-protect pages, a fairly technical but consequential change to how page-move vandalism is handled.

A noticeable theme across the non-AI noticeboard threads: a cluster of long-running, high-comment-count disputes (airport articles, Fiber, the school-naming RfC) reflect chronic friction in specific topic areas rather than single breaking news events — worth tracking for eventual resolution, but none is at a decisive tipping point today except the live Ggck2 topic-ban vote.
