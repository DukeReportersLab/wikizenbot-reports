# Wikipedia Discussion Report — October 7, 2026

## AI-related discussions

AI enforcement and policy dominate today's activity, with several fresh, behaviorally-concrete cases alongside the long-running policy fights.

**[Itsekiriwriting: Continued AI use and disruptive editing after being warned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Itsekiriwriting%3A_Continued_AI_use_and_disruptive_editing_after_being_warned)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, a venue for reporting urgent behavioral problems, abbreviated ANI) — A new report alleges that an editor who was explicitly told in July that "AI cannot be used to produce content for articles" has kept producing large language model (LLM)-generated prose, pointing to telltale hedging phrases ("the surviving evidence does not establish either year precisely") and edit summaries that read like AI self-descriptions. The editor has shifted their story across the thread, from denying AI use entirely to admitting they use it "to refine wording" — a real test of how the community enforces its no-LLM-content guideline, abbreviated WP:NOLLM, against a repeat offender.

**[Critical Hippo: aspersions, disruptive editing, revenge AfDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%7B%7Bu%7CCritical_Hippo%7D%7D%3A_aspersions%2C_disruptive_editing%2C_revenge_AfDs)** (ANI) — A dispute that began over an article flagged for AI-generated content escalated into accusations of retaliatory "Articles for Deletion" (AfD) nominations. What makes this one notable for AI-watchers is the level of technical detail editors now use to identify machine-written text — citing specific guideline shortcuts for AI "tells" like vague unsourced attribution and empty phrases such as "repeated in trade press coverage" with no named source. It shows how granular Wikipedia's informal AI-detection vocabulary has become.

**[Disruptive gatekeeping and systemic violations of the guideline against biting newcomers by User:Gurkubondinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Disruptive_gatekeeping_and_systemic_violations_of_WP%3ABITE_by_User%3AGurkubondinn)** (ANI) — The flip side of the above: a prolific AI-content patroller is accused of being too aggressive, allegedly telling non-native English speakers who use translation tools to "go write in your native language" instead. Commenters are split — some call it xenophobic gatekeeping, others note that modern translation apps use LLMs internally and that the patroller's underlying concerns about undisclosed AI use are often correct. This is the same editor involved in a separate, long-running "Onus for maintenance templates" dispute (see Ongoing, below), suggesting a recurring flashpoint between AI-cleanup efforts and good-faith non-native contributors.

**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard, abbreviated AN; previously featured once) — What began as a volunteer-recruitment post has turned into hard data: a contributor tallied conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests by month and found explosive, AI-attributed growth — 737 new requests in August 2026 alone, compared to a historical monthly average below 150 as recently as 2022. The poster explicitly attributes this to AI making it easier to generate "surficially passable" requests, a striking quantitative signal of AI's effect on Wikipedia's paid-editing and COI pipeline.

**[LLM Paste Check – live later this week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28technical%29#LLM_Paste_Check_-_live_later_this_week)** (Village Pump (technical); previously featured once) — Time-sensitive: the Wikimedia Foundation's new automatic detector for pasted chatbot text — which flags edits containing metadata that popular chatbots embed — is set to go live this Thursday. Since it last appeared, discussion has moved into practical edge cases, like whether merging or splitting old articles could trigger false positives, and editors are actively rewriting the tool's default wording to align with English Wikipedia's strict anti-LLM rules rather than the softer Foundation defaults.

**[Permit usage of LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions)** (Village Pump (policy)) — A brand-new proposal to carve out exceptions to the no-LLM-content guideline for personal user pages and image captions is getting swift, mostly negative responses within hours, including "no to both" and accusations that it's yet another attempt to "chip away" at the ban. Small discussion so far but a clean illustration of how any proposed loosening of AI rules meets immediate resistance.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump (idea lab); previously featured once) — An editor is pitching an official AI-powered natural-language search button for Wikipedia itself, partly to compete with chatbots that are (in the proposer's view) siphoning off Wikipedia's traffic. Pushback so far questions how this would differ from just asking a general chatbot to cite Wikipedia sources.

**Smaller AI items worth flagging:** In **[AI agent on the loose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent_on_the_loose)** (ANI), a self-identified autonomous AI agent posted to Wikipedia's Reference Desk and was promptly blocked — a small but concrete sign that fully autonomous bot accounts are now showing up in the wild, not just assistance tools used by humans. And a now-closed **[personal attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#personal_attacks)** case ended with an editor's own dispute statement being called out as AI-generated — wry evidence that AI authorship suspicion has become a routine feature of ordinary conduct disputes.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[Request for Comment (RfC): Mugshot as infobox photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AClavicular_%28influencer%29#RfC%3A_Mugshot_as_Infobox_Photo)** (talk page Request for Comment, abbreviated RfC, on the article about influencer Braden "Clavicular" Peters) — A biography-of-living-persons (BLP) dispute over whether to use a subject's mugshot as his main photo, since it's the only freely-licensed image available. A clear "no" consensus is forming, with editors citing the guideline against using mugshots to imply criminality is central to someone's notability (shortcut WP:MUG) and comparing it to how Wikipedia handles other controversial living subjects.

**[New section of AIV for talk-page-access and email revocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#New_section_of_AIV_for_TPA_and_email_revoke)** (Village Pump (proposals)) — A fresh, actively-discussed proposal (50 comments, 26 participants) to let administrators revoke a blocked user's talk-page and email access through the existing vandalism-reporting noticeboard rather than ad hoc, which would streamline anti-abuse processes.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump (proposals); previously featured once) — A technical permissions proposal with heavy engagement (73 comments, 25 participants) about restricting a particular user right related to page moves; still active after a week.

**[Mass removal of archive.is links by possible proxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Mass_removal_of_archive.is_by_possible_proxies)** (ANI; previously featured once) — An investigation into coordinated removal of archive links, suspected to involve proxy/sockpuppet accounts — the kind of slow-burn integrity issue that can indicate a wider manipulation campaign.

**[AZERTAC reliability discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AReliable_sources/Noticeboard#AZERTAC)** (Reliable sources noticeboard) — A fresh, fast-moving discussion (29 comments in a short time) about whether Azerbaijan's state news agency should be treated as a reliable source, relevant to Wikipedia's ongoing struggles with state-backed media.
