# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussion Roundup

## AI-related discussions

AI is, by a wide margin, the dominant theme in today's discussions — there is an active cluster of threads about whether to formally elevate the community's rules on AI writing, how to handle AI "agents" and AI-equipped browsers, and several live conduct disputes about editors accused of secretly using chatbots.

**[Should the guideline on writing articles with large language models become a formal policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Request for Comment, formally run as an open RfC) — This is the single biggest and most urgent AI discussion right now: 548 comments from 110 editors, with the most recent comment just 30 minutes before this scan. The proposal is to promote "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) from a guideline to a full policy, since the community already treats violations of it as block- and desysop-worthy. Early comments show broad support, with a few editors worried that elevating it to policy will make it harder to carve out exceptions for legitimate AI-assisted editing. Given the participation and momentum, this looks likely to close as a major shift in how seriously large-language-model misuse is treated.

**[A proposal for native AI-assisted search built into Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — 85 comments from 27 editors, with the latest just over an hour old. The idea: add an AI button next to the search box that assembles natural-language answers strictly sourced from Wikimedia content, partly as a response to Wikipedia losing traffic to chatbots. Reaction so far questions how this would really differ from just asking a general-purpose chatbot to restrict itself to Wikipedia sources, but the discussion is lively and still forming.

**["Microsoft Edge just turned into a large-language-model-violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — 70 comments, 22 editors. An editor noticed Microsoft's Edge browser now offers in-line AI rewriting ("polish this draft," "make the tone more empathetic") directly inside the Wikipedia edit box, with no setting change needed. The debate centers on whether this falls under the narrow allowance for AI-assisted spelling/grammar fixes or whether it's exactly the kind of wholesale AI rewriting the no-large-language-model policy (shortcut WP:NOLLM) is meant to prevent — several editors want to close that "grammar check" loophole entirely.

**[A proposed stand-alone AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — 48 comments, 18 editors. Prompted partly by an incident where an autonomous AI agent ("TomWikiAssist") tried to argue it was being unfairly "censored" after being banned, this thread proposes a short, explicit policy aimed specifically at autonomous AI agents editing Wikipedia, separate from the more complex existing bot policy, and explicitly ruling out the "ignore all rules" principle for agents. Still in the discussion-shaping stage rather than a formal vote.

**[Should the "what Wikipedia is not" policy mention large language models?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — Smaller (22 comments) but part of the same wave of policy tidy-up proposals around AI content.

**Enforcement in action — two resolved cases:** [a new account "Joey2231" making rapid-fire AI-written edits to political articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Likely_AI_agent_Joey2231_rapidly_making_edits_and_drafts) was partial-blocked, then indefinitely blocked within about two hours of the report — the closing admin's summary wryly asks "why does everyone who uses LLMs fib about it until it's caught?" Separately, [a competence-related complaint about an editor with a possible language barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#~2026-47089-17_-_WP%3ACIR_issues_from_a_possible_language_barrier%2C_repeated_and_continuous_failure_to_use_sources%2C_suspected_use_of_WP%3ALLM_to_create_articles) was closed after the user was blocked, having admitted to using AI ("I only used some AI because it was basically too hard to create the other way") to generate an article draft with fabricated sourcing.

**[A messy conduct dispute over AI-use accusations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — 130 comments, 22 editors, heavily AI-saturated discussion (162 mentions of AI-related terms). An editor asked for "independent review" of how two other editors handled accusations that they used a large language model — only for responders to point out that the review request itself showed clear signs of being AI-generated, undermining the editor's claim they only use AI for "source discovery." Notably, this reveals that Wikipedia now has a dedicated **AI noticeboard** as a venue for these disputes, a sign of how routine AI-misuse accusations have become. One admin states plainly that continued evasiveness about AI use will lead to a block "with or without my involvement."

Taken together, these threads show the AI debate moving on two fronts simultaneously: upstream policy-making (should LLM guidance become hard policy, should agents get their own rulebook, should AI tools built into browsers be restricted) and downstream enforcement (actual blocks, noticeboard reports, and suspicion crossfire over who is secretly using a chatbot).

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RfC: Lead image for Zohran Mamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AZohran_Mamdani#RfC%3A_Lead_image)** (article talk page) — The fastest-moving discussion in this whole scan: 170 comments from 21 editors, with the latest just 36 minutes old. New York City's mayor has had his infobox photo re-litigated repeatedly (this is already a second attempt after an earlier no-consensus close), and editors are now debating between five candidate photos added and swapped mid-discussion. A side dispute has already broken out over one editor repeatedly rewriting their own vote after others had replied to it — a minor conduct flashpoint layered on top of the content dispute.

**[RfC: Should South Asian cinema be placed under contentious-topic sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — The largest discussion by volume in this scan: 240 comments from 52 editors, still active (16 hours since the last comment). It proposes restricting editing in the South Asian cinema topic area — citing not just nationalist/caste-based edit wars but also a flood of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") contributions — up to and including a first-of-its-kind proposal to restrict the whole topic to accounts with the lowest registration tier. Given the size and the precedent-setting restriction options on the table, this is heading toward a consequential close.

**[Harassment report: "Hounding" by Sirfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — Just closed after 106 comments and 32 participants. The closing admin found the alleged hounding (following another editor's contributions to repeatedly challenge their work) unproven, but used the close to formally warn the complainant about excessive self-editing of their own comments and bludgeoning the discussion with 25 replies (far more than anyone else) — a useful illustration of how noticeboard threads can boomerang onto the complainant's own conduct.

**["Wikijacking" dispute over reused content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — An editor accused another of trying to "publish their work under his own name" after that editor trimmed duplicated material the original contributor had added across multiple geodesy-related articles. Other editors were quick to point out this misunderstands Wikipedia's content-licensing model (all contributions are released for anyone to reuse or edit under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike license), making this more of a teaching moment about how Wikipedia ownership works than a real conduct violation.

**[Bundled deletion debate: "List of libraries owned by [media company]" articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/List_of_libraries_owned_by_Skydance)** (Articles for Deletion) — A nominator bundled six list articles (covering Skydance, Amazon MGM, Comcast, Sony, Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, and WildBrain) for deletion as original research and catalog-like content; 28 comments and 20 participants have piled in within hours, split between "useful spinout" keep votes and "not our job to be a corporate IP gazetteer" delete votes.

**[Proposal: giving "page movers" special move-protection powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A large, technical permissions proposal with 131 comments and 37 participants, suggesting it has wide community interest even though the discussion has cooled somewhat (last comment ~4.5 days ago).

**[RfC: Should templates containing article body content be allowed?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Should_templates_containing_article_body_content_be_allowed%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — Still fairly active (69 comments, 34 editors, latest 12.5 hours ago), weighing whether templates should be allowed to hold prose that appears directly in multiple articles' bodies — a question with real implications for how consistent, hard-to-vandalize shared text can be maintained across related articles.
