# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI is the dominant storyline across venues right now — there's a major policy Request for Comment (RfC) (Request for Comment) in progress, multiple enforcement disputes, and a browser vendor forcing the issue.

### The big one: promoting the large language model (LLM)-writing guideline to policy
**[Promote WP:LLM from guideline to policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section) is a full-blown Request for Comment on elevating the guideline "Writing articles with large language models" (shorthand WP:LLM) to formal policy status. It is enormous and still moving fast: 413 comments, 97 participants, and the last comment landed under an hour before this report was compiled. The proposer, RoySmith, argues the community already enforces this guideline *as if* it were policy (blocking editors and even desysopping an administrator over violations), so the label should just catch up with practice. Opponents counter that this is either pointless bureaucracy or premature — some want a built-in allowance for legitimate AI-assisted editing before locking the rule in as policy. This is the single most consequential AI governance conversation on Wikipedia right now and is trending toward a real, binding outcome.

A closely related but smaller thread, **[Should something about LLMs be mentioned at the "what Wikipedia is not" policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)**, is wrestling with where (and whether) a human-authorship principle belongs in policy text, with editors disagreeing over whether a catchy "written by humans, for humans" slogan is even accurate anymore given how much undetectable LLM-assisted text already exists.

### A new policy for autonomous AI "agents"
**[Wikipedia:AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) is a fresh proposal — still in pre-vote discussion, 44 comments and climbing within the last 20 minutes — for a short, explicit policy aimed squarely at autonomous AI agents that interact with Wikipedia (as opposed to human editors merely using AI tools). It follows an incident in which an AI agent ("TomWikiAssist") was banned and publicly complained about it. Proposers want something agents can discover and "decide to violate" rather than relying on the dense, process-heavy bot policy, which never even uses the word "agent."

### A browser is already breaking the rules
**["Microsoft Edge just turned into a WP:NOLLM violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy; 55 comments, still active as of 2.5 hours ago) reports that Microsoft Edge now pops up an AI rewriting tool directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, offering to "polish" or "expand" text with one click. Editors worry this will flood the encyclopedia with undisclosed AI-generated prose from well-meaning but rule-unaware editors, and the thread is debating technical opt-outs as well as where to draw the line between "simple grammar correction" (currently permitted) and wholesale AI rewriting (not permitted). This is a time-sensitive, practical enforcement problem rather than an abstract policy debate.

### Enforcement and cleanup of AI-generated content already in articles
- **[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard, 21 comments) is a serious quality-control question: an editor indefinitely blocked for using AI-generated, copyright-infringing content had also personally reviewed and promoted 33 "Good Article" nominations, including pieces on Lady Gaga songs. Editors are now debating whether to systematically re-review all of that editor's past approvals through formal Good Article Reassessment — a real, retroactive consequence of AI misuse reaching into Wikipedia's quality-badge system.
- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Incidents noticeboard, 28 comments, most recent 1.5 hours ago) documents an editor, already topic-banned once for AI-related conduct issues, now accused of inserting a quotation fabricated by Google Gemini into a Falun Gong article, then denying having added it, on top of chronic dominating-the-discussion behavior (the guideline against this is called WP:BLUDGEON).
- **["AI-powered drafting and sealioning"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI-powered_drafting_and_sealioning)** (Incidents, 3 comments but brand new) involves a single-purpose account repeatedly posting AI-generated questions about a rejected draft article at help venues despite being told this isn't allowed, with a side suspicion of an undisclosed conflict of interest.
- Several other shorter Incidents-noticeboard reports touch on AI: **["Unsourced, neutral point of view (NPOV) edits, LLM usage, biographies of living persons (BLP) violations (part 2)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Unsourced%2C_NPOV_edits%2C_LLM_usage%2C_BLP_violations_%28part_2%29)** (biographies of living persons concerns plus AI text), **["Hajiani, Dr.Seyed Firdawoosbee Hanifdeen and AI use"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Hajiani%2C_Dr.Seyed_Firdawoosbee_Hanifdeen_and_AI_use)**, and the **[CIR/LLM report tagged ~2026-47089-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#~2026-47089-17_-_WP%3ACIR_issues_from_a_possible_language_barrier%2C_repeated_and_continuous_failure_to_use_sources%2C_suspected_use_of_WP%3ALLM_to_create_articles)** (competence concerns compounded by suspected AI-generated article creation). None of these individually is a major event, but together they show AI-content enforcement is now a routine, daily category of noticeboard business rather than an edge case.

### Should an AI product have its own Wikipedia article?
**[ChatGPT Deep Research (Articles for Deletion)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/ChatGPT_Deep_Research)** is a small but tidy case of Wikipedia's own notability rules meeting a specific AI product: editors are unanimously leaning toward merging the article on OpenAI's "Deep Research" feature into the main ChatGPT article, arguing the feature doesn't warrant standalone coverage.

### Should Wikipedia build its own AI search tool?
**["AI-assisted search"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Idea Lab, 79 comments, by far the heaviest concentration of AI discussion of any thread with 207 mentions of AI-related terms) is a brainstorming discussion about whether and how Wikipedia's own search function should incorporate AI-generated summaries or semantic search — a question of Wikipedia potentially becoming an AI *producer* rather than just policing AI *consumption* by editors. Given the reader's interest, this slow-burning idea-lab conversation (37 hours since the last comment) is worth watching even though it's less urgent than the policy RfC.

## Other discussions worth attention

1. **[RfC: Should South Asian cinema be placed under contentious-topic sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — By far the largest non-AI discussion running, with 227 comments and 44 participants. It would extend special editing restrictions (requiring confirmed or "extended-confirmed" account status) to Indian, Pakistani, Sri Lankan and Bangladeshi film articles, which editors say are plagued by caste-driven edit wars, nationalist disputes, and — notably — AI-generated "slop" content. It's a governance decision with real teeth (who gets to edit a whole content area) and is still actively being argued.

2. **[Allegations of hounding against User:Sirfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A sprawling, 94-comment dispute (27 participants) over whether one editor has been improperly following another's edits to pick fights, the kind of conduct case that can end in sanctions.

3. **[RationalWiki is canvassing about its own deletion debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#RationalWiki_is_canvassing_about_its_AfD)** (Incidents noticeboard, 44 comments) — The subject of a fourth deletion nomination for its Wikipedia article publicized the debate on its own site, raising "canvassing" (off-wiki recruitment of supporters) concerns; the thread has also turned into a dispute about the nominator's own conduct, a reminder that noticeboard reports often boomerang onto the reporter.

4. **[RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday (1972) article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** — An 85-comment, 26-participant dispute over naming a still-living former soldier in connection with a historic killing, a classic clash between notability/sourcing rules and the biographies-of-living-persons (BLP) policy of caution.

5. **[General sanctions discussion on "Uyghur mass changes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Uyghur_mass_changes_%28general_sanctions_topic%29)** (Incidents noticeboard) — 43 comments with the most recent only 30 minutes old, concerning coordinated or disputed bulk edits in an ethnically and politically sensitive topic area already under general sanctions.

Across several of these threads — the Indian cinema sanctions RfC, the hounding complaint, and multiple shorter Incidents reports — AI-generated content keeps surfacing as an aggravating factor even in disputes that aren't primarily "about" AI, reinforcing how embedded the issue has become in ordinary content and conduct disputes.
