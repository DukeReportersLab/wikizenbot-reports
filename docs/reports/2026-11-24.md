# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI issues are unusually dense across the board right now — from a major policy promotion effort, to a brand-new policy for autonomous AI "agents," to several live disputes over editors accused of secretly using AI, to an AI-written wall of text showing up as evidence at a noticeboard about something else entirely.

**[Promote the large-language-model writing guideline to full policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section) — This is the single biggest discussion on the site right now: 633 comments from 128 editors, so large it had to be split onto its own subpage. The proposal is to upgrade "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) from a guideline to a binding policy, on the grounds that Wikipedia already blocks editors and even removes administrator privileges for violating it, so it should formally carry that weight. Support is broad but not unanimous; some oppose on the grounds that formalizing it won't change behavior, or that it should first include clearer allowances for legitimate AI use. Given the volume of participation, a close is likely to be a landmark moment for how Wikipedia treats AI-generated content going forward.

**[Should Wikipedia's new AI agent policy become official policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_AI_agent_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section — open Request for Comment) — A fast-moving, very fresh discussion (last comment just over two hours old) proposing a dedicated policy for autonomous AI "agents" editing Wikipedia, distinct from the existing bot policy. Proponents argue that large language models are trained to recognize the difference between a "policy" and a mere "guideline," so giving agents an unambiguous, clearly labeled policy (backed by the Wikimedia Foundation's Terms of Use) is the most effective lever for deterring unwanted automated editing. Support is running strong with 31 participants so far.

**[Microsoft Edge's built-in AI rewriting tool conflicts with the no-LLM-writing policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy section) — An editor discovered that Microsoft's Edge browser now pops up an AI rewrite/"polish" tool directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, with prompts like "polish this draft" or "make the tone more empathetic." The discussion is a microcosm of the larger enforcement problem: such browser-level AI tools go well beyond the policy's narrow exception for grammar/spelling assistance, and could flood the encyclopedia with unlabeled AI-smoothed prose from well-meaning editors who never deliberately sought out an AI tool. Technical mitigations (an HTML attribute that can disable the feature) are being discussed, alongside calls to simply eliminate the "spelling and grammar" exception that's seen as a loophole.

**[Conduct dispute centered on accusations of secret AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — An editor who was accused by two others of using a large language model asked for "independent review" of how those accusers handled the dispute — but reviewers quickly noted the review request itself showed telltale signs of being AI-generated, undercutting the editor's claim that they only use AI for "source discovery." With 131 comments and extremely heavy AI discussion throughout, this thread illustrates how difficult it has become to adjudicate AI-use accusations, and it edges toward a possible block if the editor can't reconcile their explanation with the evidence.

**[A sock-puppet network apparently automating malicious edits with AI/scripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Interesting_case_of_agentic_or_LLM-assisted_abuse_%28Update%3A_actually%2C_action_is_needed_now%29)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A detailed investigation into dozens of accounts that created near-identical fake user pages and sandbox edit patterns before vandalizing articles (such as replacing legitimate links with malicious ones) across many language Wikipedias simultaneously, with telltale signs — missing standard editing-tool tags, multi-language synchronization, and adapted "retry" behavior after reverts — suggesting scripted or AI-agent-driven abuse rather than a single human. Admins have already blocked and globally locked several accounts and added a filter, but the thread's author is using it to push for better detection of this kind of automated, multi-account abuse going forward.

**Other notable AI-related activity, briefly:**
- **[Accusations of AI use being weaponized in a content dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Carrot_juice%2C_aspersions%2C_and_AGF)** (Incidents noticeboard) — an editor reports being repeatedly, baselessly accused of secretly using a chatbot (among other bad-faith accusations) during a content dispute over the "plant intelligence" article, raising the broader question of how casually "you used AI" is now thrown around as an attack.
- **[An apparent AI-generated wall of text submitted to the incident noticeboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#URGENT%3A_Repeated_Deletion_of_Robert_A._Dressler%E2%80%99s_Documented_Family%2C_Unsupported_Parentage_Claims_and_Public_Accusations_%E2%80%94_Request_for_Blocks_and_Page_Protection)**, flagged and collapsed by other editors as likely large-language-model output, underneath what is otherwise a family/biography-of-living-persons dispute — a vivid example of AI-generated text now showing up even in formal complaints.
- **[An editor's unblock request was denied, in part, for being AI-generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_for_LLM_misuse)** (now closed/archived): an editor indefinitely blocked for large-language-model misuse had their appeal rejected partly because the appeal itself appeared AI-written, and because they were caught evading the block in the meantime — a clear case study in how AI-related sanctions are currently being enforced.
- **[A proposal for an official "AI-assisted search" feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Idea Lab): a long, 86-comment brainstorm about whether Wikipedia should build its own AI search assistant that strictly surfaces and summarizes real Wikipedia content, as a way of competing with external AI chatbots that are siphoning off readers.
- **[An AfC (Articles for Creation) reviewer's unusually fast approvals of likely AI-assisted, promotional drafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F)** is under scrutiny at the Incidents noticeboard, echoing an earlier banned-editor case.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[Renewed conflict-of-interest edit-warring by Ggck2 on "Flavonoid"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (Incidents noticeboard) — The most urgent non-AI item: 120 comments and still active within the last two hours. An editor previously warned for undisclosed conflict-of-interest editing on flavonoid/nutrition topics returned from a month away and immediately resumed edit-warring, then filed what several editors call a "frivolous" case with the Arbitration Committee. A topic ban from food science and nutrition is the leading proposal.

**[Should South Asian cinema be placed under tighter contentious-topic restrictions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals section — open Request for Comment) — An enormous Request for Comment (243 comments, 53 participants) on whether to impose special editing restrictions (ranging from "extended-confirmed" status requirements to full autoconfirmed-only editing) on South Asian film articles, which have become a magnet for caste- and nationalism-driven disputes — and, as several participants note, for a wave of low-quality AI-generated content. This would be a significant expansion of Wikipedia's contentious-topics system if adopted.

**[Request to partially block a prolific creator of low-quality, barely-sourced articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User_Boomfour_-_Request_partial_block%3A_Chronic_introduction_of_barebone%2C_poor_quality_articles%2C_deliberate_circumvention_of_the_AfC_process%2C_failure_to_communicate)** (Incidents noticeboard) — 51 comments, still active, concerning an editor accused of routinely bypassing the Articles for Creation review process with thin, poorly sourced articles and refusing to communicate about it.

**[Dispute over a citation-fixing bot allegedly causing damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AHeadbomb%5D%5D_running_citation_bot_in_a_damaging_fashion)** (Incidents noticeboard) — 30 comments, active within the last 8 hours, over whether a widely used citation-formatting bot is being run in a way that introduces errors faster than they're fixed — a dispute with real consequences for article quality at scale.

**[Request for comment on the reliability of researcher Adrian Zenz on Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AXinjiang_re-education_camps#RfC%3A_Reliability_of_Adrian_Zenz)** (article talk page RfC) — One of three simultaneous, related RfCs on the same contentious topic (how to describe the Xinjiang re-education camps), this one on source reliability saw its most recent comment only 18 minutes before this report was compiled, reflecting ongoing, high-stakes disagreement over how to characterize a politically sensitive and disputed historical topic.

**Pattern to note:** AI shows up not just in dedicated policy threads but as a recurring undercurrent in unrelated disputes — conflict-of-interest fights, contentious-topic proposals, and even a biography dispute — suggesting that accusations and realities of AI use are becoming entangled with nearly every category of Wikipedia conflict, not just the discussions explicitly about AI policy.
