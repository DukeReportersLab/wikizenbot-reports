# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI is clearly the dominant theme in today's discussions, spanning formal policy debates, a feature proposal, and a cluster of conduct cases where editors (and even an administrator) are accused of using or defending AI-generated content.

### Policy and guideline debates

**[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy) — This large Request for Comment (153 comments, 47 participants) just closed with **"no change"** to the guideline against using large language models on Wikipedia (shortcut: NOLLM). The proposal would have explicitly permitted or explicitly banned using AI to format the underlying markup code of an article (Wikitext). The closer found that most editors still don't trust AI even for "basic" formatting tasks, so the existing ambiguous guideline stands. This is the most substantial AI policy discussion running right now and sets the tone for several of the conduct disputes below.

**[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — A related, slower-burning discussion (31 comments) asking whether the guideline on AI-assisted talk-page comments (shortcut: LLMTALK) actually bans AI-generated comments the way NOLLM bans AI-generated articles, or merely allows AI-"refined" comments. Editors agree the guideline's wording is outdated and looser than practice on the ground, where repeated LLM use in discussions is often treated as a sign the editor can't communicate competently enough to collaborate.

**[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/LLM are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — A smaller companion thread (21 comments) asking the community to clarify, in plain terms, which AI uses are safe. It underscores how much confusion the ambiguity above is causing for ordinary editors.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A substantial brainstorm (69 comments, 194 AI mentions) proposing that Wikipedia build its own AI-powered natural-language search feature to compete with chatbots that are siphoning off traffic, while keeping the AI strictly as a "pointer" to real, human-written Wikipedia content rather than a generator of new text. Reaction is mixed, with skeptics asking how this differs from just using an external chatbot with Wikipedia as a source.

**[Template rejection for AI-generated unblock requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Template_rejection_for_AI-generated_unblock_req)** (Village Pump, proposals) — Quickly resolved: a proposal to create a standard curt rejection template for blocked editors who submit AI-written unblock appeals turned out to already exist (`{{Decline-ai}}`). Minor, but shows how normalized AI-detection has become in routine administrative workflow.

### Conduct cases and enforcement

**[Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** plus the related **[administrator recall petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ADavidLevinson%7CDavidLevinson%5D%5D)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — This is the most consequential AI-adjacent story right now. An administrator, self-disclosed as a transportation-studies professor, added roughly 150 citations to his own papers and journals across Wikipedia in a short window — with edit timing and volume that raised suspicion of automated or AI-assisted bot use. The community reaction was swift: the recall petition against him has now **passed with 25 supports**, meaning he must stand for a re-request for adminship or lose his tools. This combines a conflict-of-interest spam problem with a concrete administrative accountability consequence.

**[Itsekiriwriting: Continued AI use and disruptive editing after being warned (part 2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Itsekiriwriting%3A_Continued_AI_use_and_disruptive_editing_after_being_warned_%28part_2%29)** (ANI) — A second noticeboard filing against an editor who kept inserting telltale AI-generated text patterns into articles while giving inconsistent explanations of their AI use. The editor has now been **indefinitely blocked**, after admins rejected the claim that "refining wording" with AI was ever permitted.

**[User:FavCourtier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3AFavCourtier)** (ANI) — Closed with a **community ban**. The editor submitted a stream of AI-generated or undisclosed AI-assisted drafts about Holocaust-related topics, then responded to scrutiny with a long, emotionally charged, and suspicion-raising reply (including vague threats that foreign governments would intervene). A stark example of how AI-generated content issues can surface in sensitive, high-stakes topic areas.

**[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)** (ANI) — A 13-year veteran editor is accused of repeatedly adding unsourced, duplicated content to election articles, including one edit that literally pasted a chatbot's conversational output (meant for the editor, not the encyclopedia) directly into an article. Still unresolved; frustration is building that no administrative action has been taken despite repeated warnings.

**[Smokinmans – sock / AI-use / undisclosed paid editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Smokinmans_-_sock_/_AI-use_/_UPE.)** (ANI) — A lively, slightly comic thread debating whether a three-day-old account editing only Elon Musk-related companies is a sockpuppet of a blocked user or an AI-assisted newcomer, based on "tells" like an overly polished userpage. It's a good window into how editors informally try to detect AI use — and how contested that detection can be.

**[Advertising on Wikipedia's main page with LLM content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Advertising_on_Wikipedia%27s_main_page_with_LLM_content)** (ANI) — Brand new (posted minutes before this report). An article about a mobile game, apparently AI-generated (complete with a leftover hidden instruction originally meant for the LLM rather than readers), was nominated and approved for the "Did You Know" feature and briefly displayed on Wikipedia's most-viewed page before deletion. A proposed topic ban from main-page processes is on the table for the conflict-of-interest editor involved. Worth watching as it develops.

**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Not a conduct case but a systemic one: a volunteer who reviews conflict-of-interest edit requests presented data showing the queue has exploded this year (from roughly 150/month historically to nearly 500 in September alone), attributing the surge directly to AI making it easier for paid and conflicted editors to generate polished-looking requests. This is a quietly important data point about AI's structural effect on Wikipedia's workload.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A sprawling month-long dispute (321 comments, 66 participants) over an editor's abrasive conduct culminated in a **site ban** for the long-time editor known as "Roxy the dog." The closing admin had to weigh four competing proposals (ban, six-month block, doubled block lengths, and conditional unblock) — a good example of how a single behavioral complaint can snowball into a major community sanction.

**[RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** (article talk page) — An active, evenly-split debate (72 comments) over whether to name a British soldier accused of killings during the 1972 "Bloody Sunday" events in Northern Ireland, whose identity is protected by a UK court order but has been reported in the press. This pits the policy on biographies of living persons against the principle that Wikipedia is not censored, with a previous 2023 attempt having ended in no consensus.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A technical governance proposal (110 comments) to create a new, intermediate page-move protection level usable by trusted "page mover" editors without requiring a full administrator, similar to how template-editor protection already works. Broad early support, with debate mostly over implementation details.

A pattern worth noting: several of today's most significant threads (DavidLevinson, FavCourtier, Sandeep kumar 83, Itsekiriwriting) show administrators and the community converging on the same message — undisclosed or dishonestly-described AI use is being treated increasingly harshly, up to and including bans, regardless of the editor's seniority or credentials.
