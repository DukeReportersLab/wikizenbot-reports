# Wikipedia Discussion Report

AI-related activity is unusually dense across venues right now — not just the expected policy RfCs, but concrete enforcement actions (a community ban, an indefinite block, an admin's conduct under fire) and structural strain (a conflict-of-interest backlog now partly blamed on AI-written requests). I've grouped these first, then covered the other discussions worth attention.

## AI-related discussions

### Policy: how far does the AI ban reach?
Three overlapping threads on the Village Pump (policy) — the main forum for policy proposals — are trying to pin down the boundaries of the rule against using large language models (LLMs, the technology behind chatbots like ChatGPT) to generate Wikipedia content, a guideline nicknamed "NOLLM."

- **[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** — This is the big one: 150 comments from 45 editors, still getting replies after 33 hours. It's a follow-up to an earlier inconclusive discussion about whether AI may be used to format Wikipedia's underlying markup syntax (citations, tables, etc.) even if not to write prose. Early voting is already split between "no change," "explicitly permit it with safeguards," and "explicitly prohibit it," with some editors complaining this is the second RfC on nearly the same question in weeks. Given the size and the fact it will likely produce binding wording, this is the single most consequential AI discussion happening right now.
- **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** — A parallel argument over whether the rule against AI-generated *article* content should also apply to AI-generated *talk-page comments*. Editors are debating a carve-out for AI-assisted translation versus a blanket ban, with one editor noting that repeated LLM use in discussions is already being treated informally as a sign of incompetence to communicate — "botspeak" being worse, in practice, than imperfect English.
- **[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/LLM are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** — A smaller, related thread asking editors to simply list what's settled, reflecting the broader confusion these other two threads are trying to resolve.

### Enforcement: AI misuse is producing real sanctions
- **[Up to 400 recent AfDs may contain AI-generated !votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Up_to_400_recent_AfDs_may_contain_AI-generated_%21votes)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, ANI) — This has just closed with the user **Kukuryza community banned**. The case involved an editor posting votes in Articles for Deletion (AfD) debates at suspiciously regular 3–6 minute intervals for eight hours straight, including confidently assessing sources in languages the editor had no prior connection to (Romanian, Hindi) within minutes — a strong signature of an automated or AI-assisted voting pattern. With up to 400 affected debates, this is a genuinely significant integrity incident for the deletion process, now resolved.
- **[Martinpri – blocked for AI, promised to stop, didn't](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Martinpri_-_blocked_for_AI%2C_said_they_wouldn%27t_use_AI_anymore%2C_you%27ll_never_guess_what_happened_next)** (ANI) — A short but telling case: an editor unblocked after promising not to use AI to "identify citations" was caught a week later adding a reference copy-pasted straight from ChatGPT (complete with a `utm_source=chatgpt.com` tracking tag in the URL). Now indefinitely blocked.
- **[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)** (ANI) — A 13-year veteran editor accused of pasting raw, unedited chatbot output into election articles — in one case, literally including the chatbot's own conversational preamble meant for the user, not the encyclopedia.
- **[BrowseTQ Battlegrounding & IDHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT)** (ANI) — Mainly a conduct dispute over a rejected draft biography, but notable for a side-argument in which an editor forensically dates different paragraphs of the disputed draft to different "eras" of AI chatbot writing style (2024-era versus mid-2026-era phrasing), illustrating how AI-text detection is becoming routine evidence in content disputes.

### Systemic pressure: AI is reshaping Wikipedia's workload
- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard, AN) — A volunteer who reviews conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests — submissions from people with a personal or financial stake in an article, who are asked to propose changes rather than edit directly — presented data showing request volume has roughly doubled in 2026 alone, and attributes this explicitly to AI: either AI has gotten better at producing surface-plausible requests, or COI editors have simply started using it en masse. The backlog is now large enough that reviewers can't keep pace.
- **[Spam and possible AI bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (AN) — An administrator is accused of adding roughly 150 self-citations to his own academic journals across articles, with a burst of 90+ edits in under four hours at a pace ("2 minutes apart") that raised suspicion of automated or AI-assisted editing on top of the underlying self-promotion problem. The community is pushing for a voluntary resignation of admin tools; a formal **[administrator recall petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ADavidLevinson%7CDavidLevinson%5D%5D)** has since been opened against him — worth watching for where this lands.
- **[Newcomer tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** (Village Pump, miscellaneous) — A sprawling 328-comment thread (the largest in this report) where new and experienced editors alike describe the automated "suggested edit" system as actively harmful: it points newcomers toward articles they have no expertise to fix, generates vague instructions like "remove bias" where none exists, and drives away exactly the contributors it's meant to recruit. Given its size and the recurring references to AI-related tooling within it, this is worth monitoring even though the opening complaints are about the suggestion algorithm generally rather than generative AI specifically.
- **[AI noticeboard overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#AI_noticeboard_overhaul)** (AN) — A brief announcement that the dedicated noticeboard for reporting AI-related problems has been restructured and streamlined, a sign of how much traffic that venue is now handling.

### Proposals
- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A proposal to add an AI-powered natural-language search feature to Wikipedia itself, as a defense against readers migrating to chatbots instead of the encyclopedia. Discussion is skeptical, with one editor asking why this would differ from just prompting a general-purpose chatbot to only cite Wikipedia sources.
- **[Bot proposal: automatic infobox addition for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A bot proposal to add infoboxes to ~9,000–12,000 company articles, using an LLM (Claude Opus) as a safety filter to flag uncertain cases for human review. Several editors are uneasy about deploying an LLM inside automated editing at all, even in this constrained, low-risk role.

## Other discussions worth attention

1. **[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (AN) — Despite the anodyne heading, this 321-comment, month-long discussion just closed with a **site ban for the long-time editor Roxy the dog**, over a pattern of hostility and incivility across multiple article talk pages. One of the largest and most consequential closures in this report.
2. **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (ANI) — A heated, still-active (243 comments, last reply under 4 hours ago) conduct dispute over unsourced flight-schedule content in airport articles, with accusations of forum-shopping and bullying flying in both directions.
3. **[Should this be characterized in relation to CSAM?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** (Request for Comment on the Lolicon talk page) — A contentious, actively-discussed RfC (109 comments, last reply under 3 hours ago) on whether the lead sentence should explicitly describe this genre of illustrated media as child sexual abuse material (CSAM). High-stakes for a sensitive biographies-of-living-persons-adjacent topic area, and notable to this reader because the discussion was cross-posted to the "Generative AI pornography" talk page, pointing to a related front in the same debate.
4. **[TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** (ANI) — A proposed topic ban (TBAN) against a nutrition researcher with a declared conflict of interest, who returned under a new username after a previous block and continues to edit articles related to his own research funders.
5. **[ARBPIA Topic Ban Appeal (JDiala)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#ARBPIA_Topic_Ban_Appeal_%28JDiala%29)** (AN) — An appeal of a topic ban from the Arab-Israeli conflict area (one of Wikipedia's most tightly restricted subject areas), drawing 52 comments from 27 editors.
