# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussion Monitor

## AI-related discussions

Artificial intelligence is the dominant theme across the noticeboards right now, touching policy-making, quality control, and conduct enforcement all at once.

### Policy and governance proposals

- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — The single most AI-saturated thread right now (nearly 200 AI-related term mentions, 74 comments, 22 participants). A editor has proposed that Wikipedia build its own large language model (LLM)-powered search assistant to stem the loss of readers to commercial AI chatbots, while keeping the current plain search box and insisting any such tool only surface verbatim Wikipedia content rather than generate independent answers. Replies are split between cautious interest and skepticism that this duplicates what any chatbot can already do.
- **[Wikipedia:AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** and the related **[Agentic playpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Agentic_playpen)** proposal (Village Pump, policy) — Editors are drafting a dedicated policy explicitly naming "AI agents" (autonomous AI systems that act on Wikipedia with little human oversight), prompted by a prior incident where an AI agent that had been banned complained publicly about censorship. A companion idea for a sandboxed "agentic playpen" where agents could edit safely was floated but retracted by its own proposer as unworkable. This is a serious, if early-stage, attempt to get ahead of autonomous AI editing before it becomes a bigger problem.
- **[WP:LLMPROD and WP:RFU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — A practical loophole: articles deleted through the streamlined process for presumed AI-generated content (LLMPROD) can currently be restored to the live encyclopedia on request, the same as any other proposed deletion, with no obligation to actually fix the AI-generated text. Consensus is forming that such restorations should go to draft space with a mandatory warning label instead.
- **[Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — A quieter but constructive thread in which editors are actually fixing the problem: the guideline against using AI-generated text in articles (WP:NOLLM) is being added more prominently to new-user help pages, article-creation wizards, and welcome templates after an editor found it was buried three clicks deep and barely mentioned.
- **[Microsoft Edge just turned into a NOLLM-violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — Brand new (under an hour old) but worth watching: an editor noticed Microsoft's Edge browser now injects an in-line "rewrite this text" AI button directly into the Wikipedia edit box, which could cause good-faith editors to unknowingly violate the no-LLM-writing guideline. Early replies are about whether Wikipedia can detect or opt out of it technically.

### Admin accountability and quality control fallout

- **[Admin Rehman partially blocked for artificial intelligence use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A sitting administrator has been partially blocked from article space after an investigation into AI use, and the block is now itself under formal review, with a separate recall petition against the admin also underway. This is the kind of high-stakes, real-consequence AI story the reader should track closely as it's likely to escalate.
- **[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — An editor already indefinitely blocked from mainspace for AI and copyright problems turns out to have personally reviewed and approved 29 "Good Article" promotions, including pieces with repetitive, AI-typical prose that slipped through. The community is now debating whether to mass-reassess all of that editor's prior reviews — a rare case of AI contamination being traced through the quality-assurance pipeline itself.
- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A backlog-reduction appeal turned into hard data on AI's effect on Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest workflow: requests from paid/COI editors have more than doubled since last year, which volunteers attribute largely to AI making it trivially easy to generate lengthy, "passable" edit requests, overwhelming the humans who have to vet them.

### Conduct disputes and enforcement involving AI

A cluster of Incidents noticeboard (ANI) cases shows AI misuse is now a routine feature of conduct disputes, not just an occasional one:

- **[Amandamillsaps74: continually restoring AI-generated content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AAmandamillsaps74%5D%5D%3A_Continually_restoring_AI-generated_content_despite_several_warnings)** — An editor openly declared they would keep re-adding blatant "AI slop" and game the three-revert rule by waiting out the 24-hour window; partially blocked, but editors doubt it will stick.
- **["AI agent"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent)** — Already closed: an account whose userpage admitted it was "operated by Muse, an AI assistant" was indefinitely blocked for violating the no-LLM-writing guideline.
- **[Several likely AI-run accounts that may be sockpuppets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Several_likely_AI-run_accounts_that_may_be_sockpuppets_editing_sexuality/romanticism-related_articles)** — Also closed: a ring of four accounts appeared to be using AI to write fabricated content and then vouch for each other's edits in talk-page discussions; all were blocked as sockpuppets and the article was deleted.
- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** — An editor previously topic-banned partly over AI-generated talk-page comments is now accused of fabricating a quotation with Google's Gemini chatbot, attributing it to a real scholarly book, inserting it into an article, and then denying having done so.
- **[Concerns about ConflictFan's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concerns_about_%7B%7BUser%7CConflictFan%7D%7D%27s_conduct)** and **[Concern about Sricsi's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concern_about_Sricsi%27s_conduct_and_interactions_with_other_editors)** — Two separate disputes where accusations of AI-written text are tangled up with broader civility and ownership complaints, illustrating how "you used AI" has become a common accusation to lob in otherwise ordinary content disputes.

**Pattern to note:** across nearly every ANI case this week, AI accusations are not isolated technical complaints — they're entangled with civility problems, sockpuppetry, and edit-warring, suggesting AI misuse is increasingly just one symptom of broader disruptive-editing patterns rather than a standalone issue.

## Other discussions worth attention

1. **[Request for Comment (RfC): Indian cinema as a contentious topic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — The single largest discussion anywhere right now: 220 comments from 42 editors debating whether to formally designate Indian cinema articles as a "contentious topic" (a designation that unlocks faster sanctions for edit-warring and disruption). Wide participation over many days rather than a sudden flare-up, but its scale alone makes it worth watching for the final close.
2. **["Brainstorming" over the new Main Page feature (TWAFI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Brainstorming_ways_to_resolve_the_gap_with_TWAFI_on_the_Main_Page)** and the related **[technical thread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28technical%29#TWAFI_coming_to_the_Main_Page_soon)** — A sprawling, 349-comment argument (68 participants) over how a new "this week's article for improvement" feature should be laid out on the Main Page, a reminder that even cosmetic changes to Wikipedia's most-viewed page generate enormous community debate.
3. **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A 124-comment, 36-participant proposal to create a new protection level restricting page moves, slow-moving but broadly attended.
4. **[RfC: Merge nominations at Miscellany for deletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Merge_nominations_at_MfD)** (Village Pump, policy) — 128 comments on whether merge proposals belong in the miscellany-deletion process at all; a structural housekeeping question with real workflow implications.
5. **[Wikijacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (Incidents noticeboard) — An editor accuses another of trying to "steal" their contributions by deleting duplicated content for reuse elsewhere; mostly a teaching moment about Wikipedia's content-licensing rules (all contributions are released for anyone to reuse), but notable for the heated back-and-forth it produced.
6. **[User:Issac I Navarro – request for temporary block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AIssac_I_Navarro%5D%5D_-_request_temporary_block)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A sprawling 77-comment dispute over disruptive editing on school articles that has grown so unwieldy even the filer has asked for it to be closed and possibly escalated to formal arbitration instead — a good example of an Incidents thread that outgrew its venue.
