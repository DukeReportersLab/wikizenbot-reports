# WikiZenBot Report

## AI-related discussions

Wikipedia's AI policy machinery is in overdrive right now, with multiple interlocking discussions about how strictly large language model (LLM) use should be policed — easily the most important story for anyone tracking AI and Wikipedia today.

**[Promote the large-language-model writing guideline to full policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy%3F)** (Village Pump, policy section) — This is the big one: a formal Request for Comment with 318 comments from 83 editors, still getting replies within the hour. The proposal is to elevate "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) from a guideline to a full policy. Proponents argue the community already enforces it like policy — blocking editors and even desysopping administrators for violations — so the label should match the practice; opponents worry this forecloses any future path for legitimate AI-assisted editing and makes the rule harder to amend. Given the participation level and momentum, this looks likely to result in a real, binding change to how AI-generated content is governed.

**[What happens when an "LLM-PROD" deletion gets requested back?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — A sharper, more technical companion debate: articles deleted via the expedited "proposed deletion for LLM content" process (WP:LLMPROD) are increasingly being requested back through the ordinary undeletion process (WP:RFU), which normally restores deleted pages without question. Editors are converging on a fix — restore to draft space only, with a mandatory warning banner, rather than straight back to the encyclopedia — because people are currently able to get AI-generated drafts restored to mainspace by simply asserting (without doing) the review that's supposed to be required. One case cited involves a request where the undeletion request itself appeared to be AI-written.

**[Good Article reviews by a now-blocked AI-content editor are under scrutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — After an editor was indefinitely blocked in September for using AI-generated content and copyright violations, it has emerged they also personally reviewed and promoted 29 articles to "Good Article" status, including pieces about Lady Gaga songs that other editors had already flagged for AI-style prose (repetitive phrasing like the word "highlighted" appearing eight times in one section). The community is now discussing a coordinated batch of Good Article Reassessments to re-check all of this editor's approvals — a concrete, retroactive consequence of AI misuse rippling through the quality-review system.

**[Microsoft Edge's built-in AI rewriting tool is quietly violating the no-LLM rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — An editor noticed Microsoft's Edge browser now offers an inline "rewrite this" button while editing Wikipedia, with prompts like "polish this draft" and "make the tone more empathetic." The debate is whether this falls under the narrow allowance for AI-assisted grammar/spelling fixes, or whether it's a stealth path for undisclosed AI rewriting baked into browser software millions of people use by default — with one editor flagging it as exactly the kind of loophole ("I just used it for grammar") that keeps getting exploited.

**Other active AI threads worth flagging briefly:**
- **[Should a proposed "AI agent policy" be adopted?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — a 39-comment discussion on rules specifically for autonomous AI agents (as opposed to human-prompted chatbot assistance) editing Wikipedia.
- **[Should the "What Wikipedia is not" policy explicitly mention LLMs?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — a smaller but related proposal to bake AI restrictions into a core content policy.
- **["AI-assisted search" idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (idea lab) — a brainstorm (78 comments) about the Wikimedia Foundation building its own LLM-powered natural-language search tool restricted to Wikimedia sources, as a defense against readers drifting to commercial chatbots.
- **[Several likely AI-run sockpuppet accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Several_likely_AI-run_accounts_that_may_be_sockpuppets_editing_sexuality/romanticism-related_articles)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — already resolved: four suspiciously similar accounts that appeared to be AI-generated and even vouched for each other's "no AI was used" claims on a fabricated-sounding article have all been blocked as a sockpuppet ring, and the underlying article deleted.
- **[A self-report for "unknowing" LLM use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Self-report_for_unknowing_LLM_use)** and **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (both Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, known as ANI) are smaller individual conduct cases that touch on undisclosed or disputed AI use.

**Pattern to note:** almost every major AI thread right now is about tightening enforcement and closing loopholes (undeletion, browser tools, agent behavior, retroactive review of past AI-tainted approvals) rather than expanding what AI is allowed to do — a sign the community's mood has hardened since the original LLM guideline was adopted.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[Alleged hounding by Sirfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (ANI) — The most active thread on the whole incidents board right now (59 comments, still getting replies within the last hour). An editor accuses another of a year-long pattern of tracking and reverting their edits across politically charged sidebar templates (on Trump-era immigration policy, "authoritarianism," and the Gaza war). It's a genuinely contentious behavioral dispute with potential for sanctions on one or both sides, playing out in real time.

**[RationalWiki is canvassing about its own deletion debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#RationalWiki_is_canvassing_about_its_AfD)** (ANI) — The nominator of a fourth deletion debate about the RationalWiki article discovered the site discussing the nomination on its own forum, including a semi-anonymous account admitting to effectively evading scrutiny ("I have a Wikipedia account... I'm not going to use it"). The thread has devolved into a heated argument about whether this counts as improper off-wiki canvassing, with accusations of bludgeoning (dominating the discussion with repeated replies) flying in both directions — a classic cross-wiki meta-conflict.

**[Large RfC on Indian cinema as a "contentious topic"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A sprawling Request for Comment (225 comments, 43 participants) on whether Indian film-related articles should get special "contentious topic" procedural protections (extra sourcing/behavioral rules used in heated subject areas). Large and still moving, though not at crisis speed.

**[Geographic disambiguation of US school article titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3ARequests_for_comment/Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (standalone Request for Comment) — A deceptively mundane-sounding naming-convention RfC that has drawn 217 comments and 29 participants, suggesting a lot of editors have strong feelings about how to disambiguate school names.

**[Bloody Sunday (1972): should the article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** (article RfC) — An 85-comment, 26-participant RfC over naming a still-living former soldier in connection with the 1972 Troubles killings — a sensitive biographies-of-living-persons (BLP) question with real reputational stakes, still being actively discussed.
