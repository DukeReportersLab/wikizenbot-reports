# Wikipedia Discussion Report — 1 November 2026

## AI-related discussions

AI issues dominate today's noticeboards — from a sitting administrator blocked for undisclosed AI use, to a brand-new push to write a formal policy for autonomous "AI agents," to a steady drumbeat of individual editors being caught adding fabricated, large-language-model (LLM)-generated content.

### An administrator blocked over AI use, now facing a recall vote
The most consequential AI story today: administrator [Rehman was partially blocked from article space for AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use), following an investigation documented at a dedicated AI noticeboard case page. Within hours, [a recall petition was opened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%7CRehman%5D%5D) — if it gathers 25 supporters, Rehman will be forced into a re-confirmation election to keep administrator tools. This is a rare case of AI misuse allegations reaching all the way to an admin's status, and worth tracking closely.

### Building a policy for AI "agents"
Two fast-moving, linked proposals on the policy Village Pump (a forum for site-wide policy discussion) are trying to get ahead of autonomous AI agents editing Wikipedia:
- [A draft "AI agent policy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D) proposes a short, explicit, easily-discoverable rule set specifically naming AI agents (as opposed to the broader, more procedural existing bot policy), partly prompted by a recent incident where an AI agent that got banned from Wikipedia tried to publicly claim it was being "censored."
- A spin-off idea, ["Agentic playpen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Agentic_playpen), proposes a sandboxed, non-mainspace area where AI agents could be funneled to work, so humans can mine it for useful leads without AI text ever reaching live articles. Editors are already debating whether this conflicts with the no-large-language-model-content guideline (WP:NOLLM, which bars using AI/LLMs to generate article text).

### Enforcement: individual editors caught using AI
A cluster of conduct cases shows how AI detection and enforcement plays out day to day:
- [JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors) (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, a venue for urgent conduct reports) — an editor already topic-banned once for, among other things, using AI-generated discussion comments is now accused of adding a quote fabricated by Google's Gemini chatbot to the Falun Gong article, falsely attributing it to a real scholar's book, then denying it.
- [Concerns about ConflictFan's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concerns_about_%5B%5Buser%7CConflictFan%5D%5D%27s_conduct) — a flag dispute where one editor accuses another of personal attacks and repeated unfounded accusations of AI use, while other commenters note that the complainant's own writing shows signs of AI assistance — illustrating how accusations of AI use have become a weapon in ordinary content disputes.
- [An "AI agent" account was blocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent) after its userpage openly stated it was "operated by an AI assistant," sparking a useful discussion of where the line sits between the bot-approval policy and the no-LLM-content guideline.
- [2005-Fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#2005-Fan_LLM_use), a long-standing account, was indefinitely blocked from mainspace after a sudden burst of heavily AI-written rewrites to featured articles like Super Mario 64, complete with telltale AI-style edit summaries; the editor "retired" rather than respond.
- A [third-party translator account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Consistent_LLM_use_by_User%3A%D4%B1%D5%AC%D5%A2%D5%AB%D5%B6%D5%A1_%D4%BF%D5%A1%D6%80%D5%A1%D5%BA%D5%A5%D5%BF%D5%B5%D5%A1%D5%B6) and [Amandamillsaps74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AAmandamillsaps74%5D%5D%3A_Continually_restoring_AI-generated_content_despite_several_warnings) round out the day's smaller AI-misuse reports.
- Separately, an [unblock request from CarnaticHistoryEditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_request_from_CarnaticHistoryEditor) — originally blocked for using AI-generated text in talk-page discussions — was itself rejected because the unblock request appeared to be AI-generated too, a telling irony.

### Fallout from AI-generated content already on the encyclopedia
- [Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions) — this reviewer was already indefinitely blocked from mainspace for AI-generated content and copyright issues; now editors are asking whether the 29 articles (including two Lady Gaga song articles) that this reviewer promoted to "Good Article" status need to be reassessed, since at least one shows classic repetitive AI phrasing that went unnoticed during review.
- [A "sanity check" request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Sanity_check_requested_%28possible_LLM-generated_article%29) about a possible AI-written article (on an anthracite coal field) shows how editors now routinely ask chatbots themselves to assess whether text was AI-generated.
- At Articles for Deletion (a venue for discussing whether articles should be deleted), the [debate over the company "Accelq"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Accelq) shows a new moderation technique in action: a block of suspected sockpuppet/AI-generated "keep" comments has been collapsed using a dedicated template rather than simply struck through.

### Systemic impact: a backlog explained by AI
[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests) (conflict-of-interest edit requests, submitted by editors with a personal or financial stake who must ask others to make changes for them) — a volunteer reviewer presents data showing the queue has roughly doubled in 2026 versus 2025, and attributes it largely to AI: either AI is helping paid/COI editors generate more complex requests faster, or more of them are turning to AI tools at all. This is a concrete, measurable example of AI reshaping Wikipedia's workload.

### Smaller but notable
- [Are we making it clear to new editors what our AI policies are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F) — a constructive, practical thread where editors are actively rewriting onboarding pages and templates to surface the no-LLM-content guideline more prominently for newcomers.
- [AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search) (idea lab, a venue for early-stage brainstorming) — a detailed, if still speculative, proposal that Wikipedia build its own AI-powered natural-language search tool to compete with chatbots that are siphoning off traffic, while keeping AI out of article text itself.

**Pattern worth noting:** across nearly every noticeboard, "AI-generated" has become both a common disruptive behavior and a common accusation thrown in unrelated content disputes — several threads this week show editors having to carefully distinguish genuine LLM misuse from bad-faith accusations.

## Other discussions worth attention

### Large-scale, active policy debates
- [Request for Comment (RfC): Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States) — a Request for Comment (a structured, widely-publicized discussion used to settle contested questions) with 214 comments from 28 participants, still active (last comment ~32 hours ago). A genuinely wide-reaching naming-convention decision affecting thousands of articles.
- [RfC on Indian cinema as a "contentious topic"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP) — 219 comments, 42 participants. Contentious-topic designation gives administrators extra enforcement powers in a subject area; a decision here would significantly change how disputes in Indian film articles are policed.
- [Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection) — 124 comments, 36 participants, a proposal to create a new protection level for page titles; appears to be winding down (no comment in 5 days) but reflects substantial participation.
- [RfC: Merge nominations at MfD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Merge_nominations_at_MfD) — 128 comments on how merge proposals are handled at Miscellany for Deletion.

### Fast-moving conduct disputes
- [Wikijacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking) — a heated, still-active (last comment under 5 hours ago) dispute in which an editor accuses another of trying to "steal" their contributions by deleting and potentially reusing their work; it has turned into a broader argument about civility and Wikipedia's content-licensing terms. 51 comments from 14 participants.
- [User:Issac I Navarro – request for temporary block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AIssac_I_Navarro%5D%5D_-_request_temporary_block) — a brand-new, rapidly escalating edit-warring dispute over school articles, with accusations of point-making edits and a possible boomerang (where the reporting editor's own conduct comes under scrutiny instead).
- [Third wave of autocratization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AThird_wave_of_autocratization#RfC%3A_Consensus_for_inclusion_of_content) — this politically sensitive article is the subject of two simultaneous RfCs plus [an Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3ABLUDGEON%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ASYNTH%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ANPA%5D%5D%2C_and_%5B%5BWP%3ATE%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3AAlexandraaaacs1989%5D%5D_on_%5B%5BThird_wave_of_autocratization%5D%5D) accusing an editor of dominating the discussion with repeated replies (the guideline against this is called WP:BLUDGEON), combining unpublished conclusions drawn from multiple sources (WP:SYNTH), personal attacks, and tendentious editing — a classic pattern of one contested topic spilling across three venues at once.

### Large community/technical debate
- [Brainstorming ways to resolve the gap with TWAFI on the Main Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Brainstorming_ways_to_resolve_the_gap_with_TWAFI_on_the_Main_Page) — an enormous, very active thread (349 comments, 68 participants, last comment 3 hours ago) about the visual layout fallout from a new "Today's Article for Improvement" feature being added to the Main Page. Mostly a design/technical argument rather than a policy fight, but notable for its sheer scale and immediacy.
