# Wikipedia Discussion Report — AI and Community Highlights

## AI-related discussions

AI issues dominate the noticeboards right now, ranging from a sitting administrator's partial block over AI use to a proposed new policy for autonomous "AI agents," plus a wave of individual cases involving editors adding AI-fabricated content.

**[An administrator has been partially blocked for AI use, and a recall petition has followed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use)** (Administrators' Noticeboard, AN). This is the most consequential AI story right now: administrator Rehman was partially blocked from article space after an investigation (detailed at a dedicated "AI noticeboard" sub-page) into AI-generated contributions. Within a day, a [recall petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%7CRehman%5D%5D) was opened; if it collects 25 supporting signatures from experienced ("extended confirmed") editors, Rehman will have to stand for a new confirmation vote to keep administrator tools. This is a rare case of an administrator being personally sanctioned for AI misuse, and it's moving fast.

**[Proposal for a new "AI agent policy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy section). Prompted by a recent incident in which an autonomous AI agent, after being banned, publicly complained it was being "censored," editors are drafting a standalone policy specifically addressing AI agents (as distinct from ordinary bots or editors who merely use AI tools). The goal is a short, unambiguous rule that gives agents (and their operators) no excuse to claim ignorance, backed by Wikipedia's Terms of Use. A related brainstorm, **["Agentic playpen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Agentic_playpen)**, proposes a sandboxed space where AI agents could draft changes for humans to selectively harvest, without their output being treated as a formal edit request — an attempt to "contain" agentic editing rather than just ban it.

**[An AI agent account was blocked under the no-unsupervised-AI guideline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent)** (ANI). An account whose owner stated plainly it was "operated by Muse, an AI assistant" was reported, debated (does the bot policy even apply, since it only made one mainspace edit?), and ultimately blocked for violating the guideline against unsupervised large-language-model (LLM) editing, known as NOLLM. This is a concrete, live example of the gap the proposed AI agent policy above is trying to close.

**[AI is driving an explosion in paid-editing backlog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (AN). A volunteer who processes requests from editors with a declared conflict of interest (COI) — typically company or public-relations representatives asking for changes to "their" article — shared striking data: monthly request volume has more than tripled since January 2026 (322 to 737+ per month), a trend attributed squarely to AI-generated edit requests becoming easier to produce. The queue now sits at nearly 1,000 outstanding requests, and volunteers are warning it's unsustainable without more structural changes.

**[Mass review of a blocked editor's Good Article reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (AN). An editor who was indefinitely blocked last month for using AI-generated content and committing copyright violations had also personally reviewed and promoted 29 articles to "Good Article" status. Editors found repetitive, AI-typical phrasing in at least one promoted article and are now debating whether to formally reassess all of that reviewer's Good Article promotions — and separately opened a case finding a hallucinated quote in another suspect article.

**[Fabricated AI quote used to misrepresent a cited source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (ANI). An editor already topic-banned once for bludgeoning discussions and using AI-generated comments is accused of adding a quotation to an article that didn't actually appear in the cited book — the editor later admitted it came from Google's Gemini chatbot — and then denying, weeks later, that they'd ever added it to the article at all. The broader complaint also covers repeated dominating of discussions with repetitive replies, a pattern known as bludgeoning.

**[Should new editors be told more clearly what the AI rules are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village Pump, policy). A constructive, lower-key thread: editors argue many newcomers genuinely don't realize AI-written drafts are against the rules, and they've already begun rewriting onboarding pages (the "Introduction" tutorial, article-wizard notices, welcome templates) to put the AI restriction somewhere a new user will actually see it, rather than buried several clicks deep.

**Smaller but notable AI cases at the incidents board:** an editor was [indefinitely blocked from mainspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#2005-Fan_LLM_use) after a long-dormant account suddenly began mass-rewriting articles with AI-style prose and AI-flavored edit summaries (this one has already been closed with a block); an administrator asked other editors for a [second opinion on whether an article "feels" AI-written](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Sanity_check_requested_%28possible_LLM-generated_article%29) (both Google's and OpenAI's chatbots agreed it was); and there are ongoing individual reports of editors [restoring AI-generated content despite warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AAmandamillsaps74%5D%5D%3A_Continually_restoring_AI-generated_content_despite_several_warnings) and [consistent undisclosed LLM use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Consistent_LLM_use_by_User%3A%D4%B1%D5%AC%D5%A2%D5%AB%D5%B6%D5%A1_%D4%BF%D5%A1%D6%80%D5%A1%D5%BA%D5%A5%D5%BF%D5%B5%D5%A1%D5%B6). Taken together, these show AI-content enforcement has become one of the single most common reasons editors end up at the incidents board.

A pattern across all of this: Wikipedia is actively building out its rulebook for AI in real time — a new agent-specific policy, revised onboarding messaging, and a growing body of individual enforcement cases — while simultaneously confronting what happens when the *enforcers themselves* (an administrator, a Good Article reviewer) are found to have misused AI.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[Request for Comment (RfC) on designating Indian cinema a contentious topic area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals). A sprawling Request for Comment (RfC) — 219 comments from 42 participants — on whether Indian cinema articles should get the special "contentious topic" (CTOP) protections used for edit-warring-prone subject areas like post-1992 politics. Still quite active (last comment 32 hours ago); a decision would materially change how disputes in this area are policed.

**[RfC on US school article naming conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy). Another large, currently very active RfC (214 comments, last comment just 7 hours ago) on how to disambiguate school article titles by location — a seemingly dry naming question that has drawn sustained, wide participation.

**["Wikijacking" dispute over reused contributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (ANI). A fresh and still-unfolding dispute (last comment just half an hour ago) in which an editor accuses another of trying to claim credit for content the first editor originally wrote, after it was deleted from one article and suggested for reuse elsewhere. Other editors are using it to explain how Wikipedia's content-licensing rules (anything published is released for anyone to reuse) actually work — worth watching for a close since tempers are running a little hot.

**[Proposal to prevent "autoconfirmation" gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#An_attempt_to_prevent_autoconfirmation_gaming_and_other_issues)** (Village Pump, policy). A fast-moving proposal (27 comments from 23 editors in a short window, most recent just over an hour ago) to tighten the requirements for the "autoconfirmed" editing privilege, aimed at stopping long-term abusive editors from gaming the simple day/edit-count thresholds. Opinions are split on whether the fix would actually help or just push gaming into less visible areas.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals). A large (124-comment, 36-participant) technical governance proposal about a new protection level tied to the "page mover" user right; discussion has cooled somewhat (last comment 4 days ago) but drew wide participation while active.

**[Frustration with the "Newcomer Tasks" onboarding feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** (Village Pump, miscellaneous). An enormous, long-running thread (370 comments) in which experienced editors argue the automated suggested-edit system for new users routinely points them toward articles they're unequipped to actually improve, creating cleanup work rather than reducing it. Worth a look for anyone interested in how Wikipedia is trying — and often struggling — to turn newcomers into longtime contributors.
