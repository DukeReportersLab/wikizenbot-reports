# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussion Roundup

## AI-related discussions

AI use and policy is by far the most active storyline across venues right now, spanning formal policy debate, admin accountability cases, and a wave of individual conduct disputes at the incident noticeboards.

**[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — This Request for Comment, with 153 comments from 47 editors, just closed with a "no change" result. The question was whether the guideline against using large language models to generate article content (known by its shortcut, "WP:NOLLM") should be explicitly amended to permit or prohibit AI use specifically for formatting wiki markup (the underlying code of an article, often called "Wikitext"). The closer found that most editors still distrust AI even for basic formatting tasks, so the ambiguous status quo stays in place. This is the second major AI-formatting RfC to fail this autumn, following a similar one on AI-generated citations — a sign the community keeps probing for carve-outs that keep getting rejected.

**[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — A smaller but substantive discussion (31 comments) asking whether the guideline on AI-assisted talk-page comments (shortcut "WP:LLMTALK") should be tightened to match the stricter no-AI-generation rule for articles. Participants are split on whether "AI-refined" comments (as opposed to AI-translated ones) should still be tolerated, with several editors noting the guideline's wording is outdated and looser than practice at the incident noticeboard already suggests.

**[Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — A newer, smaller thread (9 comments) that has already produced concrete action: one editor spent a day adding AI-policy notices to new-user-facing templates (the "Articles for Creation" pre-submission template, warning messages, the article creation wizard) and improving the wording in Wikipedia's main introductory help page, which gets over 100,000 views a month. Worth watching as a rare case of a policy gap being fixed in near-real time.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A detailed proposal (69 comments, 20 participants) for Wikipedia to build its own AI-powered natural-language search tool, pitched as a way to stem the loss of visits to chatbots while keeping users grounded in sourced, human-written Wikipedia text. Other editors immediately raised cost and reliability objections. Not urgent, but notable as the community's most developed vision yet for embracing AI as a tool rather than just policing its misuse.

**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — What started as a routine backlog appeal became a notable data-driven finding: one editor who tracks the conflict-of-interest edit request queue found that monthly submissions have roughly tripled in 2026 versus historical norms, and attributes it directly to AI — either making it easier for paid/conflicted editors to generate "surficially passable" requests, or simply more people now using AI to draft them. This is a concrete, measurable example of AI's systemic effect on Wikipedia's editorial workload.

**[Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A serious admin-accountability case: an administrator who is also an academic journal editor added around 150 self-citing references to his own papers across dozens of articles, including bursts of edits just 1–2 minutes apart, raising suspicion of automated or AI-assisted editing. Multiple editors are calling for him to voluntarily resign his administrator tools at the bureaucrats' noticeboard; this remains unresolved and is worth following for the outcome.

**[Advertising on Wikipedia's main page with LLM content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Advertising_on_Wikipedia%27s_main_page_with_LLM_content)** (Incidents noticeboard) — Already closed, but illustrative: a conflicted editor used an AI tool to help draft an article about their own video game, which then passed human review (including the "Did You Know" process) and appeared on Wikipedia's most-viewed page — until someone spotted a literal leftover AI instruction ("Verify that the current App Store description...") hidden in the article's markup. The case exposed a real gap: AI-detection tools and reviewers all missed it, and it's prompting follow-up discussion about tightening the "Did You Know" review process for conflicted contributors.

**[Smokinmans - sock / AI-use / undisclosed paid editing (UPE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Smokinmans_-_sock_/_AI-use_/_UPE.)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A messier, still-unresolved case where editors are debating whether a suspicious new account editing only Elon Musk-related companies is a sock puppet (someone evading a previous block with a new account) evading detection, an undisclosed paid editor, or simply unusually fluent with AI tools — illustrating how hard it's becoming to distinguish these categories from behavioral evidence alone.

**[Sanity check requested (possible LLM-generated article)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Sanity_check_requested_%28possible_LLM-generated_article%29)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A brand-new, small thread where an administrator asked for a second opinion before accusing someone of AI-generated writing; both AI chatbots and human reviewers agreed the article was AI-written. Minor on its own, but a good illustration of the normalized workflow Wikipedia has developed for flagging suspected AI content.

**A recurring pattern at the Incidents noticeboard:** three separate, very similar cases are running in parallel — [Itsekiriwriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Itsekiriwriting%3A_Continued_AI_use_and_disruptive_editing_after_being_warned_%28part_2%29) (now indefinitely blocked after repeatedly denying AI use that they had previously admitted to), [Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83) (a 13-year veteran editor pasting unsourced, sometimes AI-generated content into Indian election articles), and [Tran tuan 2511](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Tran_tuan_2511%3A_LLM_use_and_NOENGAGE) (using AI to translate/generate text with broken citations, while almost never responding on talk pages). Taken together these show a consistent enforcement pattern: AI-generated content paired with a refusal to communicate is now treated by administrators as a serious, blockable combination — even when editors are established and acting in apparent good faith.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (Incidents noticeboard) — The single most active thread this cycle (275 comments, 52 participants), a month-long editor conduct dispute in the aviation topic area that just escalated: it has been closed and referred to Wikipedia's Arbitration Committee for a full case, meaning the underlying behavioral and content disputes will now get a formal tribunal-style hearing rather than further noticeboard back-and-forth.

**[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — A sprawling naming-convention debate (208 comments, still getting comments within the hour) trying to settle years of inconsistent practice on whether US school articles should be disambiguated by city, state, or both. Low drama, but notable for its sheer scale and the fact that four previous attempts to resolve this have failed.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A technical permissions proposal with 116 comments and ongoing rapid activity (last comment 30 minutes before this report), concerning a new protection level restricted to editors with the "page mover" user right. Worth watching given the pace of discussion, though it's a narrow technical/process matter rather than a high-stakes dispute.

**[Closure Review Request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Closure_Review_Request)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A challenge to how a discussion was closed on the long-running, high-profile dispute over whether the historical figure Yasuke should be described as a samurai. This reopens one of Wikipedia's more infamous content controversies of the past two years; the closer is being accused of inadequately summarizing a complex, interleaved discussion.

**[Brainstorming ways to resolve the gap with "Today's Wikipedia Article for Improvement" on the Main Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Brainstorming_ways_to_resolve_the_gap_with_TWAFI_on_the_Main_Page)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — Extremely high engagement (327 comments, 68 participants, still active) but purely a layout/design discussion about fitting a newly-added Main Page module alongside existing ones — interesting for its volume, not for any underlying conflict.

**[Newcomer tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** (Village Pump, miscellaneous) — The largest thread scanned (364 comments, 251,000+ characters), a sustained critique of the automated system that suggests edits to new users, with multiple editors reporting that suggested tasks are poorly matched to newcomer skill levels and create more cleanup work than they save. Some of the discussion touches on whether the suggestion engine itself should be reformed with more human curation versus automation.
