# WikiZenBot Report — Current State of English Wikipedia Discussions

## Top-tier: High-stakes conduct disputes (ANI/AN)

**1. [Topic Ban from XfD: Randy Kryn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Topic_Ban_from_XfD:_Randy_Kryn)** — *ANI*
226 comments, 46 participants — by far the largest, most contested thread in this scan. A proposal to topic-ban a long-tenured editor from all XfD participation over allegedly policy-untethered "inclusionist" arguments has split the community: several experienced editors (Levivich, fiveby) initially opposed but Levivich has since flipped to support "based on new evidence." This is exactly the kind of split, high-participant discussion likely heading toward a contentious close — worth watching for how it resolves.

**2. [Deacon Vorbis on requested redirects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Deacon_Vorbis_on_requested_redirects)** — *ANI*
62 comments, 20 participants, **last comment just over an hour ago** — actively developing. What started as a single IP's complaint (dismissed as "drama-mongering") escalated once other editors backed it up with a pattern of Deacon Vorbis declining/nominating plausible redirects for deletion at RfD, with Deacon pushing back that the "cadre of RFD regulars" is the real problem. Fast-moving and unresolved.

**3. [Reywas92 - Repeated civility failings, aspersions...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Reywas92_-_Repeated_civility_failings,_aspersions,_and_refusal_to_take_responsibility_for_their_edits)** and **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** — *ANI*
98 and 50 comments respectively. Two separate but thematically linked disputes over sourcing standards on aviation/airport articles (flight trackers, booking engines, unsourced destination charts). Together they suggest **WikiProject Aviation is a current hotspot** for sourcing battles and incivility — worth a look as a cluster rather than two isolated incidents.

**4. [Challenging a Close... Tang Dynasty article RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard#Challenging_a_Close_and_Other_Issue_with_a_Tang_Dynasty_article_RFC)** — *AN* (already resolved, but instructive)
97 comments. This close-review thread uncovered a suspicious brand-new account posting LLM-generated text to back one side of a map dispute — and the whole affair snowballed into a **community ban of the involved editor ("Guile's Theme")**, cross-referenced with a separate 70-comment ANI thread on the same user's "AI use and behavior issues." Now archived/closed, but a good example of how content disputes plus suspected AI-assisted sockpuppetry escalate quickly to sanctions.

## Policy discussions worth tracking

**5. [RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Village_pump_(policy)#RfC:_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** — *VP Policy*
Brand new (last comment **<1 hour ago**), 33 comments already. This directly follows the just-closed 145-comment "RfC: AI use for generating citations" — the community is clearly still working through unresolved edges of WP:NOLLM, and several editors are pushing back on "RfC fatigue" from repeatedly re-litigating AI policy. Expect this to grow fast.

**6. [RfC: Name of the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Village_pump_(proposals)#RfC:_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button)** — *VP Proposals*
91 comments, 41 participants — unusually broad participation for what sounds like a small UI naming question, suggesting deeper disagreement about interface philosophy/newcomer-friendliness.

**7. [Newcomer tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Village_pump_(miscellaneous)#Newcomer_tasks)** — *VP Misc*
269 comments — the single largest thread scanned anywhere. Slower now (67.5h since last comment) but massive in scope, reflecting sustained community concern about WMF-driven newcomer-task features.

**8. [Topic ban (Rathfelder)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard#Topic_ban_(Rathfelder))** — *AN*
An unban/topic-ban-lift appeal from an editor with a serious history (sockpuppetry, COI, attack-page concerns from ~2019) is being reopened; editors are asking for a more thorough, properly-documented appeal rather than a quick lift. Worth monitoring given the gravity of the original offenses.

## Notable but lower urgency
- **AfD logs**: nothing shows the "many comments in a few hours" urgency pattern this cycle — most AfDs have modest (2–18) comment counts spread over many hours, i.e. routine deletion discussion traffic, not a breakout controversy.
- Numerous smaller ANI reports (LLM-tag removal disputes, sockpuppetry, vandalism) reflect a **recurring theme this cycle: AI/LLM-generated content and its detection/tagging** is generating friction across multiple boards (The-Pope removing LLMPRODs, Guile's Theme AI use, the two AI-wikitext RfCs, mass LLMPROD emailing at AN).

## Pattern callouts
- **AI policy is the dominant thread of the moment**, spanning VP Policy RfCs, ANI conduct cases, and AN — not one discussion but a whole cluster of related, unresolved questions about acceptable AI use.
- **Aviation/airport articles** are a live conduct-dispute hotspot (sourcing standards, ownership-like reverting).
- The Randy Kryn and Deacon Vorbis threads represent the "real-time drama" category — active back-and-forth with consequences (topic bans) imminent — while the AI-policy RfCs and Newcomer Tasks thread represent the "slow-burn, wide-consensus-building" category. Both matter, but for different reasons: the former needs eyes now before a close; the latter rewards patient reading of accumulated argument.
