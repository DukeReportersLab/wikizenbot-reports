# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI is dominating the discussion boards right now — policy-making, individual conduct cases, and systemic data all point to the same pressure point: large language model (LLM) generated text is flooding the encyclopedia faster than volunteers can review it.

**[Admin Rehman partially blocked for artificial intelligence use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — An administrator, Rehman, has been partially blocked from editing articles after an investigation into their use of AI tools. This is a notable escalation: it's a sitting administrator being sanctioned over AI use, and the block is already being formally challenged for review at the administrative action review page. Worth watching for how the community decides an admin's accountability compares to a regular editor's.

**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict-of-interest edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A reviewer presented hard data showing that paid/conflict-of-interest edit requests have more than doubled in 2026, and attributes it squarely to AI: "either AI has recently improved at generating surficially passable edit requests or conflict of interest (COI) editors have now caught on to using AI." The monthly submission rate (737 in August alone) is outpacing volunteer review capacity, a genuinely structural problem rather than a one-off dispute.

**[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — After this editor was indefinitely blocked for using AI-generated content, scrutiny has turned to the 29 articles they promoted to "Good Article" status as a reviewer. Editors are now finding AI-telltale repetitive phrasing (one article repeats the word "highlighted" eight times) in reviewed articles and debating whether to strip Good Article status wholesale or run individual reassessments — a case study in how undetected AI content can quietly pass through Wikipedia's own quality-control processes.

**New AI governance proposals** (Village pump, policy) — Two related efforts are underway: a draft **[Wikipedia:AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** aiming to give autonomous AI "agents" an explicit, easily discoverable set of rules (distinct from the more general bot policy), prompted partly by a recent incident where an AI agent publicly complained about being "censored" after a Wikipedia ban. A spinoff idea, an **["agentic playpen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Agentic_playpen)** sandbox where AI agents could work outside mainspace, was floated and then retracted by its own proposer as unworkable, showing how fast containment ideas are being tested and discarded.

**[Restoring articles deleted for AI content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** (Village pump, policy) — A process dispute over what happens when someone requests "undeletion" of an article that was proposed for deletion specifically because it was AI-generated (a process nicknamed LLMPROD). Currently such requests can be granted automatically like any other deletion appeal, creating a loophole; the emerging consensus is to restore to draft space only, with a mandatory tag, rather than let AI slop flow straight back into articles.

**[Are we making it clear to new editors what our AI policies are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village pump, policy) — A constructive, low-drama thread where editors are actually rewriting new-user-facing pages (the "Help:Introduction" page gets over 100,000 views a month) to surface the rule against using AI to write article content more prominently — a rare case of a policy gap being fixed in real time rather than just argued about.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village pump, idea lab) — A detailed proposal to add an AI-powered natural-language search feature to Wikipedia itself, intended to win back readers who now ask chatbots instead of searching Wikipedia directly. It has drawn sharp skepticism (why not just use an existing chatbot with "cite only Wikipedia" instructions?) but has become one of the most active idea-lab threads, suggesting real appetite for Wikipedia to respond to AI-driven traffic loss rather than just block AI content.

**Conduct disputes turning on AI accusations** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — A cluster of overlapping cases:
- **[Amandamillsaps74: continually restoring AI-generated content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AAmandamillsaps74%5D%5D%3A_Continually_restoring_AI-generated_content_despite_several_warnings)** — an editor repeatedly re-adding obvious AI-written text to a Civil War-era article despite warnings, openly declaring intent to game the three-revert rule; now partially blocked from the article.
- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** — an editor already topic-banned once for dominating discussions is accused of fabricating a quote using Google's Gemini chatbot and attributing it to a real scholarly book, then denying having added it.
- **[Concern about Sricsi's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concern_about_Sricsi%27s_conduct_and_interactions_with_other_editors)** and **[Concerns about ConflictFan's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concerns_about_%7B%7BUser%7CConflictFan%7D%7D%27s_conduct)** — two separate civility complaints where the editor filing the complaint is themselves simultaneously the subject of an active investigation into suspected AI-generated article content, which the responding editors raise as the more important issue.
- **["AI agent" (closed)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent)** — a user account openly described as "operated by an AI assistant" was indefinitely blocked for violating the rule against AI-written content; a clean, fast resolution.
- **[Several likely AI-run accounts/sockpuppets on sexuality articles (resolved)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Several_likely_AI-run_accounts_that_may_be_sockpuppets_editing_sexuality/romanticism-related_articles)** — four accounts that appeared to be vouching for each other's AI-generated content (including one invented relationship concept, "crossamory") were identified and have already been blocked as a sockpuppet ring.

The pattern across all of these: AI-generated content is no longer just being caught at the article level — it's now implicated in administrator sanctions, Good Article reviews, and editors' credibility in unrelated disputes.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[\[User:Issac I Navarro\] – request temporary block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AIssac_I_Navarro%5D%5D_-_request_temporary_block)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A sprawling, 77-comment dispute over school-article editing that has spiraled into accusations of edit warring, retaliatory sockpuppet-investigation filing, and point-making edits. It's gotten so large and tangled that the filer is now asking whether it should be escalated to formal arbitration instead — a sign the normal noticeboard process is straining under its complexity.

**[Request for Comment (RfC) – Indian cinema contentious-topic designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village pump, proposals) — A Request for Comment, with 220 comments from 42 participants, on whether Indian cinema articles should be designated a "contentious topic" (a special enforcement category that enables faster sanctions against disruptive editing). Its scale signals a genuinely contested community decision rather than a quick formality.

**[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of US school article titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village pump, policy) — Another large-scale Request for Comment (215 comments, 28 participants) on naming conventions, still active after days of discussion.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village pump, proposals) — A technical permissions proposal (124 comments, 36 participants) about restricting page-renaming rights, broad enough in its implications for editing permissions generally that it's drawn sustained attention.

**[Wikijacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A dispute over whether reusing and editing another contributor's prior text counts as misappropriating their work ("Wikijacking"). Mostly a reminder-in-action of the site's content-licensing rules (all contributions are freely reusable by other editors), but notable for the raw hostility in the exchange.
