# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

### Policy: where do large language models fit?

The biggest AI policy fight of the moment just concluded. The **[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) ran for over a month with 153 comments from 47 editors and has just been closed as **"no change"** to the guideline against using large-language-model output on Wikipedia (shortcut: WP:NOLLM). Editors had proposed either explicitly permitting AI to format wiki markup (with the human held responsible for errors) or explicitly banning it outright; the closer found that most editors still don't trust AI even for basic formatting tasks, so the deliberately loose, restrictive status quo stands. This is a meaningful data point for how the community is handling AI — repeated attempts to carve out "safe" AI uses keep failing to gain consensus.

Running in parallel, **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) is a less decisive but substantive debate over the separate guideline on AI use in *talk-page comments* (as opposed to articles). Editors disagree about how much "AI-refined" wording should be tolerated versus treated as equivalent to the outright ban on AI-generated article text; one editor flagged that this guideline predates and hasn't been reconciled with the newer, stricter article-content guideline. A companion thread, **[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/LLM are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)**, is a good-faith attempt to map out a grey zone — using AI to locate sources in a huge scanned document, for instance — that current policy doesn't clearly address. A smaller related thread, **[Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)**, worries that newcomers simply don't know the rules exist before they're sanctioned for breaking them — a theme that recurs throughout the enforcement cases below.

On the more speculative side, **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (idea lab) proposes a Wikimedia Foundation–run AI search assistant built into the search box, intended to pull readers back from commercial chatbots by answering natural-language queries using only verified Wikipedia content. It's drawing skepticism (why not just use an existing chatbot pointed at Wikipedia?) but is a sign that some editors see "fight AI with our own AI" as a viable strategy.

### Enforcement: AI use keeps landing editors in front of administrators

A striking pattern across the incident noticeboards this week is editors being caught using AI and then denying it, sometimes unconvincingly:

- **[Advertising on Wikipedia's main page with LLM content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Advertising_on_Wikipedia%27s_main_page_with_LLM_content)** (incident noticeboard, now closed) is the most vivid case study: a conflict-of-interest editor used an LLM to draft an article about their own video game, which then passed "Did You Know" review and briefly appeared on Wikipedia's main page — hidden LLM instructions to the article's creator (literally left in the invisible wikitext) were only spotted afterward. Both an AI-detection tool and a human reviewer missed it. The thread exposes real gaps in Wikipedia's review pipeline for catching AI-generated promotional content.
- **[Itsekiriwriting: Continued AI use and disruptive editing after being warned (part 2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Itsekiriwriting%3A_Continued_AI_use_and_disruptive_editing_after_being_warned_%28part_2%29)** ended in an indefinite block after the editor gave shifting explanations for AI use (first "refining wording," then "advice on whether edits are appropriate") while continuing to edit-war to restore AI-flavored text.
- **[Smokinmans - sock / AI-use / undisclosed paid editing (UPE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Smokinmans_-_sock_/_AI-use_/_UPE.)** is a still-open, almost comic case where experienced editors debate forensic "AI tells" (rounded CSS boxes, emoji banners, overly fluent wikitext) to decide whether a new single-purpose account editing only Elon Musk-related companies is an AI user, a sockpuppet, or both — alongside a likely undisclosed paid editing problem.
- **[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)** (closed, "socks washed") involved a 13-year veteran editor pasting raw, unedited LLM chatbot output — including the chatbot's own commentary meant for the editor — directly into election articles.
- **[Tran tuan 2511: LLM use and NOENGAGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Tran_tuan_2511%3A_LLM_use_and_NOENGAGE)** raises a related problem: an editor apparently using AI-generated replies to avoid genuinely engaging with other editors' concerns.

Higher up the trust ladder, **[Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (Administrators' noticeboard) concerns a sitting administrator — also a university professor — who added citations to his own academic journals to roughly 150 articles in a short, suspiciously bot-like burst, raising both conflict-of-interest and possible-automation concerns; the community consensus so far is that he should resign his administrator tools.

Finally, **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) is less a dispute than a data point worth knowing: a volunteer who reviews conflict-of-interest edit requests found that monthly submissions have more than doubled in 2026 compared with 2025, and attributes the surge largely to paid editors now using AI to generate requests that look more polished and take reviewers much longer to vet — a backlog with no sign of slowing down.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (incident noticeboard, closed): a month-long, 275-comment conduct dispute in the aviation topic area has escalated all the way to a full arbitration case, Wikipedia's most serious dispute-resolution process. It's a good example of how a single revert dispute can balloon into a full topic-area crisis when sourcing standards (unreliable flight-tracking sites and plane-spotting photo sites) are contested repeatedly across many articles.
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy): a sprawling 209-comment debate over how to title school articles consistently, still drawing comments within the last day.
- **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals): a 121-comment, still-very-active proposal to create a new protection level restricting page moves, relevant to long-running abuse of page-renaming.
- **[RfC: Indian cinema as a contentious topic area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals): 109 comments and still active within the last five hours, proposing special editing restrictions for Indian film articles due to recurring disruption.
- **[Closure Review Request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Closure_Review_Request)** (Administrators' noticeboard, closed "endorsed"): a challenge to the closure of a contentious discussion about whether the historical figure Yasuke should be described as a samurai; the challenge was closed as a snow-clear endorsement, and the challenger is being warned under the Arbitration Committee's enforcement powers for the contentious-topic area covering that dispute.
- **[Newcomer tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** (Village Pump, miscellaneous): by far the largest thread scanned (369 comments, over 250,000 characters), a sustained complaint that the automated system suggesting starter edits to new editors is recommending confusing, poorly-matched, or nonsensical tasks, creating cleanup work rather than reducing it — worth watching since the suggestion algorithm's failures echo broader worries about automated/AI-driven tooling making decisions without adequate human oversight.
