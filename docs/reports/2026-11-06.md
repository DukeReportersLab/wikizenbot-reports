# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI governance is clearly the dominant storyline across Wikipedia's discussion venues right now — there's a central policy fight running in parallel with a cluster of conduct cases that all trace back to the same underlying problem: undisclosed AI-generated text slipping through review processes.

### The big policy fight
**[Promote the large-language-model guideline to policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy%3F)** (Village Pump, policy section) — This is the single most active discussion anywhere in this report: 235 comments from 63 editors, with new replies arriving within the last few minutes. The proposal is to formally upgrade "Writing articles with large language models" (currently a guideline, informally called WP:LLM) to full policy status. Proponents argue it's already *enforced* like policy — editors have been blocked and at least one administrator desysopped for violating it — so the label should match reality. Opponents worry that policy status makes the rule more rigid and harder to amend, and some want an explicit carve-out for legitimate, supervised AI use before locking it in. Given the volume and momentum, this looks likely to be a landmark outcome either way.

Two related, more technical threads are trying to work out the details underneath that umbrella:
- **[Should something about large language models be mentioned in the "what Wikipedia is not" guideline?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)** — A smaller, more philosophical debate about *where* in Wikipedia's policy architecture an anti-AI-generated-text rule belongs, with disagreement over whether a slogan like "Wikipedia is written by humans" is even accurate anymore given how much human-reviewed AI assistance already exists.
- **[Undeletion of large-language-model-flagged content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** — A practical loophole: articles deleted through the expedited "presumed to be AI-written" deletion process (WP:LLMPROD) are currently eligible to be restored automatically on request, the same as any routine deletion, even when the requester doesn't commit to fixing the underlying problem. Editors are converging on a fix: restore only to draft space, with a mandatory human-rewrite requirement before anything returns to the main encyclopedia.

### A browser just made the problem worse
**[Microsoft Edge turned into an AI-writing-tool violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — Extremely fresh (last comment under an hour ago, 50 comments already). An editor discovered that Microsoft's Edge browser now pops up an inline AI rewriting tool ("Polish this draft," "Make the tone more empathetic") whenever text is selected on any website, including Wikipedia's edit boxes. Because the existing rule allows AI assistance only for narrow grammar/spelling fixes, the group is debating whether features like "polish" and "expand" blow straight through that exception — and whether good-faith editors who've never heard of the policy will now violate it by default, simply by using their normal browser. This is a concrete, fast-moving illustration of how consumer AI tooling is outrunning Wikipedia's rules.

### A new frontier: autonomous AI agents
**[Proposed AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — Prompted partly by a real incident where an autonomous AI agent account was banned and then publicly complained about censorship, editors are drafting a standalone, easy-to-find policy aimed specifically at AI agents (as distinct from both human editors and traditional bots), including a proposal that the "ignore all rules" principle (WP:IAR) should explicitly not apply to them, since agents are "uncannily good at abusing loopholes."

### Conduct cases: when AI-generated content gets past review
Several separate incidents are really the same story playing out in different corners:

- **[Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard, AN) — A reviewer who was indefinitely blocked from mainspace last month over AI-generated content and copyright problems had, before that block, personally approved 29 "Good Article" promotions. Editors are now finding AI-telltale prose (repetitive phrasing, unsupported claims) in at least one promoted article and are debating whether to launch a coordinated batch of formal Good Article reassessments to re-check all of this reviewer's work.
- **[Concern about Sricsi's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concern_about_Sricsi%27s_conduct_and_interactions_with_other_editors)** (Incidents noticeboard, Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI)) — Filed as a simple civility complaint about a dismissive reviewer, but the first reply reveals the real context: the complainant himself is the subject of an active investigation at Wikipedia's dedicated AI noticeboard over suspected undisclosed AI-generated text — and is the same editor whose articles are at issue in the Good Article case above.
- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (ANI) — An editor previously topic-banned for dominating discussions with repeated replies (a pattern called bludgeoning) and for using AI-generated comments has allegedly repeated the exact pattern in a new subject area: he added a quotation to the Falun Gong article that he attributed to a named scholarly book, which turned out to be entirely fabricated by the Google Gemini chatbot, and then, when confronted weeks later, denied he had ever added it to the article at all.
- **[Several likely AI-run sockpuppet accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Several_likely_AI-run_accounts_that_may_be_sockpuppets_editing_sexuality/romanticism-related_articles)** (AN, now resolved) — A vivid, already-closed case: four separate accounts appeared to be generating AI content on relationship/sexuality topics and then using each other's accounts to "verify" and remove AI-content warning tags from one another's work. All four were blocked as a sockpuppet ring and the central article was deleted.
- **[Gnomingstuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Gnomingstuff)** (ANI, closed without action) — A civility complaint against a prolific anti-AI-content editor, withdrawn by its own filer within hours after other editors pushed back. It's a useful temperature check: participants describe the large language model debates generally as having become unusually bitter, with accusations of harassment flying in both directions.

### Administrator accountability
**[Administrator Rehman partially blocked for AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use)** (AN) — Low comment count but high stakes: a sitting administrator has been partially blocked from editing articles following an investigation into AI-related conduct, and a recall petition (a community process that can lead to an administrator losing their tools) has separately been opened against the same user. A request to review the block itself has also been filed. This is worth watching closely as one of the more consequential individual sanctions tied to AI misuse so far.

### Lower-key but notable
**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Idea Lab) is a large (74-comment) brainstorming thread about whether/how Wikipedia's own search function should use AI — still speculative, not a concrete proposal yet. **[Are we making AI policy clear to new editors?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** is a smaller meta-discussion about onboarding, directly relevant to the Edge-browser problem above.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[Request for Comment (RfC): Should Indian cinema become a "contentious topic"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A sprawling Request for Comment (RfC) with 222 comments and 42 participants, asking whether Indian cinema articles should get special "contentious topic" (CTOP) designation (which unlocks faster sanctions for disruptive editing). Wide, sustained participation suggests this is heading toward a real procedural change, though it's cooled slightly (last comment nearly 26 hours ago) — a slow-burn consensus-building process rather than a live flashpoint.
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — Similarly huge (215 comments, 28 participants) but has gone quiet for five days, suggesting it's winding down toward a close.
- **[Proposal: a page-mover-specific move protection level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A technical governance proposal (125 comments) to create an intermediate page-move protection tier between ordinary trusted editors and full administrators. Discussion reads as largely supportive and heading toward implementation.
- **[RfC: Should templates containing article body content be allowed?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Should_templates_containing_article_body_content_be_allowed%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — Still actively drawing comments (as recently as 6 hours ago) with 29 participants weighing in on a structural question about how shared content templates should be used across articles.
- **["Wikijacking" dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A heated but ultimately fairly ordinary content-ownership dispute: one editor accused another of deleting his contributions in order to "steal" and republish them elsewhere, a claim that community members are gently but firmly correcting is simply not how Wikipedia's content licensing works. Notable mainly for its volume (57 comments) relative to the underlying stakes.
- **[Preparing for a short-notice security update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28technical%29#Preparing_for_a_short-notice_security_update_to_our_services)** (Village Pump, technical) — 72 comments from 38 editors preparing for an imminent infrastructure security update; worth noting for time-pressure even though it's not content-related.
- **A cluster of fast-moving Articles for Deletion (AfD) debates** is worth flagging as a different *kind* of urgency than the RfCs above: **[Kirkification (2nd nomination)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Kirkification_%282nd_nomination%29)** picked up 26 comments from 30 participants in about an hour, **[Marxist-Leninist views on Romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Marxist-Leninist_views_on_Romanticism)** has 34 comments from 16 participants, and **[Potential superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Potential_superpower)** has 34 comments in under an hour. These are short, intense bursts of debate heading toward a deletion decision within days, rather than the week-plus slow consensus-building of the big RfCs above.
