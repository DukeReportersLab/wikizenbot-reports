# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI is clearly the dominant undercurrent on Wikipedia right now — it shows up in policy debates, deletion disputes, user conduct cases, and even backlog statistics. Several threads below are new or newly active; where a discussion looks headed toward an actual guideline change or sanction, I've flagged that.

### Policy fights over what counts as acceptable AI use

- **[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — The biggest AI discussion running right now: 147 comments, 43 participants, still getting replies within the last four days. It asks whether the guideline against using large language models (LLMs) on Wikipedia, known by the shortcut "NOLLM," should be explicitly changed to either permit or forbid using AI to generate or format wiki markup (the underlying code of a page). Early comments already show a split between editors who want a blanket ban and those who think a narrow, supervised exception is fine — a preview of a fight that will likely shape how aggressively AI-generated formatting gets removed going forward.
- **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — Freshest activity of the bunch (last comment 16 hours ago). LLMTALK is the guideline section about using AI to help write talk-page comments. Editors are debating whether it quietly permits "AI-refined" comments in a way that conflicts with the stricter no-AI-content rule for articles, and whether that loophole needs closing.
- **[Onus for maintenance templates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Onus_for_maintenance_templates)** (Village Pump, policy) — Nominally about maintenance tags in general, but the entire dispute was triggered by an "AI-generated" warning tag placed on the *Deep State* article, where the tagging editor refused to say which passages were the problem. It's really a proxy fight over how much burden editors doing AI clean-up should bear to justify their tags — relevant to how aggressively AI-cleanup work gets done project-wide.
- **[Permit usage of LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions)** (Village Pump, policy) — Already closed ("this is going nowhere") after a wave of near-unanimous rejection, several replies quite heated. Worth noting mainly as a sign of how little patience the community currently has for proposals that loosen AI restrictions even slightly.

### Enforcement: a steady drumbeat of AI-misuse cases

A pattern across the incident noticeboards: editors are increasingly caught submitting AI-generated text and then denying it, and admins are responding fast.

- **[Up to 400 recent AfDs may contain AI-generated !votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Up_to_400_recent_AfDs_may_contain_AI-generated_%21votes)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, or ANI) — The most urgent item in this report: 66 comments, 35 participants, still active within the last 8 hours. An editor named Kukuryza is accused of posting AI-generated or AI-assisted "votes" across roughly 400 Articles for Deletion (AfD) discussions at 3–6 minute intervals for 8 hours straight, including suspiciously confident evaluations of sources in languages the editor doesn't speak. If confirmed, this could mean mass-invalidating votes across hundreds of deletion debates — a real, large-scale integrity problem, not just a slow-burning policy question.
- **[Dtf1978 AI and lying about it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Dtf1978_AI_and_lying_about_it)** (ANI) — Already resolved: the editor was indefinitely blocked for posting obviously AI-generated talk-page messages and then denying AI use, and also lost "extended confirmed" editing rights (a permission level gained through edit count, normally required to edit certain protected pages) for gaming that status with trivial edits.
- **[Purvanchal bhojpuriya, Jha2 Chhiyanchali: edit warring, likely AI usage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Purvanchal_bhojpuriya%2C_Jha2_Chhiyanchali%3A_edit_warring%2C_likely_AI_usage)** (ANI) — Two editors edit-warring across several India/Nepal-related articles, with AI-generated talk-page replies cited as evidence of bad-faith "sealioning" (repeatedly demanding sources/justification in bad faith).
- **[Promotional temporary accounts refusing to communicate without AI help](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Promotional_TAs_refusing_to_communicate_without_LLMs_in_ctop_page)** and the related **[request for administrator review of conduct in the NY-10 congressional election discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Request_for_Administrator_Review%3A_Editor_Conduct_in_NY-10_Congressional_Election_Discussion)** (both ANI) — A set of logged-out "temporary accounts" (Wikipedia's newer alternative to showing raw IP addresses) tied to a real-world congressional candidate repeatedly posted AI-generated edit requests and an AI-generated complaint about other editors. The accounts and a shared IP address have since been blocked.

### AI and sensitive content

- **[RfC on the debated file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AGenerative_AI_pornography#RfC_on_the_debated_file)** (Talk:Generative AI pornography) — A large, five-week-old Request for Comment (121 comments, 16 participants) over whether an AI-generated image should illustrate the "Generative AI pornography" article, given its resemblance to a depiction of a sexualized child-like character. It directly intersects with a separate, ongoing community and Wikimedia Foundation legal discussion about "open" AI image models lacking safety guardrails — a genuinely difficult case about where free-content principles collide with content-safety concerns.

### AI as a tool, not just a problem

- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A detailed proposal (61 comments) to let readers query Wikipedia using a natural-language AI search tool that cites back to original articles, as a response to Wikipedia losing traffic to AI chatbots. Early responses are skeptical about cost and quality but the conversation is sustained and thoughtful — worth watching as a sign of how the community might eventually reconcile "no AI content" with "readers now expect AI interfaces."
- **[Bot proposal: automatic addition of infobox for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A bot proposal that would use an LLM (Claude Opus) to flag uncertain cases while mechanically adding standard infoboxes to ~9,000-12,000 company articles. Several editors are already uneasy about using an LLM inside automated editing at all, even for a narrow, low-risk task — a useful illustration of how reflexively suspicious the community now is of anything AI-adjacent.
- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict-of-interest edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Not framed as an AI story at first, but a volunteer's data breakdown shows conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests have more than doubled in 2026 compared to recent years, which the poster attributes largely to requesters now using AI to generate "surficially passable" requests, overwhelming the volunteers who review them.
- **[Should admins be running the AI noticeboard?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Should_admins_be_running_AI_Noticeboard%3F)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — Older and quieter now (last comment 15 days ago), but useful background: a proposal to put administrators in charge of the AI noticeboard (AINB), the venue that handles AI-content cleanup and conduct reports, was largely rejected — the consensus view is AINB needs more volunteer labor, not more administrator authority.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — The single largest thread scanned today: 320 comments, 65 participants, still active within 6 hours. A long-time editor ("Roxy the dog") is under scrutiny for incivility and possible non-neutral editing on textile-related articles, with a self-issued apology followed immediately by more of the same conduct elsewhere — this looks headed toward a sanction.
- **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (ANI) — 222 comments and 43 participants in a recurring dispute over unsourced flight-schedule content ("flightcruft") in airport articles, with accusations of forum-shopping on both sides. Part of a chronic pattern of conduct problems in the aviation topic area.
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — 160 comments and climbing, very active (last comment under 10 hours ago). A genuinely wide-reaching naming-convention question — whether US school article titles should include city and/or state — that could affect thousands of articles and has already failed four times before.
- **[RfC: Name of the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RfC%3A_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button)** (Village Pump, proposals) — 124 comments, 48 participants: a surprisingly contentious interface-wording question about what to call the tab that leads to a page's talk page.
- **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — 78 comments, 30 participants, proposing a new protection level restricted to editors with the "page mover" permission, to curb disruptive page-renaming.
- **[RfC: Mugshot as infobox photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AClavicular_%28influencer%29#RfC%3A_Mugshot_as_Infobox_Photo)** (Talk:Clavicular (influencer)) — 76 comments, 28 participants, still very active (9 hours since last comment); a biography-of-living-person dispute over whether to use a police booking photo as the lead image, a recurring values clash between documentation and fairness-to-subject norms.
