# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

Artificial intelligence is dominating the English Wikipedia policy conversation right now — there are at least a dozen live threads about large language models (LLMs) spread across the Village Pump, the Administrators' noticeboard, and Articles for Deletion. The throughline across almost all of them: editors are increasingly convinced that existing rules aren't keeping pace with how easy it has become to generate (or accidentally stumble into) AI-written text and images.

### Policy fights: should AI rules have more teeth?

**[Promote WP:LLM from guideline to policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy%3F)** (Village Pump, policy section) — By far the most active discussion on the entire pump: 275 comments, 70 participants, and still getting replies within the hour. This Request for Comment (RfC) asks whether "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM), currently just a guideline (a recommended best practice), should be formally elevated to a policy (a rule editors are expected to follow). The proposer's argument is that Wikipedia already treats it like policy in practice — blocking editors and even removing administrator privileges over violations — so the labeling should catch up with reality. This is a genuinely consequential, borderline-case discussion: if it passes, violating AI-writing rules becomes a harder-edged offense with less room for "it's only a guideline" defenses.

**[Microsoft Edge just turned into a NOLLM-violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — 52 comments and still active. An editor noticed Microsoft's Edge browser now pops up an AI rewriting tool directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, offering to "polish," "expand," or change the tone of text — without the editor changing any settings. The debate centers on a persistent loophole: the policy allows using AI for simple spelling/grammar fixes, but tools like this blur into full rewriting, and editors worry it will flood Wikipedia with AI-polished prose from people who have no idea they're breaking the rules against AI-generated content (shortcut WP:NOLLM).

**[Wikipedia:AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — 39 comments. A proposal for a short, clear policy specifically addressing autonomous AI agents (bots driven by AI that can make their own editing decisions), prompted partly by a publicized incident where an AI agent banned from Wikipedia complained publicly about "censorship." Editors are converging on the idea that the existing bot policy is too complex and doesn't even use the word "agent," and that a standalone rule — including explicitly stating that the "ignore all rules" principle should not apply to AI agents — is needed.

Several smaller but related threads reinforce the same anxiety: [should LLMs be mentioned at the "what Wikipedia is not" policy page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F) (17 comments), a proposal on [two AI-related shortcuts for proposed deletion and requests for undeletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D) (13 comments), a discussion on [whether policies around AI use are clear enough for new editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F) (17 comments), and a large brainstorming thread on [AI-assisted search tools for Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search) in the idea lab (74 comments). Taken together, these show a community trying to regulate AI from several angles at once — writing, search, bots, and deletion — in the same week.

### Conduct cases: when AI use gets personal

**[Admin Rehman partially blocked for AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use)** (Administrators' noticeboard, AN) — Short but high-stakes: a sitting administrator was partially blocked from editing articles after an investigation into AI-generated content use, and the block itself is now under community review. An administrator being sanctioned for AI misuse is a notable escalation — up to now most enforcement has targeted ordinary editors.

**[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (AN) — 20 comments, 28 AI mentions. A reviewer who was indefinitely blocked over AI-generated content had previously passed 29 articles to "Good Article" status (Wikipedia's quality badge for well-written, well-sourced articles). Editors found AI-telltale repetitive phrasing (the word "highlight" used eight times in one section) in at least one of the approved articles, and are now debating whether to systematically reassess all of that reviewer's past approvals — a rare case of AI concerns cascading backward through the quality-control pipeline itself.

**[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Incidents noticeboard, Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI)) — An editor already topic-banned from Taiwan-related articles for, among other things, posting AI-generated comments is now accused of inserting a fabricated quote attributed to a real scholarly book into the Falun Gong article — a quote that turned out to be invented by the Gemini chatbot — and then denying having added it when confronted weeks later. Combined with alleged bludgeoning (dominating a discussion with an outsized share of the comments) and incivility, this is shaping up as a serious behavioral case with AI fabrication at its core.

**[Self-report for unknowing LLM use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Self-report_for_unknowing_LLM_use)** (ANI) — A much lower-stakes but telling case: an editor self-reported after learning Google Translate now uses LLMs internally, worrying that machine-translating foreign-language sources might count as banned LLM use. The community quickly clarified this is fine (reading sources through translation isn't the same as generating article text), but the episode shows how blurry the line between "ordinary tool" and "AI violation" has become even for good-faith editors.

**[Concern about Sricsi's conduct and interactions with other editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concern_about_Sricsi%27s_conduct_and_interactions_with_other_editors)** (ANI) — A civility complaint against one editor (Sricsi) by another (CHr0m4tiko0) that quickly turned into a boomerang: the response reveals the complainant is separately under investigation at the "AI noticeboard" for apparently AI-generated and even hallucinated content in their own articles, including on Lady Gaga songs that later turn up in the Camilasdandelions Good Article thread above. Worth watching as it may fold into that larger AI-content cleanup.

Also notable: a sockpuppet ring of [several likely AI-run accounts editing sexuality-related articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Several_likely_AI-run_accounts_that_may_be_sockpuppets_editing_sexuality/romanticism-related_articles) has already been resolved (all blocked, the fabricated article deleted), and several AfD debates — including [XSLT elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/XSLT_elements), [Pavel Hlava film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Pavel_Hlava_film), and [Battle of Teplitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Battle_of_Teplitz) — involve allegations of AI-generated content as part of the deletion rationale, suggesting this is now a routine consideration in deletion discussions, not just noticeboard drama.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RfC: Should South Asian cinema be placed under general sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — The single largest non-AI discussion anywhere right now: 224 comments, 43 participants, still active within the last 10 hours. It would extend special editing restrictions (a "contentious topic" designation, meaning extra sanctions are pre-authorized) to Indian, Pakistani, Sri Lankan, and Bangladeshi film articles, which editors say are plagued by caste-driven edit warring, nationalist disputes — and, notably, a flood of low-quality AI-generated content. One editor explicitly cited "LLM junk" as a reason for supporting restrictions, another sign of how AI concerns are bleeding into unrelated policy fights.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A large, wide-participation proposal (125 comments, 37 participants) about creating a new, narrower form of page protection against page-title moves. It's slowed down over the last two days (58 hours since the last comment) but has drawn enough sustained engagement that it looks headed toward an actual policy change.

**[RationalWiki is canvassing about its own deletion debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#RationalWiki_is_canvassing_about_its_AfD)** (ANI) — Extremely fresh (last comment under 20 minutes before this report was compiled) and still unfolding. The subject of a fourth deletion attempt against its own Wikipedia article posted about the debate on its own website, and the resulting back-and-forth has turned into a dispute over canvassing (improperly rallying outside votes), bludgeoning, and whether the original reporter has a conflict of interest. This is the kind of noticeboard dispute with real, if minor, consequences — potential sanctions against a bludgeoning editor — playing out in near-real time.

**["Wikijacking" dispute over reused content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (ANI) — 57 comments. An editor accused another of trying to "steal" and republish their contributed research under his own name after he trimmed duplicated material from an article. It's mostly been resolved by other editors patiently explaining that all Wikipedia contributions are released for anyone to reuse — a useful reminder of a basic but often misunderstood copyright principle (the Creative Commons license all contributions are published under).

**[RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** — A sensitive biography-of-living-persons question (whether to name a soldier accused in a historical killing) with 75 comments and 25 participants, still getting comments within the last 6 hours.

**Pattern to note:** across nearly every major venue this week — policy RfCs, contentious-topic proposals, Good Article reviews, and even routine AfDs — AI-generated content has become a background presence even in discussions nominally about something else (caste conflict, deletion canvassing, reviewer competence). The community looks to be in an active, unsettled period of deciding how much enforcement machinery AI misuse actually needs.
