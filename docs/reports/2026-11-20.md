# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

### [Proposal to make the large-language-model writing guideline a full policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)
**Venue: Village Pump (policy), formal Request for Comment (RfC).** This is the single biggest and most consequential discussion right now: 612 comments from 121 editors on whether "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) — currently just a guideline (a recommended best practice) — should be elevated to a full policy (a standard editors are expected to follow). The proposer, RoySmith, argues the community already enforces it like policy (blocking editors and even desysopping administrators for violations), so the label should match the practice. Opposition centers on concerns that policy status would be too rigid and that there's no accepted mechanism yet for constructive AI use. Given the sheer scale of participation, this is likely to become the definitive statement of how seriously Wikipedia treats AI-generated content going forward.

### [Microsoft Edge's built-in AI rewriting tool conflicts with the no-LLM-writing rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)
**Venue: Village Pump (policy).** 81 comments, still active. An editor noticed Microsoft Edge now auto-offers an AI rewrite/"polish" popup while editing — a feature that could cause good-faith editors to unknowingly violate the policy against AI-written prose (shortcut WP:NOLLM). The thread has turned into a broader argument about how far the narrow exception for AI-assisted grammar/spelling corrections should extend, with some editors (like Athanelar) wanting to eliminate that exception entirely because it's being used as a loophole for wholesale AI rewrites, and others (like GeogSage) defending tools like Grammarly as genuinely useful. Directly relevant to how AI tools are creeping into the editing experience itself, not just article text.

### [Dispute over an editor accused of undisclosed AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)
**Venue: Administrators' Noticeboard.** 130 comments, 162 AI mentions. An editor (May6man) asked for "independent review" of how two other editors handled accusations that they used an AI chatbot to generate their talk-page posts and edits — while claiming they only used AI for "source discovery." Other editors (Nil Einne, IrisChronomia) picked apart inconsistencies in that explanation, suspecting a "semi-agentic AI" account. This is a vivid, almost real-time case study of how the community now investigates and litigates suspected AI misuse — useful material on both the detection methods and the social friction such accusations cause.

### [Proposal for an AI-assisted search tool for Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)
**Venue: Village Pump (idea lab).** 85 comments, 27 participants, 209 AI mentions. A detailed proposal to add an official AI search assistant to Wikipedia's search box — restricted to synthesizing and linking existing Wikipedia/Wikimedia content rather than generating free-standing answers — partly as a response to Wikipedia losing traffic to AI chatbots and search summaries. Early responses are skeptical about cost and necessity, but it's a notable sign of Wikipedians treating AI competition as an existential concern rather than purely a content-integrity problem.

### [Pattern of an editor fast-tracking AI-flavored, promotional drafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F)
**Venue: Administrators' Noticeboard/Incidents (ANI).** 18 comments, still unfolding. Editors flagged reviewer Reading Beans for unusually fast "Articles for Creation" approvals of promotional, apparently AI-generated drafts about Nigerian subjects, drawing comparisons to a previous banned editor (SafariScribe) who ran a similar pattern. The accused reviewer has pushed back forcefully, framing the complaint as biased against Nigerian editors, which has escalated the tone considerably. Worth watching for how the community distinguishes between national/cultural bias accusations and genuine undisclosed paid editing enabled by AI-generated text.

### [Sbb1413 unban request — granted, with explicit AI-literacy conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unban_request_from_Sbb1413)
**Venue: Administrators' Noticeboard.** Already closed as successful. Notable mainly for process: as a condition of unbanning a long-blocked editor, other editors required them to read and acknowledge the AI-writing guideline and a related "AI talk-page comments" guideline (shortcut WP:AITALK) before returning — illustrating how AI literacy has become a standard part of Wikipedia's behavioral expectations, even outside of content disputes.

## Other discussions worth attention

### [RfC: Should Indian/South Asian cinema be placed under tighter editing restrictions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)
**Venue: Village Pump (proposals), formal RfC.** The largest non-AI discussion by far: 239 comments, 51 participants. It asks whether South Asian cinema should get the same special enforcement powers ("general sanctions") already used for caste and Indian military history, with options ranging from no change to restricting all editing to confirmed, trusted accounts. Several supporters explicitly cite a flood of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") content as part of the disruption driving this proposal — an example of an AI problem reshaping governance even in a thread not primarily about AI.

### [Topic-ban proposal for a conflict-of-interest editor on Flavonoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)
**Venue: ANI.** 110 comments, highly active in the last 24 hours. An editor with a declared conflict of interest around flavonoid/nutrition topics resumed edit-warring immediately after a prior inconclusive ANI discussion, then filed what other editors call a "frivolous" case with the Arbitration Committee rather than following normal dispute resolution. Strong support is building for a topic ban, though some editors note the earlier discussion also raised legitimate concerns about the opposing editor's conduct — a real case of overlapping behavioral and content disputes.

### [Proposal for a page-mover-specific move protection level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)
**Venue: Village Pump (proposals).** 131 comments, 37 participants, broadly supportive tone. A technical governance proposal to create a new page-protection tier that trusted "page movers" (but not full administrators) could bypass, reducing the backlog of routine but currently admin-only page moves. Low drama, but high engagement — the kind of structural change that quietly affects a lot of day-to-day editing.

### [RfC: When should templates be allowed to contain article prose?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Should_templates_containing_article_body_content_be_allowed%3F)
**Venue: Village Pump (policy), formal RfC.** 74 comments, 36 participants, still getting replies. Resolves a long-standing ambiguity about templates (like the ones generating standardized Rotten Tomatoes or "List of..." text) that store actual article sentences rather than formatting. A niche but genuinely unsettled policy question with real implications for how much consistency versus local editorial control Wikipedia articles should have.

**Pattern to note:** AI shows up as a subplot even in discussions that aren't nominally about it — the Indian cinema sanctions RfC cites "LLM junk" as a disruption driver, and the Flavonoid and SafariScribe disputes both touch on how AI-polished or AI-written text complicates conduct investigations. Combined with the enormous policy-promotion RfC, it's a strong signal that AI enforcement has moved from a niche concern to a load-bearing part of Wikipedia's day-to-day governance.
