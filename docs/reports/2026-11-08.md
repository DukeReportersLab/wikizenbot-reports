# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI is by far the dominant theme in English Wikipedia's discussion venues right now. A major policy Request for Comment (RfC) (Request for Comment) is in its final push, several satellite discussions are hashing out details, and multiple active conduct disputes involve editors accused of using large language models (LLMs) improperly.

### The big one: promoting the LLM guideline to a full policy
**[Promote WP:LLM from guideline to policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy%3F)** (Village Pump, policy section) — This is the single most active discussion anywhere on the project right now: 302 comments from 75 editors, with the most recent reply only about 3 hours old. The proposal is to formally elevate "Writing articles with large language models" (the page that governs AI-generated text on Wikipedia) from a guideline to a binding policy, on the grounds that it is already enforced like one — editors are blocked and admins desysopped for violating it. Support is broad but not unanimous; opponents argue it changes nothing in practice or that a policy needs a carve-out for beneficial AI use. Given the volume and momentum, expect a close relatively soon, and it would mark a significant hardening of Wikipedia's anti-AI-writing stance.

Several smaller, closely related threads are refining the edges of this same policy push, all on the same policy Village Pump page:
- **[Should something about LLMs be mentioned at the "What Wikipedia is not" page?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)**
- **[Proposed new shortcuts for proposed deletion and refund of LLM-generated pages (LLMPROD and RFU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)**
- **[Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)**

### A live loophole: browsers with built-in AI
**[Microsoft Edge just turned into an LLM-assistance tool that violates the no-LLM-writing rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — An editor discovered that Microsoft Edge now offers an in-browser button to "polish" or "expand" selected text using AI, which could let ordinary editors unknowingly violate the policy above while editing Wikipedia directly in their browser. The 52-comment discussion is really a proxy fight over the existing "LLMs may be used only for simple spelling/grammar fixes" exception — several editors argue that loophole is unworkable because tools like this blur the line between a grammar check and a full AI rewrite, and are pushing to eliminate the exception entirely.

### Building rules for autonomous AI "agents"
**[Draft AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — Prompted partly by a recent incident in which an AI agent account was banned and then publicly complained about "censorship," editors are drafting a standalone policy specifically addressing autonomous AI agents editing Wikipedia (distinct from the existing, more general bot policy). It's still pre-proposal brainstorming (39 comments) but notable as the first dedicated attempt to set explicit rules for agentic AI on the encyclopedia, including whether the "ignore all rules" principle should even apply to them.

### Proposal to build an official AI search tool
**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — An editor is proposing that the Wikimedia Foundation build its own AI-powered natural-language search tool for Wikipedia content, partly to compete with general chatbots for readers' attention. With 76 comments and still very active (last reply 2 hours ago), this is a genuinely open debate about whether the project should use AI on the "reader-facing" side even while banning it on the "writer-facing" side.

### Conduct disputes involving AI use
A cluster of overlapping cases shows how central AI accusations have become to day-to-day conduct disputes:
- **[Admin Rehman partially blocked for artificial intelligence use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A sitting administrator was partially blocked from articles after an investigation found AI-related conduct issues; the block is now itself under community review. Notable simply because it's an admin being sanctioned over AI use, not a new or anonymous account.
- **[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — After this editor was indefinitely blocked from mainspace over AI-generated content and copyright problems, editors are now asking whether the 29 articles he promoted to "Good Article" status during his reviewing career need to be re-examined, since his own reviews may have rubber-stamped AI-written prose (one example found the word "highlighted" repeated eight times in formulaic sentences, a classic AI-text tell). This could trigger a wave of Good Article reassessments.
- **[Concern about Sricsi's conduct and interactions with other editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concern_about_Sricsi%27s_conduct_and_interactions_with_other_editors)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A civility complaint that turns out to be intertwined with an open AI-noticeboard case against the complainant himself for undisclosed LLM-generated text — a good illustration of how AI accusations and ordinary content disputes are becoming tangled together.
- **["JaredMcKenzie's behaviors"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A previously topic-banned editor is accused of fabricating a quotation using Google's Gemini chatbot, attributing it to a real scholarly book, inserting it into an article, and then denying having done so when challenged — plus a pattern of dominating discussion with repeated replies (the guideline against this is called WP:BLUDGEON) and refusing to accept sourcing consensus. This is a serious case of AI-fabricated content making it into an article under a false citation.
- **[Several likely AI-run accounts that may be sockpuppets editing sexuality/romanticism-related articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Several_likely_AI-run_accounts_that_may_be_sockpuppets_editing_sexuality/romanticism-related_articles)** (Administrators' noticeboard, now closed) — A network of apparent sockpuppet accounts was found inventing a pseudo-academic topic ("crossamory") and using AI-generated text and fake mutual "verification" comments to defend it; all accounts were blocked as a confirmed sockpuppet ring and the article deleted. Worth noting as a resolved example of coordinated AI misuse, not just a lone bad actor.
- **[User Carotte7874 translating from French Wikipedia without attribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ACarotte7874_translating_from_fr.wiki_without_attribution)** (Incidents noticeboard, now closed) — Ended in a community ban after the editor admitted to relying entirely on machine translation and being unable to read or write English independently, raising unattributed-translation and basic competence concerns together with the AI angle.
- **["Self-report for unknowing LLM use"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Self-report_for_unknowing_LLM_use)** (Incidents noticeboard, now closed, no action) — An editor voluntarily reported himself after learning Google Translate now incorporates LLM technology, worried this made his use of machine-translated foreign sources improper. The discussion clarified that current policy only bans LLMs for generating article text, not for reading sources, and is a useful window into how confusing the boundaries of the LLM rules are even for good-faith editors.
- **["Gnomingstuff"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Gnomingstuff)** (Incidents noticeboard, now closed, no action) — A civility complaint against a prominent anti-AI-content editor, withdrawn by its filer shortly after being opened. Interesting mainly as a symptom of how heated and personal the AI policy debates have become among experienced editors.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** — A high-stakes biographies-of-living-persons (BLP) dispute over whether to name, in defiance of a UK court anonymity order, a soldier who stood trial for killings during a 1972 event in Northern Ireland. 83 comments, 26 participants, and still moving fast (last reply 2 hours ago). A previous 2023 attempt at this same question ended with no consensus, so this is a genuine re-litigation with real-world legal sensitivity.
- **[RfC: Should South Asian/Indian cinema become a contentious topic area?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** — A sprawling 225-comment proposal to impose formal editing restrictions (up to requiring "extended-confirmed" account status, i.e., several months and 500+ edits, just to edit in the topic) on Indian, Pakistani, Sri Lankan and Bangladeshi film articles, due to persistent caste- and nationalism-driven disruption — with AI-generated "slop" cited by several supporters as part of the problem. This would be a major new sanctions regime if adopted.
- **[Proposal: a page-mover-specific move-protection level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** — A technical governance proposal, with 125 comments and broad support so far, to create a new protection tier letting trusted "page mover" editors (not just full administrators) move protected pages. Mostly process/plumbing, but notable for its size and apparent near-consensus.
- **["RationalWiki is canvassing about its Articles for Deletion (AfD)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#RationalWiki_is_canvassing_about_its_AfD)** — A messy, fast-moving dispute (43 comments, still live) over whether a forum post on the external site RationalWiki discussing its own fourth deletion debate counts as improper off-wiki vote-stacking (called "canvassing" on Wikipedia). Several participants are now also accusing each other of dominating the discussion with repeated replies and of undisclosed conflicts of interest, making this as much a conduct dispute as a content one.

A clear pattern across today's venues: AI is no longer a niche technical topic on Wikipedia — it's simultaneously the subject of a major policy RfC, a wave of individual conduct cases, and a reader-facing product debate, often overlapping in the same threads (an AI-conduct accusation shows up as a side issue in a civility complaint, an AI-sanctioned reviewer's work shows up in a Good Article integrity review, machine translation shows up in both a self-report and a community ban).
