# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

**[Promote WP:Large language model (LLM) from guideline to policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section) — This is by far the largest and most consequential AI discussion right now: 618 comments from 125 editors, still getting replies within the last few hours. The proposal is to formally elevate the guideline on writing articles with large language models (LLMs) — currently called "WP:LLM" — to full policy status. Supporters argue the community already enforces it like policy (blocking editors and even removing administrator privileges over violations), so the label should match reality; opponents worry this would make the rule harder to amend and warn it won't change outcomes on its own. Given the sheer scale of participation, this is likely to become one of the defining AI-governance decisions on Wikipedia this year.

**[Request for Comment (RfC): AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_AI_agent_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section) — A companion proposal asking whether a separate "AI agent policy" — rules specifically targeting autonomous AI agents (bots that act somewhat independently, as opposed to simple scripts) that edit Wikipedia — should become binding policy rather than just a guideline. Support is currently unanimous among the dozen participants, with an interesting argument that large language models are trained to treat "policy" as a firmer category than "guideline," so naming it policy may make automated agents more likely to actually respect it. One editor has even drafted a machine-readable version of the policy for agents to parse directly.

**[Microsoft Edge just turned into an AI-guideline-violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy section) — A practical, time-sensitive concern: Microsoft's Edge browser has started offering an AI rewrite button directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, prompting worries that ordinary editors will unknowingly violate the policy against undisclosed use of AI-generated text (shortened on-wiki to "NOLLM"). The 81-comment thread turns into a broader argument about where the line sits between permitted AI grammar/spelling assistance and prohibited AI rewriting — several editors want to narrow or eliminate the "spelling and grammar only" exemption entirely because AI tools keep stretching it to justify full rewrites.

**[Request for independent review of users conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A heated, fast-growing thread (131 comments, 23 participants, still active) in which an editor accused of secretly using AI tools is contesting that accusation and asking for review of how two other editors handled the dispute. Other participants quickly point out apparent inconsistencies in the editor's own explanation (for instance, claiming to need an AI tool just to "discover" a talk page they had already visited), and the tone has grown tense, with warnings that a block may follow if the discrepancies aren't resolved. It's a vivid, real-time example of how AI-use accusations are adjudicated — and how quickly they escalate — on Wikipedia.

**[Another SafariScribe?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — Editors suspect a reviewer at "Articles for Creation" has been rapidly approving promotional, likely AI-generated drafts about Nigerian subjects (echoing an earlier banned editor, "SafariScribe"), including at least one article approved just four minutes after submission and another approved after being flagged for AI content once already. The accused reviewer has pushed back forcefully, framing the complaint as nationally biased, which has further inflamed the discussion. This sits at the intersection of two recurring Wikipedia problems: undisclosed paid editing and AI-generated promotional text slipping through review.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — An early-stage but heavily discussed (86 comments, 28 participants) proposal to let readers run AI-assisted natural-language searches across Wikipedia and sister projects, producing a synthesized answer with links back to verifiable wiki text rather than independent chatbot output. It's a notable case of editors trying to channel enthusiasm for AI into something compatible with Wikipedia's human-sourced, verifiable model rather than simply rejecting AI outright.

**Smaller but telling AI-related flashpoints:**
- **[URGENT: Repeated deletion dispute on the Robert A. Dressler biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#URGENT%3A_Repeated_Deletion_of_Robert_A._Dressler%E2%80%99s_Documented_Family%2C_Unsupported_Parentage_Claims_and_Public_Accusations_%E2%80%94_Request_for_Blocks_and_Page_Protection)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) is nominally a family/biography-of-living-persons dispute, but notably the entire original post has been wrapped in a template flagging it as likely AI-generated — a small but concrete glimpse of community tooling now used to triage suspected AI text even in urgent complaints.
- **[Carrot juice, aspersions, and AGF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Carrot_juice%2C_aspersions%2C_and_AGF)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) is primarily a civility complaint over a fringe-science content dispute (plant intelligence), but it includes unsupported accusations that the complaining editor used an AI chatbot to "quote-mine" sources — illustrating how "you used AI" has become a common rhetorical weapon in unrelated disputes, a pattern worth watching.
- **[Unban request from Sbb1413](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unban_request_from_Sbb1413)** (Administrators' noticeboard) has already closed successfully with topic bans, but is worth noting because reviewing admins suspected the unblock appeal itself was AI-polished and required the editor to affirmatively disclaim AI use and demonstrate familiarity with the AI-use guidelines before being reinstated — a new de facto condition for returning editors.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RfC: Should South Asian cinema be placed under contentious-topic sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — The single most active discussion found in this scan: 241 comments from 52 participants, with new replies arriving within the last two hours. It proposes restricting editing on South Asian film articles (India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh) the way caste and Indian military-history topics already are restricted, citing coordinated disruption, caste/nationalist edit-warring, and (per some participants) a flood of low-quality AI-generated additions. Several competing restriction levels are on the table, and the debate already includes accusations of canvassing — this one is headed for a consequential, closely-watched close.

**[Ggck2/Flavonoids Yet Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — A long-running conflict-of-interest and edit-warring case: an editor previously warned over conflict-of-interest editing on flavonoid-related articles returned from a month-long break and immediately resumed edit-warring, then filed a since-widely-mocked arbitration request instead of using normal dispute resolution. With 112 comments and a clear trend toward a topic ban, this looks close to a resolution.

**[User:Headbomb running citation bot in a damaging fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AHeadbomb%5D%5D_running_citation_bot_in_a_damaging_fashion)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — Brand new (last comment under 15 minutes old at time of writing) but worth watching: a complaint that a high-volume citation-formatting editor is overriding an article's established citation style and stripping URL/access-date fields while preparing for a Featured Article nomination, allegedly ignoring an explicit bot-opt-out tag. Still too fresh to have a clear outcome, but the editor's very high edit count and the featured-article stakes make this one likely to escalate.

**[Deletion review of the RationalWiki article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikipedia%3ADeletion_review/Log/2026_November_8%23RationalWiki)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — Already closed with no action, so not urgent, but notable for how contentious it got: accusations flew that the review was "canvassed" by the RationalWiki community itself, though other editors concluded the apparent vote swing was better explained by a related noticeboard thread drawing in regular Wikipedia editors rather than any off-site campaign.

A recurring theme across today's discussions: Wikipedia is simultaneously trying to tighten its rules against AI-generated content (the policy-promotion RfC, the agent-policy RfC, the Edge browser concern) while also fielding a steady stream of individual disputes where "AI use" has become a go-to accusation — sometimes well-founded (SafariScribe-style promotional drafts), sometimes used more as a rhetorical cudgel in unrelated content fights (the fringe-science and conduct-review threads above).
