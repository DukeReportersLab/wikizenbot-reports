# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

### [Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext) — Village Pump (policy)
The single biggest AI story right now: 147 comments from 43 editors, still getting replies as of 15 hours ago. It's a follow-up to an earlier, informally-run discussion about whether the guideline against using large language models to generate content (known by its shortcut, WP:NOLLM) should explicitly allow or explicitly forbid using AI to format "wikitext" (the markup language articles are written in, including citations). Early comments already show the community split between "leave it ambiguous" and "ban it outright," with some editors accusing the question of being a reheated version of a citation-formatting debate that just closed with no consensus. This will likely shape how aggressively AI-assisted editing tools can be used going forward.

### [The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK) — Village Pump (policy)
A smaller but pointed discussion (22 comments, active as of 19 hours ago) asking whether the guideline on AI-written talk-page comments (shortcut LLMTALK) should flatly ban AI-generated comments the way the article-content guideline does, or whether "AI-refined" comments remain acceptable. Editors note the talk-page guideline predates the stricter article guideline and never got updated, creating a real enforcement gap that shows up constantly at the incident noticeboards (see below).

### [AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search) — Village Pump (idea lab)
Brand new proposal, still getting comments within the last hour: should Wikipedia build its own AI-powered natural-language search button, restricted to surfacing and summarizing Wikimedia content (as a defense against readers drifting to general-purpose chatbots)? Early responses are skeptical, questioning cost and whether it differs meaningfully from just asking ChatGPT to cite Wikipedia. Worth watching as an example of the community wrestling with "fight AI with AI."

### [Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests) — Administrators' noticeboard
Started as a routine call for volunteers to clear a backlog of edit requests from paid or conflict-of-interest (COI) editors, but a reviewer posted striking data: requests submitted by COI editors have nearly doubled in 2026 versus 2025, which the poster attributes directly to AI making it easier to mass-produce "surficially passable" requests. This is a concrete, numbers-backed illustration of AI's operational burden on volunteer moderation.

### [RfC on the debated file](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AGenerative_AI_pornography#RfC_on_the_debated_file) — Talk:Generative AI pornography
A content dispute (120 comments, 16 participants) over whether an AI-generated sexualized image should illustrate the article about AI-generated pornography, given its resemblance to content resembling simulated child pornography. This sits at the intersection of AI policy, content moderation and child-safety concerns, and links to a parallel Wikimedia Commons deletion request and a Wikimedia Foundation legal team discussion — so the outcome here could be overtaken by a Commons-level deletion regardless of what this Wikipedia RfC decides.

### Enforcement of the AI content ban keeps generating noticeboard traffic
A clear pattern across several smaller threads: AI misuse accusations are now a routine driver of conduct disputes.
- [Wellness guru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Wellness_guru) and three companion articles are up for deletion specifically because they were created with a large language model, testing whether "the article is now being improved by a human" is a valid defense against the no-LLM-generated-articles rule.
- ["Itsekiri writing" disruptive behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User_%22Itsekiri_writing%22_disruptive_behavior%2C_removal_of_sourced_content_and_edit_warring) is a heated back-and-forth over whether an editor's writing was "basic copyediting" with AI assistance (permitted) or AI-generated content (not), following a prior noticeboard report on the same editor just a week earlier.
- [BrowseTQ Battlegrounding & IDHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT) involves a new account aggressively pushing an AI-generated draft article through the review process while refusing to accept feedback (the guideline against dominating discussion with repeated replies is WP:BLUDGEON; the guideline against ignoring feedback is "I didn't hear that," or WP:IDHT).
- [Behaviour needs attention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Behaviour_needs_attention_-_ongoing_at_the_time_of_report_initiation) and [Pointbreak47 -- AI use after repeated warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Pointbreak47_--_AI_use_after_repeated_warnings) are two more active cases of editors accused of undisclosed AI-generated edits or summaries.

### Bot and policy proposals touching AI use
- [Bot proposal: automatic addition of infobox for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles) (68 comments, very active) would use a large language model (Claude Opus 5.5) to flag uncertain cases while a bot auto-adds standard infoboxes to roughly 9,000–12,000 company articles. Pushback so far centers less on the mechanics and more on discomfort with "throwing an LLM into" what some consider a contentious topic area.
- [Onus for maintenance templates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Onus_for_maintenance_templates) is nominally about tagging etiquette but is really about AI-cleanup practice: who must justify a tag flagging an article as containing AI-generated text when the tagging editor won't explain which passages are suspect.
- [Permit usage of LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions) was shut down by an administrator as "going nowhere" after a wave of blunt rejections — a sign of how little appetite there currently is for loosening AI restrictions in any form.
- [Should admins be running AI Noticeboard?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Should_admins_be_running_AI_Noticeboard%3F) raised governance questions about the specialized noticeboard for AI-content disputes, though it has gone quiet (no comments in 12 days).
- A one-comment pointer to a [new essay, "Wikipedia and generative AI, different things with different purposes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#New_essay%3A_%22Wikipedia_and_generative_AI%2C_different_things_with_different_purposes%22), suggests continued community effort to articulate first-principles arguments against conflating Wikipedia with AI tools.

## Other discussions worth attention

### [Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber) — Administrators' noticeboard
By far the largest thread anywhere right now: 307 comments from 63 editors, still receiving replies minutes ago. What began as a complaint about snarky reverts by a long-time editor ("Roxy the dog") on textile-related articles has grown into a broader examination of a years-long block history and pattern of incivility, including an on-the-record apology that was immediately undercut by further abrasive comments. This looks headed toward a sanction discussion.

### [ARBPIA Topic Ban Appeal (JDiala)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#ARBPIA_Topic_Ban_Appeal_%28JDiala%29) — Administrators' noticeboard
This started as a routine appeal of a 2024 topic ban from Israeli-Palestinian-conflict articles, but the discussion uncovered a pattern of "Jew tagging" edits and escalated sharply — it has since closed with a full site ban imposed by community consensus after what the closing administrator described as the appellant "epically melting down" mid-discussion. A dramatic outcome worth noting even though the debate itself has concluded.

### [RfC: Should this be characterized in relation to CSAM?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F) — Talk:Lolicon
A high-participation (101 comments, 21 editors), highly sensitive debate over whether the lede of the Lolicon article should characterize the genre in relation to child sexual abuse material (CSAM). Still drawing comments as of roughly three days ago; not AI-specific, but it overlaps conceptually with the "Generative AI pornography" dispute above and could set precedent relevant to it.

### [Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection) — Village Pump (proposals)
A technical-policy proposal (77 comments, 29 participants, active as of 19 hours ago) to create a new, narrower form of page-move protection for editors with the "page mover" permission. Steady, substantial engagement suggests this is heading toward an actual implementation decision soon.

### Fast-moving conduct cases at Incidents
[Truthnuke90 — continued disruptive editing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Truthnuke90_-_continued_disruptive_editing_and_accusations_of_vandalism_despite_warnings) (35 comments, very recent) and the [TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2) (comment posted within the last hour) are both live sanction discussions likely to close within a day or two — the kind of noticeboard business that moves fast but affects only one editor each, in contrast to the sprawling, wide-participation debates above.

---

**Pattern to note:** AI now shows up as a factor in an unusually wide range of venues simultaneously — not just dedicated policy RfCs, but routine conflict-of-interest backlogs, bot-approval requests, article-deletion debates, and day-to-day conduct disputes at the incident noticeboards. The throughline across nearly all of them is enforcement ambiguity: existing guidelines (NOLLM for articles, LLMTALK for talk pages) are being stretched to cover edge cases — wikitext formatting, "AI-refined" prose, bot-assisted maintenance — faster than the community can cleanly update the wording.
