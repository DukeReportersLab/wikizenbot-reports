# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-Related Discussions

AI use and enforcement is the dominant thread running through today's noticeboards. A cluster of fresh Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI) cases shows the community actively catching and acting on AI-generated content, while several policy pages are trying (again) to pin down exactly where the line should be drawn.

**Enforcement is moving fast and visibly.** Several new incident reports this week involve editors accused of using large language models (LLMs, the technology behind chatbots like ChatGPT) to generate article text, edit summaries, or even appeals:
- [Pointbreak47 — AI use after repeated warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Pointbreak47_--_AI_use_after_repeated_warnings) (ANI): A user with a prior final warning for AI use was reported and indefinitely blocked within minutes — one of the fastest resolutions in today's reports.
- [Repeated recreations of AI-generated and non-English content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Repeated_recreations_of_AI-generated_and_non-English_content_in_draft_and_main) (ANI): An editor kept recreating an AI-written article about a Punjabi village after repeated rejections; now blocked from article space.
- [Dispute over "Itsekiri writing"'s edits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User_%22Itsekiri_writing%22_disruptive_behavior%2C_removal_of_sourced_content_and_edit_warring) (ANI): A still-unresolved edit war where the accused editor admits to using AI for "refining" text while denying it counts as AI-generated content — a good real-world test of how blurry that line is in practice.
- [BrowseTQ battleground conduct over a rejected draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT) (ANI): Reviewers traced a draft's prose to distinct "AI chatbot eras," showing how experienced editors are now forensically detecting LLM authorship by writing style.
- [Behaviour dispute involving mutual AI accusations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Behaviour_needs_attention_-_ongoing_at_the_time_of_report_initiation) (ANI): Two editors each accuse the other of machine-generated content and possible sockpuppetry — unresolved and worth watching.

**At Articles for Deletion (AfD):** [Wellness guru and three related articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Wellness_guru) are being deleted en masse as LLM-generated content, following a recent precedent case. Supporters argue the articles are being actively cleaned up by humans; the nominator counters that the rule against AI-written articles (shortcut NOLLM) has no "fix it after the fact" exception — a dispute that will likely recur as more legacy AI-written articles are discovered.

**Policy pages are still catching up with practice.** [The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK) (Village Pump, policy section) asks whether the guideline on AI use in talk-page comments (LLMTALK) should be tightened to match the stricter article-content rule, since in practice admins already treat AI-written comments harshly. A related proposal, [permitting LLMs for user pages and image captions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Permit_usage_of_LLMs_for_user_pages_and_image_captions), was just closed as "going nowhere" after a wall of blunt rejections — a sign the community has little patience left for proposals seen as chipping away at the AI ban.

**New angle on how AI is straining the project's back office:** [Nearly 1,000 outstanding conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests) (Administrators' noticeboard) has been raised before, but a volunteer just published hard data: COI edit requests have nearly doubled in 2026 versus 2025, which reviewers attribute largely to paid editors and companies now using AI to mass-produce more polished-looking requests, overwhelming the volunteers who vet them.

**Also notable:** the Wikimedia Foundation's [test of AI text-to-speech ("Listening to Wikipedia") on the Android app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28technical%29#Listening_to_Wikipedia_Android_app_test) drew sharp pushback from the volunteer human-narration community, who argue it undercuts the Foundation's "human-first" messaging; and a [bot proposal to auto-add infoboxes to ~9,000 company articles using an LLM as a confidence filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles) is getting pushback specifically over "throwing an LLM into a contentious topic area" (a reference to the discretionary-sanctions regime applied to disputed subjects), even though infoboxes themselves aren't usually controversial for companies.

## Other Discussions Worth Attention

1. **[ARBPIA Topic Ban Appeal (JDiala)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#ARBPIA_Topic_Ban_Appeal_%28JDiala%29)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — **Now closed.** An editor's appeal of a topic ban from the Israel-Palestine arbitration topic area (ARBPIA) backfired badly: evidence of years of tagging article subjects' Jewish ethnicity irrelevantly surfaced mid-discussion, and the editor "melted down," leading the community to escalate straight to a full site ban. A sharp example of how an appeal can turn into something much worse.

2. **[Articles for deletion: Failed execution of Christa Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Failed_execution_of_Christa_Pike)** (AfD) — An unusually well-attended deletion debate (29 participants) over a standalone article about a botched execution, splitting editors over whether unique, heavily-covered incidents deserve their own page separate from the main biography.

3. **[TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** (ANI) — A declared-conflict-of-interest academic editor, previously blocked and unblocked under a false identity, is now facing a proposed topic ban from polyphenol-related articles amid accusations of using his expertise to push his own research and industry ties.

4. **[Request for Comment (RfC): American Law Institute under NACADEMIC #3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_talk%3ANotability_%28academics%29#RfC%3A_American_Law_Institute_under_NACADEMIC_%233)** (Wikipedia talk: Notability (academics)) — A brand-new, fast-moving Request for Comment (RfC) on how a specific notability criterion for academics should be applied; worth watching as it could set a precedent used across many biography deletion debates.

5. **[RfC: Capitalization of internet/Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_talk%3AManual_of_Style/Capital_letters#RfC%3A_Capitalization_of_%27%27internet/Internet%27%27)** (Manual of Style talk page) — A classic style dispute drawing unusually broad participation (45 editors), over whether "internet" should be capitalized.
