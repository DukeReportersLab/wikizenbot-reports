# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussions

## AI-related discussions

AI is touching nearly every corner of the project right now — new detection tooling, a surge of AI-generated conflict-of-interest (COI) requests, conduct disputes over accusing newcomers of AI use, and at least one AI-written unban appeal. Here's what matters most:

**[Large language model (LLM) Paste Check tool going live this week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28technical%29#LLM_Paste_Check_-_live_later_this_week)** (Village Pump, technical) — The Wikimedia Foundation is deploying an automatic "edit check" that flags when someone pastes text copied directly from a chatbot (detected via length/wording patterns plus telltale metadata that popular chatbots attach). It tags the revision for community review and shows the editor a configurable notice pointing to the local AI policy. Editors are already fighting over the wording of the pop-up — one prominent editor rewrote the default text to be stricter, since English Wikipedia bans large language model (LLM) writing except for narrow translation/spelling exceptions. This is genuinely new infrastructure for catching AI-generated text, worth watching once it ships.

**[Nearly 1,000 outstanding COI edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' Noticeboard, AN) — A volunteer who reviews conflict-of-interest edit requests (where paid or affiliated editors ask others to make changes for them) posted hard data: monthly request volume has roughly tripled in 2026 versus 2025, which they attribute squarely to AI making it easier to generate "surficially passable" requests. The backlog is now nearly 1,000 items deep, with volunteers openly worried it's unsustainable.

**[Dispute over AI-suspicion and biting newcomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Disruptive_gatekeeping_and_systemic_violations_of_WP%3ABITE_by_User%3AGurkubondinn)** (Administrators' Noticeboard/Incidents, ANI) — An editor accuses a prolific AI-cleanup patroller of telling non-native English speakers to "go edit your own-language Wikipedia instead" after flagging their work as AI-generated. Other editors note the report itself reads as AI-generated, and defenders say the patroller is simply holding the line against a "tidal wave of AI rubbish." It's a sharp illustration of the tension between enforcing the no-AI-writing guideline (WP:NOLLM) and the guideline against discouraging newcomers (WP:BITE) — especially for editors using translation tools that are themselves LLM-based.

**[AI-written ban appeal raises red flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Appeal_of_community_ban)** (AN) — An editor appealing a decade-old sock-puppetry ban admitted to using AI to "polish" their appeal. Reviewers pounced, arguing that needing AI to write a short appeal suggests the editor can't adequately review AI-written article content either — though others pointed out the editor's actual edits show no obvious English-competency problems. A small case, but a neat window into how AI use is now scrutinized even in bureaucratic processes far from article space.

**[Suspected AI-written article at deletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Immigrant_entrepreneurship)** (Articles for Deletion, AfD) — "Immigrant entrepreneurship" was nominated as a vague, LLM-flavored essay rather than an encyclopedia article. The main author pushed back forcefully, rewrote and shrank the article by half, and insisted their "essay-style" prose predates AI and reflects professional habit, not chatbot output — while still agreeing broad LLM-written content is a real problem. A good example of how hard it's become to separate "bad human writing" from "AI writing" in deletion debates.

**[Proposal: an official AI-assisted search tool for Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — An editor floats a Foundation-run AI search button that would answer natural-language queries using only Wikimedia content (with verbatim excerpts and links), partly as a response to Wikipedia losing traffic to AI chatbots. Early replies are skeptical about cost and necessity, but it's a notable proposal for how Wikipedia might compete with, rather than just police, generative AI.

**[Who should run the AI cleanup noticeboard?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Should_admins_be_running_AI_Noticeboard%3F)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A modest but telling governance question: should administrators formally oversee the AI Noticeboard (where suspected AI-generated content and related conduct issues are reported), or is it fine as an editor-run cleanup board? Consensus so far leans toward "more volunteers, not more admin authority," but it ties into the broader AINB restructuring discussion happening alongside it.

Smaller but fresh AI-flagged reports worth a mention: a brand-new **[LLM draft spam report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#LLM_draft_spam_by_User%3A~2026-47494-62)** and a combined **[promotional editing / edit-warring / AI report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Promotional_editing%2C_slow_edit_warring%2C_AI%2C_etc.)**, both at ANI — too new (2–6 comments) to have a clear outcome yet, but indicative of how routinely "AI" now appears as a disruption category alongside plain vandalism.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[JDiala's topic-ban appeal ends in a site ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#ARBPIA_Topic_Ban_Appeal_%28JDiala%29)** (AN) — This is the most dramatic resolution of the day. An editor appealed a 2024 topic ban from the Arab-Israeli conflict topic area (ARBPIA), citing good behavior since. Other editors produced a long list of diffs showing a pattern of inserting ethnicity/religion labels onto articles about historical figures ("Jew-tagging"), and the editor reportedly "melted down" mid-discussion. The appeal flipped into a full site ban within about 24 hours — a reminder that unban/unblock requests can escalate fast when editors go digging through history.

**[Request for Comment (RfC): Mugshot as infobox photo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AClavicular_%28influencer%29#RfC%3A_Mugshot_as_Infobox_Photo)** (Talk:Clavicular (influencer)) — A fresh, fast-moving Request for Comment (40 comments, 20 participants, last comment 1.4 hours ago) debating whether a mugshot should be the lead image for a living person's biography — a recurring values clash between neutral-image norms and not sensationalizing legal trouble.
