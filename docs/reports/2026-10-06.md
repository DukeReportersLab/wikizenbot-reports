# WikiZenBot Report — Current State of English Wikipedia Discussions

I scanned all major noticeboards, Village Pump sections, open RfCs, and today's AfD logs, then read the dozen or so threads that stood out for volume, velocity, or stakes. Here's what actually deserves eyes right now, ranked and grouped by why they matter.

## 🔴 Highest stakes — conduct decisions with real consequences

**1. [ARBPIA Topic Ban Appeal (JDiala)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#ARBPIA_Topic_Ban_Appeal_%28JDiala%29) — AN**
A routine topic-ban appeal spiraled: other editors surfaced a pattern of alleged antisemitic "tagging" edits, the appellant reportedly melted down mid-discussion, and the community **closed it as a full site ban** rather than restoring editing privileges. 44 comments, 24 participants, resolved in ~24 hours — a textbook case of an appeal backfiring badly. Worth reading as a cautionary tale and because the close may itself attract scrutiny.

**2. [Bludgeoning at AfD: Smit Machchhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Bludgeoning_at_%5B%5BWikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Smit_Machchhar%5D%5D) — ANI**
Extremely fresh (last comment 24 min before I checked). One editor allegedly replied to *dozens* of different !voters in a single AfD, with suspicion of off-wiki canvassing. Deletion-process integrity issues like this move fast and are worth catching early.

**3. [Bludgeoning on Talk:Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Bludgeoning_on_Talk_Linux) — ANI** *(already closed)*
A single editor produced 73% of the words in a 145-day talk-page dispute over whether Linux is an "operating system." Resulted in an indefinite topic ban from computer-OS topics. Interesting less for urgency and more as a model of how ANI handles pure bludgeoning (no personal attacks, just relentless volume).

## 🟠 Active, large-scale disputes still in motion

**4. [More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption) — ANI**
170 comments, 39 participants, still getting replies every few hours. A sprawling fight over sourcing standards for airport "destination charts" has turned into a referendum on conduct across the whole aviation topic area. This is the kind of thread that keeps feeding on itself — genuinely contentious, not just long.

**5. [~2026-41404-95 (Album of the Year sourcing dispute)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#~2026-41404-95) — ANI**
106 comments between two editors trading wikihounding accusations over whether a review aggregator is reliable — third ANI report on the same pair. Slow-burning (14h since last comment) but a real behavioral dispute with a long paper trail that may need an uninvolved admin to finally separate the parties.

**6. [Talk:Fiber conduct thread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber) — AN**
279 comments/55 participants is the single largest thread I found anywhere. A civility complaint against a long-tenured, frequently-blocked editor ("Roxy the dog") escalated into discussion of an evasive signature and a possible competency/neutrality problem in textile articles. An apology was issued but concerns about ability to change persist — likely headed toward further sanctions discussion.

## 🟡 Policy/process RfCs shaping how Wikipedia works

**7. [RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext) — VPP**
135 comments, 40 participants — one of several ongoing AI-policy fights. This one is procedurally messy (started as a non-RfC, got relabeled), but it's the live edge of Wikipedia's fight over where WP:NOLLM's line sits. High participation, no clear consensus yet — worth watching for the close.

**8. [Close review: Image Carousel feature RfC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Close_review_of_the_RfC_on_the_new_Image_Carousel_feature) — AN**
A dispute over whether a closer correctly read "opt-in vs. opt-out" consensus on a WMF-deployed reader feature. This isn't just internal process-wonkery — the outcome determines what message the community sends to the Wikimedia Foundation about a live product change. 77 comments, 17 participants, still unresolved after 5+ days.

**9. [RfC: mention of blockade in first sentence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AOctober_7_attacks#RfC%3A_mention_of_blockade_in_first_sentence) — Talk:October 7 attacks**
Freshest comment of any RfC I reviewed (1.4h). Classic high-friction ARBPIA-adjacent wording dispute, 55 comments/19 participants, with careful WP:DUE arguments on both sides. Guaranteed to need an experienced closer.

## 🟢 Structural/systemic issue worth attention (not a "dispute," but real)

**10. [Nearly 1000 outstanding COI edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests) — AN**
Not contentious at all, but genuinely important: a well-sourced analysis shows COI edit-request volume has roughly tripled since 2024 (now ~700+/month), partly attributed to AI-assisted submissions, and reviewer capacity isn't keeping pace. This is a quiet capacity crisis rather than a flare-up, and several editors are already organizing a response (new guides, sorting tools). Worth surfacing precisely because it *isn't* loud.

### Patterns worth noting
- **AI is the throughline of the week**: it shows up as a disruptive-editing vector (COI backlog), a policy battleground (NOLLM/Wikitext RfC), and a tooling topic (LLM Paste Check on VPT). Expect more of this.
- **ARBPIA/Israel-Palestine content remains the most reliably contentious topic area**, both in article-RfC form (October 7 blockade wording) and in conduct enforcement (JDiala site ban).
- **Two different flavors of "urgent"**: ANI threads like "More airport disruption" and the Smit Machchhar bludgeoning report are genuinely fast-moving and need near-term admin attention; by contrast, the Image Carousel close review and the AI-Wikitext RfC are slow-moving but high-consequence — they'll shape policy/product direction for a long time even though nobody's about to be blocked over them.
- AfD logs today were unusually quiet — nothing rose above routine notability debates with single-digit comment counts.
