# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussions

## AI-related discussions

### [Promote the "Writing articles with large language models" guideline to policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)
**Village Pump (policy) / open Request for Comment (RfC).** This is by far the largest AI-related discussion right now: 616 comments from 124 participants. The proposal is narrow on its face — elevate the existing guideline on writing articles with large language models (LLMs), known by its shortcut WP:LLM, to full "policy" status without changing its substance — but the scale of participation shows how central AI-generated content has become to the community's self-governance. Supporters argue the guideline is already enforced like policy (it's used to justify blocks and even the removal of administrator status), so the label should match the practice; opponents worry that "policy" status makes the rule harder to revise and worry about creating a two-tier system where notability and sourcing remain mere "guidelines." Given the comment volume, this is close to becoming a landmark decision about how Wikipedia formally governs AI-written content.

### [Microsoft Edge just turned into an AI-writing-tool violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)
**Village Pump (policy).** 81 comments, 25 participants. An editor noticed Microsoft's Edge browser now offers in-line AI rewriting ("polish this draft," "make the tone more empathetic") directly inside the Wikipedia edit box, with no setting change required. The discussion is a preview of an enforcement headache: the guideline against unauthorized large-language-model writing (shortcut WP:NOLLM) permits AI help only for "spelling, punctuation, grammar" — but editors are split on whether "polish this draft" counts as that or is already a rewrite. Several note this is the exact loophole already being exploited in articles flagged as AI slop, and some are pushing to scrap the "simple corrections" exception entirely because it's unenforceable, while others (citing their own good experiences with tools like Grammarly) resist throwing out AI-assisted proofreading altogether.

### [AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)
**Village Pump (idea lab).** 86 comments, 28 participants — a lot of engagement for an "idea lab" brainstorm. The proposal: give Wikipedia's own search box an AI button that would answer natural-language queries using only Wikimedia content (never leaving the site, with no hallucinated outside information), partly as a response to Wikipedia reportedly losing search traffic to AI chatbots. It's a genuinely interesting test case of the community's appetite for "good" uses of AI (official, licensed, source-restricted) versus its general wariness of AI-generated text in articles.

### [Request for independent review of a user's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)
**Administrators' noticeboard (AN).** 130 comments, 22 participants — contentious and still active. An editor (May6man) who was accused of undisclosed large-language-model use asked for an "independent review" of how two other editors handled the dispute, denying any AI use beyond "source discovery." Other editors quickly pointed out that the review request itself reads as AI-generated, and that the editor's own account of needing AI to "discover" a conversation they'd already read doesn't add up. This is a real, unfolding test of how the community adjudicates AI-use accusations in practice — bludgeoning behavior, credibility problems, and a strong chance of eventual sanctions.

### [Another SafariScribe?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F)
**Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI).** Only 19 comments but very fresh (last comment 2 hours ago) and fast-moving. Editors allege that an Articles for Creation (AfC) reviewer, Reading Beans, has been waving through promotional, AI-flavored drafts about Nigerian subjects in minutes rather than the usual lengthy review — echoing the pattern that got another editor, SafariScribe, banned earlier this year for undisclosed paid editing tied to AI-generated promotional content. The accused reviewer has pushed back forcefully, framing the complaint as biased against Nigerian editors, which has escalated the tone considerably. Worth watching for a possible sanction.

### [AI agent (quickly resolved)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent)
**ANI.** A short, already-closed thread: an account suspected of being an autonomous AI agent was reported and indefinitely blocked within an hour. Low drama, but notable as a sign that outright AI "agent" accounts attempting to edit Wikipedia are now showing up often enough to warrant routine, fast enforcement.

### [Drafting advice for non-native English speakers on AI use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AEditing_as_an_ESL_or_EFL_speaker%5D%5D)
**Village Pump (idea lab).** A slower-moving but thoughtful discussion (13 comments over several weeks) drafting a new essay for editors who speak English as a second or foreign language. The core tension: many such editors turn to AI tools to compensate for language gaps, which is exactly the scenario generating a large share of cases at the AI noticeboard. The group is trying to write guidance that validates legitimate machine-assisted translation while warning against using AI to paper over fluency gaps in ways that produce unreliable, "hallucinated" prose.

## Other discussions worth attention

### [RfC: Should South Asian cinema be placed under contentious-topic sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)
**Village Pump (proposals) / RfC.** The single largest discussion scanned today: 239 comments, 51 participants, still active. It proposes formal "contentious topic" sanctions (ranging from edit restrictions for newer accounts up to full semi-protection-style gating) for the entire South Asian film topic area, citing caste-related edit wars, coordinated promotional editing, and — explicitly cited by several participants — a flood of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") article content. There are also live cross-accusations of off-wiki canvassing. This is a genuine governance decision with teeth: whichever option wins will materially change who can edit thousands of articles.

### [Ggck2/Flavonoids Yet Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)
**ANI.** 112 comments, 21 participants, still active. A conflict-of-interest editor who was recently given a second chance has resumed edit-warring on the Flavonoid article immediately upon return, then filed what other editors call a "frivolous" case with the Arbitration Committee (the body that handles the most serious conduct disputes) rather than using normal dispute resolution. A topic ban from food-science and nutrition topics is on the table. This is a clear-consequence case likely to be resolved with a sanction soon.

### [RfC: Should templates containing article body content be allowed?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Should_templates_containing_article_body_content_be_allowed%3F)
**Village Pump (policy) / RfC.** 74 comments, 36 participants. A technical-sounding but consequential proposal about whether article prose can be stored in reusable templates (shared across multiple articles) rather than written directly into each article — touching on editability, attribution, and verifiability concerns. Wide participation suggests real appetite for a policy change either way.

### [Deletion review dispute over the RationalWiki article (now closed)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikipedia%3ADeletion_review/Log/2026_November_8%23RationalWiki)
**ANI.** 84 comments — a heated dispute over alleged off-wiki canvassing and incivility during a deletion review of the RationalWiki article, with accusations flying in multiple directions. It was closed with no action after participants felt the matter had run its course, but it's a useful illustration of how quickly a single article's fate can spiral into a conduct dispute.
