# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI governance is clearly the dominant storyline across Wikipedia's discussion venues right now — the single biggest, most-commented thread anywhere I scanned is about how strictly to regulate AI-written text, and several unrelated disputes (a biography complaint, a noticeboard dispute, a content fight over a fringe-science article) are all getting entangled with accusations of AI use.

- **[Promote the large-language-model writing guideline to a binding policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section) — By far the largest discussion on the whole site at the moment: 625 comments from 128 editors, so large it had to be split off onto its own subpage. The proposal is to upgrade "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) from a guideline to a full policy, formalizing something editors say is already enforced like one (it's already cited in blocks and even in an administrator's removal of their advanced permissions). Still actively being discussed as of an hour ago — this is the one to watch for the direction of Wikipedia's AI rules going forward.
- **[Request for Comment: AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_AI_agent_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section) — A fast-moving companion proposal (34 comments, 25 participants, last comment under 4 hours ago) to create a standalone policy specifically for autonomous "AI agents" that edit Wikipedia directly, separate from the existing bot policy. Early responses are overwhelmingly supportive; one participant has even drafted a machine-readable version of the policy so that compliant AI agents can parse it directly.
- **["Microsoft Edge just turned into an AI-writing-guideline-violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy section) — A practical flashpoint: Microsoft's Edge browser now pops up an AI rewriting tool directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, offering to "polish" or "expand" text. Editors are debating whether this falls inside the narrow allowance for AI-assisted spelling/grammar fixes or whether it's a backdoor to full AI rewriting — with some arguing the "simple correction" exception should be scrapped entirely because it's already being exploited as a loophole.
- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A brainstorming proposal (86 comments, 28 participants) that Wikipedia build its own AI-powered natural-language search tool, strictly scoped to summarizing and linking existing Wikimedia content, partly as a response to Wikipedia losing traffic to AI chatbots. Useful context for how the community is torn between rejecting AI for writing while considering it for retrieval.
- **[Unblock request for LLM misuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_for_LLM_misuse)** (Administrators' noticeboard, now closed) — A concrete enforcement case: an editor partially blocked for years of undisclosed large-language-model use asked to be unblocked, but was caught evading the block with a sock account mid-appeal. Admins declined the unblock and extended the block indefinitely — illustrating how unforgiving the community now is toward AI misuse compounded by block evasion.
- **[Request for independent review of a user's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A sprawling, still-active dispute (131 comments) in which an editor accused of using AI tools asked for review of the two editors who raised the accusation — only for commenters to point out that the review request itself showed signs of being AI-assisted. A good illustration of how hard it's becoming to adjudicate these accusations, and how quickly they can spiral.
- **[Carrot juice, aspersions, and good-faith assumptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Carrot_juice%2C_aspersions%2C_and_AGF)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, very active, last comment under an hour ago) — A content dispute on the "plant intelligence" article has escalated into a conduct complaint, partly because one editor repeatedly accused another of secretly using an AI chatbot to "mine" quotations from sources — with no real evidence offered. Another example of AI accusations being used as a rhetorical weapon in otherwise unrelated disputes.
- **["URGENT" biography complaint about Robert A. Dressler's family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#URGENT%3A_Repeated_Deletion_of_Robert_A._Dressler%E2%80%99s_Documented_Family%2C_Unsupported_Parentage_Claims_and_Public_Accusations_%E2%80%94_Request_for_Blocks_and_Page_Protection)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — A furious, heavily-formatted complaint from someone claiming to be the subject's daughter, demanding blocks over edits to a biography of a deceased former mayor. Notably, other editors have tagged the post itself with a template that collapses likely AI-generated noticeboard posts — showing that AI-detection heuristics are now routinely applied even to the complaints editors bring *to* noticeboards, not just to article text.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[Request for Comment: should South Asian cinema get special sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — One of the largest live discussions on the site (242 comments, 53 participants), weighing whether articles about Indian, Pakistani, Sri Lankan and Bangladeshi film should be placed under the same kind of editing restrictions already used for caste and Indian military history topics. Several participants cite a flood of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") and nationalist/caste-motivated edits as the reason tighter restrictions are needed — another sign of how AI-generated content is reshaping unrelated policy debates.
- **[Ggck2/Flavonoids Yet Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — The single most active thread anywhere right now (115 comments, last comment 30 minutes ago): an editor with an apparent conflict of interest around flavonoid supplements resumed edit-warring immediately after returning from a prior noticeboard dispute, then filed what other editors call a "frivolous" case with Wikipedia's arbitration committee. A topic ban looks likely.
- **[User Headbomb accused of running citation bot in a damaging fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AHeadbomb%5D%5D_running_citation_bot_in_a_damaging_fashion)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, very active) — A high-volume editor's automated citation-formatting tool is accused of stripping useful URL and access-date information from an article being prepared for Featured Article status, against the article's declared citation style and an explicit bot opt-out tag. Still unfolding with no comment gap longer than an hour.
- **[Articles for Deletion: Pokémon and pornography (second nomination)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Pok%C3%A9mon_and_pornography_%282nd_nomination%29)** — A fast-moving deletion debate (37 comments, 16 participants) over whether a stand-alone article on pornographic depictions of Pokémon characters meets notability and "undue weight" standards, or should be merged elsewhere — illustrative of the kind of niche-subculture articles that regularly draw outsized attention at deletion discussions.
- **[Request for Comment: lead image on the Zohran Mamdani article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AZohran_Mamdani#RfC%3A_Lead_image)** — A long-running, heavily-populated dispute (203 comments, 24 participants) over which photograph should illustrate the biography of the New York politician; a reminder that even "simple" image choices on biographies of living people can generate enormous volume.

**Pattern to note:** across almost every venue, accusations of undisclosed AI use are now a recurring rhetorical move in ordinary content and conduct disputes (the plant-intelligence fight, the "independent review" thread, even an unban request), not just something that comes up in dedicated AI-policy discussions. At the same time, the community is actively building out its formal AI rulebook — a full promotion to binding policy, plus a brand-new policy aimed specifically at autonomous AI agents — making this a genuine inflection point in how Wikipedia governs AI-assisted editing.
