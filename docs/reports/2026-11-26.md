# WikiZenBot Report: English Wikipedia Discussion Roundup

## AI-Related Discussions

AI issues dominate today's most active venues, touching policy-making, enforcement, and source reliability simultaneously.

**[Promote the large-language-model (LLM) writing guideline to full policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Village Pump (policy), moved to its own subpage) — This is the single biggest AI story right now: a formal Request for Comment (RfC) asking whether "Writing articles with large language models" should become binding policy rather than just a guideline, since it's already enforced like one (editors are blocked and administrators have even been removed from their role for violating it). It has swelled to over 630 comments from 127 editors — nearly four times the size at which Village Pump guidance says threads should be split off — and remains active. Given the scale, this will likely be one of the most consequential AI-policy decisions on the encyclopedia this year.

**[RfC: Should there be an "AI agent policy"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_AI_agent_policy)** (Village Pump (policy)) — A fast-moving companion proposal (59 comments, 40 participants, last reply only 8 hours ago) to create a dedicated policy specifically targeting autonomous AI agents editing Wikipedia, distinct from the existing bot policy. Supporters argue that large language models are trained to recognize the difference between "policy" and "guideline," so giving agents (and their operators) an unambiguous, clearly labeled policy is the most effective lever available. Early responses are overwhelmingly supportive.

**Enforcement fights are playing out in real time at the noticeboards**, showing what these policies look like in practice:
- **[Request for independent review of a user's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard, AN) — An editor accused of undisclosed AI use is contesting the accusation and alleging mistreatment by the editors who reported them; other participants argue the editor's own defense bears signs of being written by an LLM. With 132 comments and still active, it's a live test case for how the community adjudicates AI-use accusations.
- **[Unblock appeal for LLM misuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_for_LLM_misuse)** (AN, now closed) — A prolific article creator's appeal was rejected after a checkuser investigation caught them evading their partial block with a sock puppet account *while the appeal was pending*; the block was extended indefinitely. A clean example of an AI-misuse sanction being reinforced rather than relaxed.
- **["Application of AI tag for no reason"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Application_of_AI_tag_for_no_reason%2C_refusal_to_elaborate_on_article%27s_supposed_problems)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, ANI, now closed) — An article creator complained about an AI-generated-content tag being applied to their work; closing admin found the tag justified (unverifiable sources, non-existent categories) and instead warned the complainant about their own lack of good faith.
- **["Prof. JS2"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Prof._JS2)** (ANI, fresh, 5 hours old) — An editor is accused of persistent tendentious editing in Iran/Arab ethnicity articles, using ChatGPT to locate sources on genetics and demographics without reading or understanding them well enough to represent them accurately — a distinct failure mode from "AI writes my prose," worth watching as a template for future cases.
- **["Carrot juice, aspersions, and AGF"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Carrot_juice%2C_aspersions%2C_and_AGF)** (ANI) — A content dispute over the "plant intelligence" article in which one editor is accused of repeatedly, baselessly accusing opponents of secretly using AI chatbots (plus unrelated accusations about religious bias) — illustrating how "you used AI" has become a rhetorical weapon in unrelated disputes, not just a sincere policy concern.
- **["Interesting case of agentic or LLM-assisted abuse"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Interesting_case_of_agentic_or_LLM-assisted_abuse_%28Update%3A_actually%2C_action_is_needed_now%29)** (AN) — Editors uncovered a network of sock-puppet accounts behaving like an automated script or AI agent — making identical sandbox edits, generating fake identity userpages, and swapping in malicious links across many language editions of Wikipedia simultaneously. The accounts have since been globally locked, but the thread is a useful case study in detecting non-human editing patterns (e.g., absence of normal editing-tool tags).

**[LLM-generated reports in Middle East Monitor (MEMO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AReliable_sources/Noticeboard#LLM-generated_reports_in_Middle_East_Monitor_%28MEMO%29)** (Reliable sources noticeboard, very active, last reply 5 hours ago) — A news outlet already criticized for publishing unretracted AI-generated propaganda op-eds has now been caught running uncredited news reports that AI-detection tools (Pangram, GPTZero) score as mostly or entirely machine-generated, including one that misquotes a public figure's tweet. With roughly 1,280 citations to this source across Wikipedia articles, a push to downgrade it to "generally unreliable" could trigger a large citation clean-up effort.

Smaller but notable: **["Microsoft Edge just turned into an LLM-violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump (policy)) sparked a substantive debate about whether built-in browser AI rewriting tools (not just chatbots) count as violations of the "no LLM-written prose" guideline, and whether the existing "spelling and grammar only" exception is too easy to abuse — relevant background for both of the big RfCs above. A related technical thread, **["LLM Paste edit check for talk page messages"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28technical%29#LLM_Paste_edit_check_for_talk_page_messages)**, discusses building a tool to flag pasted AI-generated talk-page comments.

## Other Discussions Worth Attention

**[RfC: Should South Asian cinema become a contentious topic area?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump (proposals)) — The largest non-AI discussion by far: 243 comments from 53 editors debating whether to impose editing restrictions (ranging from "extended-confirmed only" to full new-account bans) on Indian, Pakistani, Sri Lankan, and Bangladeshi film articles, which have been plagued by caste-related edit warring, paid editing, and — notably — a flood of low-quality AI-generated content. This would create real, lasting editing restrictions across an entire topic area.

**[RfC: Lead image dispute at Zohran Mamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AZohran_Mamdani#RfC%3A_Lead_image)** (article talk page) — A sprawling 204-comment, 25-participant dispute over which photo to use for the New York City mayor's biography, the kind of seemingly small editorial question that attracts outsized attention on high-profile political figures.

**[Ggck2/Flavonoids Yet Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (ANI) — A conflict-of-interest editor who was given a second chance after a previous disruptive-editing block has resumed edit warring at the Flavonoid article and filed a "frivolous" case with Wikipedia's arbitration committee rather than use normal dispute channels; a topic ban from nutrition-related articles is now being discussed with broad support.

**[Headbomb running citation bot in a damaging fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AHeadbomb%5D%5D_running_citation_bot_in_a_damaging_fashion)** (ANI, active, last reply under 7 hours ago) — A dispute over a long-running, widely-used citation-formatting bot making unwanted or harmful edits at scale; worth watching given how much infrastructure automation underpins day-to-day editing.

**[RfC on coverage of Project DEFUSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ACOVID-19_lab_leak_theory#RfC_on_coverage_of_Project_DEFUSE)** (COVID-19 lab leak theory talk page) — Fresh activity (last comment under 3 hours ago) on how to describe a disputed, unfunded grant proposal relevant to the origins of COVID-19, a perennially contentious topic.

**[User Boomfour partial block request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User_Boomfour_-_Request_partial_block%3A_Chronic_introduction_of_barebone%2C_poor_quality_articles%2C_deliberate_circumvention_of_the_AfC_process%2C_failure_to_communicate)** (ANI) — 54 comments on a user who has been bypassing the "Articles for creation" review process to publish many low-quality articles directly.

### Pattern worth noting
A clear throughline across today's noticeboard traffic is that accusations of AI use are now a routine weapon in ordinary content disputes (seen in both the "Carrot juice" fringe-theory fight and the "independent review" case), separate from the more substantive policy work on LLM-generated prose and AI agents happening at the Village Pump. The community appears to be trying to build firmer, more precisely-scoped rules (the policy-promotion RfC, the AI agent policy RfC) partly *because* the vaguer status quo is being exploited both by genuine misusers and by editors slinging unfounded accusations at each other.
