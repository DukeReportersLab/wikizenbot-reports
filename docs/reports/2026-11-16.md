# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

These dominate today's venues — there's a genuinely major policy change in progress, several real enforcement cases, and a running theme of editors suspected of using chatbots to write their own defenses.

**[Promote the guideline on AI-written text to full policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (formal Request for Comment) — This is the single biggest story right now: a Request for Comment to promote "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) from a guideline to a full policy. It has exploded to 467 comments from 107 editors and is still getting replies within the last 20 minutes. The proposer's argument is simple — the community already blocks editors and even removes administrator status for violating this guideline, so it should formally carry the weight of policy. Given the scale of participation, this looks likely to be a landmark decision in how Wikipedia governs AI-written content.

**[Request for independent review of a user's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — A vivid, live case study of AI enforcement in action. An editor (May6man) asked for review of how two other editors handled accusations that he used an AI chatbot to write his contributions. He insists he only used AI for "source discovery," but other editors point out that his own complaint post bears telltale signs of chatbot generation — and that his explanation for needing AI to "discover" a conversation he had already visited doesn't add up. With 130 comments and 162 mentions of AI-related terms, and still being updated every few hours, this thread is a window into how Wikipedians now detect and respond to suspected covert AI use.

**[Microsoft Edge just turned into a large-language-model-violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — Editors discovered that Microsoft's Edge browser now pops up an AI rewriting tool directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, offering to "polish" or add "empathetic tone" to any selected text — far beyond the narrow spelling/grammar-only exception in the no-large-language-model policy (shortcut WP:NOLLM). With 70 comments and 89 AI-related mentions, the discussion has turned into a broader argument about whether that "grammar-check exception" is a loophole that should be eliminated entirely, since AI tools routinely rewrite whole passages while editors describe it as a "polish."

**[Do we need an AI agent policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — Prompted by a real incident (an autonomous AI agent that was banned from Wikipedia and then publicly complained of being "censored"), editors are drafting a standalone policy specifically addressing autonomous AI agents editing Wikipedia, distinct from the existing, more cumbersome bot policy. 48 comments so far, with broad agreement that current rules don't clearly address agents "by name."

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (idea lab) — A more speculative, forward-looking proposal: adding an AI-powered natural-language search feature to Wikipedia itself, as a way to keep readers from abandoning the site for chatbots. 83 comments and counting (most recently under 12 hours ago), with pointed pushback over cost, reliability, and whether it contradicts the site's human-centered writing principles.

**Smaller but notable AI threads:** [Should something about large language models be mentioned in the "What Wikipedia is not" policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F) and [Are we making AI-use rules clear enough to new editors?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F) are both modest-sized but clearly part of the same wave of policy tightening as the headline Request for Comment above.

**A recurring pattern worth flagging:** multiple unrelated threads today show editors' *own defensive writing* being scrutinized for AI fingerprints. Besides the "independent review" case above, the [unban request from Sbb1413](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unban_request_from_Sbb1413) (Administrators' noticeboard) features an admin flagging that the unban appeal "feels like it was written or refined by AI," prompting the user to explicitly deny it and cite the LLM guideline by name. And at Articles for Creation, [Another SafariScribe?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F) (Incidents noticeboard) raises concerns that a reviewer has been waving through obviously AI-generated promotional drafts about Nigerian subjects, echoing an earlier case (SafariScribe) that resulted in a site ban.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RfC: Should Indian/South Asian cinema become a contentious topic?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A sprawling Request for Comment (238 comments, 52 editors, still active within the last 8 hours) on whether to impose special editing restrictions on South Asian film articles, which have been plagued by caste-based and nationalist edit-warring as well as, notably, AI-generated junk content cited by several supporters as a reason for restriction. This is a slow-burn but very wide-participation discussion heading toward a real policy outcome.

**[Uyghur mass changes dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Uyghur_mass_changes_%28general_sanctions_topic%29)** (Incidents noticeboard) — An extremely fast-moving edit war (84 comments, last reply under an hour ago) over whether to rename "internment camps" to "re-education camps" across more than 100 Uyghur-related articles — a sanctioned topic area where the neutral-point-of-view framing itself is the battleground, and editors are now disputing whether the "bold, revert, discuss" cycle (BRD) was properly followed.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A well-supported technical proposal (131 comments, 37 participants) to create a new intermediate page-move protection level for trusted "page mover" users, reducing the backlog of routine move requests that currently require a full administrator. Less dramatic than the AI or CTOP fights, but likely to pass and have real day-to-day workflow effects.

**[Hounding complaint against Sirfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (Incidents noticeboard) — A long (106-comment), contentious case alleging that one editor was stalking another's contributions to challenge their edits, has just been closed. The outcome: the hounding accusation wasn't substantiated, but the closing admin issued a pointed warning to the complainant about excessive, repetitive replies (the behavior described by the guideline against dominating discussions, shortcut WP:BLUDGEON) and editing one's own comments after others had already replied to them.
