# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussion Roundup

## AI-related discussions

**[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — **Just closed.** This 153-comment, 47-editor Request for Comment (RfC) asked whether the guideline against using large language models (LLMs) for article content, known as "WP:NOLLM," should be explicitly changed to permit or prohibit using AI tools to format Wikipedia's underlying markup code ("wikitext"). The closer found **consensus for no change** — the existing ambiguous guideline stays as-is. The debate revealed deep distrust of AI tools even for mechanical formatting tasks, and it closely followed an earlier, related RfC about AI-generated citations that also ended in "no change." This is probably the single clearest signal right now of where community sentiment on AI sits: skeptical, and resistant to carving out explicit exceptions even for narrow, low-risk uses.

**[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/LLM are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — A smaller but thoughtful companion discussion, asking whether using AI privately for things like deep research or searching massive scanned documents is acceptable even though it's invisible to other editors. Experienced editors largely agreed such private use is fine, but that posting AI-identified sources without personally verifying them is risky — a nuance that matters a great deal given the fabricated-citation cases below.

**[Masterchief6865 potentially posting faked citations in an Articles for Deletion (AfD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AMasterchief6865%5D%5D_potentially_posting_faked_citations_in_an_AfD)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, ANI) — A vivid, concrete example of AI harm: during an Articles for Deletion (AfD) debate, an editor added "supporting" citations that turned out to reference a journal article and an arms-trade database record that simply don't exist — likely AI hallucinations. The editor admitted using AI and the thread turned into a fairly constructive teaching moment, but it's a stark illustration of how AI-fabricated sourcing can nearly corrupt a deletion discussion's outcome.

**[Tran tuan 2511: LLM use and NOENGAGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Tran_tuan_2511%3A_LLM_use_and_NOENGAGE)** (ANI) — An editor with a long history of flagged AI use (translation tools, AI-generated talk-page replies, and now a citation that misstates what its source actually says) who essentially never responds to good-faith attempts at communication. Proposed outcome is a competency-based block, since the pattern — AI-assisted edits plus near-total silence — makes it impossible to coach the editor toward compliance.

**[Itsekiriwriting: Continued AI use and disruptive editing after being warned (part 2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Itsekiriwriting%3A_Continued_AI_use_and_disruptive_editing_after_being_warned_%28part_2%29)** (ANI) — **Resolved with an indefinite block.** A second ANI filing against an editor who kept producing AI-flavored prose (with the exact telltale phrasing patterns reviewers flag as "AI tells") while repeatedly denying AI use, even after effectively admitting it earlier. Notable for how quickly consensus formed around an indefinite block once the inconsistency in the editor's own statements became clear.

**[Advertising on Wikipedia's main page with LLM content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Advertising_on_Wikipedia%27s_main_page_with_LLM_content)** (ANI) — **Closed, but with real process implications.** An article about a video game, written with undisclosed AI assistance by the game's own creator (a declared conflict-of-interest editor), passed through the "Did You Know" (DYK) review process and briefly appeared on Wikipedia's most-viewed page before being deleted — the AI tell was a hidden instruction meant for the AI ("verify that the current App Store description... support these mechanics") left in the invisible wiki markup. Several experienced editors admitted that two separate AI-detection tools and two human reviewers all missed it. This is a genuinely useful case study on how AI content can slip through layered review processes, and discussion has now moved to the DYK project talk page about tightening conflict-of-interest checks.

**[Smokinmans - sock / AI-use / undisclosed paid editing (UPE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Smokinmans_-_sock_/_AI-use_/_UPE.)** (ANI) — A messier, still-open case where editors are debating whether a new account is undisclosed paid editing (editing for pay without disclosure, "UPE"), a sockpuppet evading an existing block, or simply someone who used AI to decorate their user page. The thread is a window into the community's (sometimes overconfident) folk methods for spotting "AI tells" — down to arguing about rounded CSS boxes and emoji use — and shows how unsettled the detection question still is.

**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard, AN) — Less a dispute than a data point, but a striking one: a volunteer who reviews conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests compiled monthly statistics showing new requests have more than doubled in 2026 compared with 2025, with one contributor explicitly attributing the surge to AI — either AI getting better at generating "surficially passable" requests, or COI editors increasingly leaning on AI to produce them. The backlog is now straining volunteer capacity.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — An early-stage brainstorm proposing a Wikipedia-run AI search assistant that would answer natural-language queries using only Wikimedia content, pitched partly as a way to stop readers drifting to AI chatbots instead of visiting Wikipedia directly. Reception is mixed — some see it as a reasonable defensive move, others question the cost versus benefit given the Foundation's past expensive, underused AI-adjacent project ("Abstract Wikipedia").

A cross-cutting pattern worth flagging: AI concerns are no longer confined to "AI policy" threads — they're now a recurring ingredient in ordinary content and conduct disputes (fabricated AfD citations, a contentious-topic proposal for Indian cinema that explicitly cites "LLM junk" as a driver, main-page vetting failures, and conduct cases). The community's detection skills and norms are visibly still being improvised in real time, even as policy itself (per the NOLLM RfC) holds firm at "no change."

## Other discussions worth attention

**[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (ANI) — **Closed and escalated to the Arbitration Committee.** A month-long, 275-comment, 52-participant slog over chronic sourcing and civility disputes in the aviation topic area ended with the case referred to Wikipedia's arbitration body, the project's highest internal court, for a full case — a sign that routine noticeboard processes couldn't contain this dispute.

**[RFC - Indian cinema CTOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals; open RfC) — A proposal to place South Asian cinema under formal "contentious topic" restrictions, similar to caste and Indian military history, due to caste-based edit warring, nationalist disputes, and (as noted above) AI-generated junk edits. 117 comments and still active; a real policy decision with teeth (possible restriction of editing to experienced accounts only) is on the table.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A substantial technical/governance proposal (121 comments, 36 participants) about a new form of page protection tied to the "page mover" user right; steady activity suggests it's heading toward a real policy change rather than fizzling out.

**[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy; open RfC) — A sprawling naming-convention debate (213 comments, 117,000+ characters) about how to disambiguate school article titles by location — low drama, but a case study in how much energy purely procedural naming questions can consume.

**[Racially motivated comments by User:Hjalpingers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Racially_motivated_comments_by_User%3AHjalpingers)** (ANI) — A 61-comment conduct case alleging racially charged remarks by an editor; worth watching for the sanction outcome given the seriousness of the allegation.

**[Dispute over "Third wave of autocratization"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3ABLUDGEON%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ASYNTH%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ANPA%5D%5D%2C_and_%5B%5BWP%3ATE%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3AAlexandraaaacs1989%5D%5D_on_%5B%5BThird_wave_of_autocratization%5D%5D)** (ANI, plus two parallel open RfCs on the article's talk page) — A conduct complaint accusing an editor of dominating the discussion with repeated replies (the guideline against this is nicknamed "BLUDGEON"), combining sources in a way not supported by any one of them ("synthesis," or "SYNTH"), personal attacks, and tendentious editing, running alongside two separate content RfCs on the same article about how to describe global democratic backsliding in Wikipedia's own voice. A conduct dispute and a content dispute reinforcing each other on the same page is usually a sign of a slow-burning edit war worth tracking.
