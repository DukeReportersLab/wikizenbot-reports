# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

### The big one: promoting the large-language-model guideline to policy
**[Promote the large-language-model writing guideline (WP:Large language model (LLM)) from guideline to policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** — a formal Request for Comment (RfC) at Village pump (policy), with 441 comments from 103 editors and still getting replies as of ~13 hours ago. This is by far the largest and most consequential AI discussion right now: the proposal is to formally reclassify "Writing articles with large language models" from a guideline to a policy, on the grounds that it's already being enforced like one (editors are blocked and administrators have even been removed from their position for violating it). Early responses show broad support, though some oppose on the grounds that a policy would be harder to amend, and others want a carve-out mechanism for beneficial AI use before locking in stricter status. This thread is a bellwether for how seriously the community now treats AI-written content, and several other threads below are effectively satellite discussions spun off from it.

### Conduct disputes centered on undisclosed AI use
- **[A request for independent review of one editor's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard, 94 comments, newest reply only 18 minutes old at time of writing) — an editor, accused by two others of using an AI chatbot to write their posts and possibly their edits, asked for an "independent review" of how the accusation was handled. Other editors quickly pointed out that the review request itself reads as AI-generated, and the thread has turned into a pointed discussion of how confidently editors can detect large-language-model writing and how such accusations should be handled fairly. This is moving fast and worth watching for an outcome (block, warning, or dismissal).
- **[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, 29 comments) — an editor already topic-banned once for (among other things) using AI-generated discussion comments is now accused of fabricating a scholarly quotation using Google's Gemini chatbot, inserting it into an article attributed to a real book, and then denying having added it when confronted. Combined with alleged repeated dominating of discussions with rapid-fire replies (a pattern called "bludgeoning"), this is a serious case with real sanction risk.
- **[Unsourced, neutral point of view (NPOV) edits, LLM usage, biographies of living persons (BLP) violations (part 2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Unsourced%2C_NPOV_edits%2C_LLM_usage%2C_BLP_violations_%28part_2%29)** — a repeat report (first raised two months ago with no action taken) about an editor whose edits on biographies of living persons (BLP) repeatedly trigger the software's "paste check" warning (a sign that pasted-in, possibly AI-generated or AI-translated text is involved), combined with misleading edit summaries and point-of-view pushing in the "new religious movements" topic area.
- Also notable but smaller: **[a competence/language-barrier case with suspected AI-generated articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#~2026-47089-17_-_WP%3ACIR_issues_from_a_possible_language_barrier%2C_repeated_and_continuous_failure_to_use_sources%2C_suspected_use_of_WP%3ALLM_to_create_articles)** (38 comments) raises the recurring theme of how to distinguish genuine language-barrier struggles from undisclosed AI assistance.

### Policy infrastructure being built around AI use
Several Village pump (policy) threads are effectively working out the practical mechanics that would follow from the policy promotion above:
- **["Microsoft Edge just turned into a large-language-model-violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (70 comments) — an editor discovered that Microsoft's Edge browser now offers built-in AI rewriting suggestions directly in the edit box, with prompts like "polish this draft" or "make the tone more empathetic." The thread debates whether this crosses the line already permitted for simple grammar/spelling fixes, and whether that narrow exception should be eliminated entirely because it's being exploited as a loophole.
- **[A proposed "AI agent policy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (48 comments) — prompted by a real incident where an autonomous AI agent was banned and complained publicly about censorship, editors are drafting a concise, explicit policy specifically naming autonomous AI "agents" (as distinct from humans using AI tools), partly so that the Wikimedia Foundation's Terms of Use can point to clear, discoverable rules.
- **[Whether large language models should be mentioned in the "what Wikipedia is not" policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)** (22 comments) — a procedural question about where any eventual hard rule against AI-written text should live.
- **[Handling undeletion requests for articles deleted under the AI-content proposed-deletion process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** (14 comments) — editors want to close a loophole where articles deleted for being AI-generated get reflexively restored to the main encyclopedia (rather than to draft space) under the normal "restore any proposed deletion on request" convention.
- **[Whether new editors are adequately warned about AI policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (18 comments) — a practical thread that's already led to real changes to the new-editor "Introduction" tutorial and warning templates.

### Looking forward: AI as a tool, not just a threat
**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village pump idea lab, 82 comments) floats a proposal for an official AI-powered natural-language search tool built on Wikipedia's own content, aimed at winning back readers who now ask chatbots questions instead of visiting Wikipedia directly. It's speculative and non-binding but drawing sustained engagement.

Also of note: an Articles for Deletion (AfD) debate on **[the article "Artificial intelligence in Pakistan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/Artificial_intelligence_in_Pakistan)** is underway, a reminder that AI is now also a notable subject in its own right, not just an editing-process concern.

**Pattern to note:** across nearly every venue, the same tensions keep recurring — how to detect AI-generated or AI-assisted text reliably, whether narrow permitted uses (grammar/spelling help) are being used as a loophole for wholesale AI writing, and how much good faith to extend to editors who may not understand the rules versus those gaming them.

## Other discussions worth attention

1. **[Should South Asian ("Indian") cinema become a contentious topic area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village pump proposals, RfC, 234 comments from 50 editors, still active) — the single largest discussion scanned this round. It would extend special restrictions (already used for caste and Indian military history) to film-related articles, partly because of caste/nationalist edit warring and partly, participants note, because of a flood of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") additions in the topic area.

2. **[Harassment ("hounding") allegations against an editor over template disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, 106 comments, 32 participants) — a long, contentious case has just been closed. The closer found the hounding accusation didn't hold up, criticized the complaining editor for dominating the discussion and repeatedly editing their own posted comments, and called out one particularly inflammatory accusation of "Machiavellian" behavior as having gone unaddressed for a week. A useful example of how a sprawling conduct dispute can end with blame distributed in an unexpected direction.

3. **[Should a new move-protection level exist for trusted "page mover" editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village pump proposals, 131 comments, 37 participants) — a technical governance proposal to create a protection tier between full administrator-only and open editing specifically for page moves, with broad early support but ongoing debate over implementation details.

4. **[Renewed sanctions push against a conflict-of-interest editor on flavonoid/nutrition articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, 56 comments, very recent activity) — after a previous inconclusive discussion, the editor returned and immediately resumed edit warring and filed what other editors call a frivolous arbitration request; a topic ban proposal has fast-building support.

5. **[RfC on redesigning the standard talk-page header](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RfC_on_the_talk_header)** (Village pump proposals, 23 comments, active within the last 8 hours) — a smaller but currently fast-moving procedural RfC about the template shown atop article talk pages.
