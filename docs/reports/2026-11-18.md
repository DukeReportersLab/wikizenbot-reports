# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

**The big one:** a formal [Request for Comment on promoting the large-language-model writing guideline to full policy status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy) is running at Village Pump (policy), and it is enormous: 559 comments from 112 editors, with 830 mentions of AI-related terms. The proposal is narrow on paper — simply re-labeling the existing guideline on "Writing articles with large language models" as binding policy rather than a guideline — but the discussion reveals how central this rule has already become: supporters note it is already used as grounds to block editors and even desysop administrators, while opponents worry that elevating it to policy removes flexibility and forecloses legitimate uses of AI assistance. Given the scale of participation, this is likely to be the most consequential AI-policy decision on English Wikipedia in months.

Several smaller but related threads are feeding into the same debate at Village Pump (policy):
- [A proposal for a dedicated AI agent policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D) argues that the existing bot policy doesn't clearly address autonomous "AI agents" (software that edits Wikipedia on its own initiative), and that a short, explicit, easily-discoverable policy is needed — partly spurred by a real incident where an AI agent that got banned from Wikipedia publicly complained of "censorship."
- [A thread on Microsoft Edge's built-in rewriting tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.) (70 comments) raises alarm that browser-level AI "rewrite"/"polish" features make it trivially easy for well-meaning editors to violate the rule against using large language models to write article text, since the line between "grammar correction" (allowed) and full AI rewriting (not allowed) is blurry and exploitable.
- [A discussion on whether the "What Wikipedia is not" policy should mention LLMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F) is more exploratory, with editors largely agreeing the idea needs more precise wording than a blanket "Wikipedia is not written by AI" slogan, since bots, human-AI collaboration, and legacy AI-written text (some disclosed and accepted years ago) complicate any simple rule.
- In the idea lab, [a proposal for an "AI-assisted search" feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search) (85 comments) suggests the Wikimedia Foundation build its own AI search assistant to stem traffic loss to chatbots, while strictly separating it from article-writing — a lively but early-stage conversation with real pushback about cost, quality, and whether it contradicts anti-AI norms.

On the conduct side, AI accusations are driving real disputes:
- [A request for independent review of conduct at the Administrators' noticeboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct) (130 comments, 162 AI mentions) involves an editor who claims they only used AI for "source discovery" but is accused by other editors — and by the tone of their own posts — of using a large language model to write the very complaint itself. The thread has drawn in experienced editors who argue the complainant's story doesn't add up, and it reads as a live test case for how the community handles editors who deny or minimize AI use.
- [An incident report titled "Another SafariScribe?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F) accuses an Articles-for-Creation reviewer of rapidly approving promotional, AI-written drafts about Nigerian subjects (echoing an earlier banned editor, "SafariScribe," who did similarly). The accused reviewer has responded combatively, accusing critics of national bias, which has only intensified scrutiny of a pattern that looks like undisclosed paid editing combined with AI-generated promotional text.
- [A report on a likely AI agent, "Joey2231"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Likely_AI_agent_Joey2231_rapidly_making_edits_and_drafts), making extremely fast edits to political articles and drafts, was resolved quickly: the account was partial-blocked, then fully blocked within about half an hour once admins noticed near-superhuman editing speed and fabricated references, and an unblock request was declined. It's a small case but a clean, fast example of how administrators are now watching for AI-agent-like editing speed as a red flag.
- [An unban request from a long-blocked editor, Sbb1413](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unban_request_from_Sbb1413), picked up AI scrutiny almost incidentally: a reviewing editor suspected the unblock appeal itself had been drafted or polished with AI assistance, prompting the requester to affirm they hadn't used one and to commit to following the new AI guidelines going forward.

**Pattern worth noting:** across nearly every venue, suspicion of undisclosed AI use is now a first-line accusation in disputes — not just in article content but in the *words editors use to defend themselves* on noticeboards. Several experienced editors are explicitly on the lookout for telltale AI phrasing in talk-page posts and unblock requests, not just in articles.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[Request for Comment on applying contentious-topic restrictions to Indian cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — by far the largest non-AI discussion scanned, with 239 comments from 51 participants and still active as of roughly a day and a half ago. This would give administrators enhanced enforcement powers (akin to those used in the Israel-Palestine or India-Pakistan topic areas) over an entire national cinema topic, a significant expansion of discretionary sanctions that merits a decision.

**[Deletion review dispute over the RationalWiki article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikipedia%3ADeletion_review%2FLog%2F2026_November_8%23RationalWiki)** (Incidents noticeboard) — the most time-pressured item found: still getting fresh comments within the last hour. It centers on accusations that members of the RationalWiki community canvassed a Wikipedia deletion review in its own favor, plus allegations of incivility and bludgeoning (repeatedly dominating the discussion) against named editors. The underlying evidence for canvassing looks thin on inspection, but the dispute has escalated into accusations against an administrator's closing rationale, which raises the stakes.

**[Renewed misconduct report against editor Ggck2 over Flavonoid edit-warring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (Incidents noticeboard, 97 comments, still active within the last few hours) — a repeat conflict-of-interest editor resumed edit warring immediately after returning from a monthlong break and then filed what other editors call a frivolous case with the Arbitration Committee. A topic ban from food science and nutrition topics is being proposed with fairly strong support, illustrating how the community handles conflict-of-interest editors who won't disengage.

**[Proposal to let "page movers" apply move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals, 131 comments, 37 participants) — a substantial permissions proposal that would extend new technical powers to a trusted user group below full administrator status; broad participation suggests real community interest in getting the balance of trust right.

**[General sanctions discussion on "Uyghur mass changes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Uyghur_mass_changes_%28general_sanctions_topic%29)** (Incidents noticeboard, 86 comments, 17 participants) — an active dispute over a wave of edits to Uyghur-related articles, within an area already under heightened sanctions; worth watching given the topic's history of contentiousness.

**[Request for Comment on allowing templates that duplicate article body content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Should_templates_containing_article_body_content_be_allowed%3F)** (Village Pump, policy, 69 comments, 34 participants) — a technical-sounding but consequential content-policy question about whether shared templates should be allowed to hold prose that appears in multiple articles' bodies, which touches on editability, consistency, and verifiability.
