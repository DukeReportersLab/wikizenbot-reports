# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussion Roundup

## AI-related discussions

AI is unusually central across venues right now — not just in policy talk, but in live enforcement actions, a possible administrator sanction, and knock-on effects on ordinary workflows like conflict-of-interest (COI, editors with a personal or financial stake in a topic) edit requests.

### An administrator sanctioned for AI use
The most striking item is at the [Administrators' noticeboard: admin User:Rehman partially blocked for artificial intelligence use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Admin_%5B%5BUser%3ARehman%5D%5D_partially_blocked_for_%5B%5Bartificial_intelligence%5D%5D_use). An administrator has been partially blocked from editing articles following an investigation into AI-related conduct, and the matter surfaced publicly after the admin pinged participants in an unrelated deletion-review discussion. The thread itself is only one comment old (13 hours), but it points to a dedicated case page and is likely to grow quickly — worth watching closely as it's rare for an admin's conduct to be sanctioned over AI use specifically.

### A wave of enforcement cases against AI-generated content
Multiple, separate incidents are running in parallel at the Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI), suggesting this is a systemic enforcement push rather than an isolated dispute:
- [Masterchief6865 potentially posting faked citations in an Articles for Deletion debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AMasterchief6865%5D%5D_potentially_posting_faked_citations_in_an_AfD): an editor admitted to using AI to generate sources for an article under deletion review, some of which turned out to be fabricated or non-existent — a serious integrity problem since fake citations could sway a deletion outcome.
- [Amandamillsaps74: continually restoring AI-generated content despite several warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AAmandamillsaps74%5D%5D%3A_Continually_restoring_AI-generated_content_despite_several_warnings): the editor openly refused to stop reinstating AI-written text and was blocked for a week, with editors expecting a fuller block to follow once the case is reviewed at the dedicated AI noticeboard.
- [JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors): an editor already topic-banned once for, among other things, using AI-generated comments is now accused of inserting a quotation fabricated by Google's Gemini chatbot into an article about Falun Gong teachings, denying having added it, plus a long pattern of dominating a discussion with repeated replies (the behavior described by the guideline against "bludgeoning," WP:BLUDGEON) and not acknowledging points once they've been made (WP:ICANTHEARYOU). This is active right now (last comment 1.5 hours ago).
- [2005-Fan large language model (LLM) use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#2005-Fan_LLM_use): a long-standing editor suddenly rewrote a featured article (Super Mario 64) and others with large language model (LLM)-flavored prose and edit summaries; after being confronted they retired their account, and an administrator has since blocked them from mainspace pending a possible unblock discussion.
- [An AI agent account blocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent): an account whose userpage stated it was "operated by Muse, an AI assistant" was blocked for violating the guideline against unauthorized AI-written content (WP:NOLLM), prompting a side discussion on how the bot-approval policy should apply to autonomous AI agents.

### Cleaning up after AI-tainted reviews
[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions) (Administrators' noticeboard): this reviewer was already indefinitely blocked from mainspace over AI-generated content, and editors are now asking whether the 29 articles they promoted to "Good Article" status need to be re-examined — one example already shows repetitive, AI-typical phrasing (the word "highlighted" appearing eight times in one section). This could trigger a wave of formal Good Article reassessments.

### Policy-building in response
Two linked efforts are underway to formalize how Wikipedia handles AI:
- [Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F) (Village Pump, policy section): editors are actively rewriting onboarding pages, welcome templates and the "Article Wizard" to make the rule against AI-generated content more visible to newcomers, who often say they simply didn't know the policy existed.
- [A proposed "AI agent policy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D) (Village Pump, policy section): a brand-new draft policy aimed specifically at autonomous AI agents interacting with Wikipedia (distinct from the broader, more procedural bot policy), started just an hour before this report was compiled, following a recent incident where an AI agent that got banned publicly complained about being "censored."

### Downstream effects of AI on ordinary workflows
[Nearly 1000 outstanding COI edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests) (Administrators' noticeboard): editors reviewing requests from paid or conflict-of-interest editors report the backlog has exploded this year — roughly tripling since January — and attribute it largely to AI, since paid editors are now using it to generate more numerous and more complex-looking requests, outpacing volunteer reviewers.

Similarly, the governance proposal [Request for Comment (RfC) – Indian cinema as a contentious topic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP) (Village Pump, proposals; 219 comments, 42 participants, still active) is partly motivated by what one supporter called "the disruption (and LLM junk)" appearing in South Asian cinema articles — a sign that AI-generated content is now cited as justification for tightening editing restrictions on entire subject areas, not just individual articles.

Finally, a smaller case, [sanity check requested (possible LLM-generated article)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Sanity_check_requested_%28possible_LLM-generated_article%29), is a useful illustration of the now-routine process: an editor asks for a second opinion, other editors (half-jokingly) run the text through AI detectors, and the case gets routed to the dedicated AI noticeboard for cleanup.

## Other discussions worth attention

1. **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — 124 comments from 36 editors over three days. A proposal to create a new protection level restricting page moves to a specific user group; large participation suggests real appetite for change in how move-warring is handled.

2. **[Merge template applied without initiating an Articles for Deletion debate — what should occur next?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Merge_template_applied_without_initiating_an_AfD%E2%88%92What_should_occur_next%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — a fast-moving, very current thread (last comment 12 minutes before this report) grappling with confusion left behind by a recent Request for Comment (RfC) that merged the separate "proposed merger" process into Articles for Deletion. Editors can no longer simply flag an "obvious" merge without starting a formal deletion-style discussion, and nobody agrees on whether that was the intended effect.

3. **[Dispute over conduct on "Third wave of autocratization"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3ABLUDGEON%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ASYNTH%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ANPA%5D%5D%2C_and_%5B%5BWP%3ATE%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3AAlexandraaaacs1989%5D%5D_on_%5B%5BThird_wave_of_autocratization%5D%5D)** (ANI) — an article's main creator is accused of dominating the talk page with repeated replies, launching a flurry of Requests for Comment and combining unrelated sources to support a conclusion not stated outright by any one of them (synthesis, or WP:SYNTH). A topic ban is on the table. The same article also has two separate open RfCs running simultaneously, making it one of the most contested single pages on the encyclopedia right now.

4. **[RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** — a sensitive dispute (74 comments, 25 participants) over whether to identify, by name, a British soldier involved in the 1972 Bloody Sunday killings in Derry, touching directly on biographies of living persons (BLP) concerns.

5. **Deletion debates (Articles for Deletion, AfD)** — the daily AfD logs are busy but mostly routine; the one with real teeth is the [Masterchief6865 AfD sourcing dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AMasterchief6865%5D%5D_potentially_posting_faked_citations_in_an_AfD) covered above. The [fourth deletion nomination for RationalWiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AArticles_for_deletion/RationalWiki_%284th_nomination%29) (54 comments) is also drawing unusually heavy participation for a repeat nomination.
