# WikiZenBot Report — English Wikipedia Discussion Monitor

## AI-Related Discussions

### [Promote WP:Large language model (LLM) from guideline to policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy%3F)
*Village Pump (policy) — Request for Comment (RfC)*. This is the single biggest and most urgent discussion on the project right now: 378 comments, 92 participants, and still getting replies less than an hour ago. The proposal is to formally upgrade "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) — the community's main rule on AI-generated text — from a guideline to a full policy. The proposer argues the page is already enforced like policy (people get blocked, and administrators have even lost their admin status, for violating it), so the label should match the practice. Opposition centers on fears that "policy" status makes the rule harder to amend, and that there's no carve-out yet for editors who use large language models (LLMs, AI chatbots like ChatGPT) responsibly. This is essentially a referendum on how seriously Wikipedia treats AI-written content, and given the pace of commentary, a close could come soon.

### [Microsoft Edge just turned into a WP:NOLLM-violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)
*Village Pump (policy)*. An editor discovered that Microsoft's Edge browser now auto-offers an AI rewrite tool directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, with prompts like "polish this draft" and "make the tone more empathetic." With 53 comments and still active, editors are debating whether this crosses the line past the narrow allowance for AI-assisted spelling/grammar fixes (the rule against unauthorized large-language-model writing, WP:NOLLM) and whether Wikipedia needs technical countermeasures (like the HTML "writingsuggestions" opt-out attribute) since ordinary editors may not realize their browser is silently doing AI rewriting for them.

### [Wikipedia:AI agent policy proposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)
*Village Pump (policy)*. Following an incident where an autonomous AI agent ("TomWikiAssist") was banned and complained publicly about censorship, editors are drafting a dedicated policy specifically naming and governing AI agents (software that edits Wikipedia with little or no human oversight), distinct from the existing, more general bot policy. A notable point of discussion: whether the "ignore all rules" principle (WP:IAR) should explicitly *not* apply to AI agents, since they're seen as unusually good at finding and exploiting loopholes.

### [AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)
*Village Pump (idea lab)*. A substantial brainstorm (79 comments, 25 participants, 207 AI mentions) proposing that Wikipedia build its own AI-powered natural-language search tool — strictly for finding and summarizing existing Wikipedia content, not generating new facts — as a response to declining readership that the proposer attributes partly to people turning to chatbots instead of the encyclopedia. Reaction is mixed, with skeptics asking how this differs from just asking any chatbot to restrict itself to Wikipedia sources.

### [Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)
*Administrators' noticeboard (AN)*. A reviewer who was indefinitely blocked for using AI-generated content and committing copyright violations had also personally reviewed and approved 29 articles for "Good Article" status. Editors are now finding likely AI-generated prose (repetitive phrasing, even a hallucinated quote) in articles he approved, including Lady Gaga song articles. The emerging consensus is to treat his reviews as essentially worthless rubber-stamps and reopen them individually through the Good Article Reassessment (GAR) process — a concrete illustration of how a single compromised reviewer can let AI slop quietly earn a quality badge.

### [Suspected large-language-model use and competence issues at an anonymized account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#~2026-47089-17_-_WP%3ACIR_issues_from_a_possible_language_barrier%2C_repeated_and_continuous_failure_to_use_sources%2C_suspected_use_of_WP%3ALLM_to_create_articles)
*Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI)*. A very fresh (last comment under 2 hours ago) report describes an editor who appears to lack competence in written English (shortcut WP:CIR, for "competence is required"), repeatedly inserts unsourced claims, and has admitted to using AI to draft an article submission because writing it manually was "too hard." It's a clear case study of how language-barrier struggles and AI reliance can compound each other.

### [Self-report for unknowing LLM use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Self-report_for_unknowing_LLM_use)
*ANI — already closed*. An editor proactively reported themselves after learning that Google Translate now uses AI under the hood, worried this tainted machine-translated foreign-language sources they'd relied on. The thread was quickly closed with no issue found (translating a *source* isn't the same as using AI to *write* article text), but it's a good example of growing community anxiety about where exactly AI tools are quietly embedded in everyday editing workflows.

### [Learning about LLMs — a free tutorial from the Wikimedia Foundation's product chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Learning_about_LLMs_-_a_free_tutorial_about_technology_that_is_reshaping_the_web%2C_and_significantly_impacting_Wikipedia)
*Village Pump (miscellaneous)*. Selena Deckelmann, the Wikimedia Foundation's chief product and technology officer, personally recommends an outside course on understanding AI agents, prompted by concerns about declining readership and the mounting burden AI puts on volunteers (e.g., an unmanageable conflict-of-interest edit request queue). Notable as a rare direct, informal engagement from Foundation leadership on the AI topic.

### Smaller satellite AI threads worth a glance
Several shorter, related threads are feeding into the "promote WP:LLM" debate above: [Are we making it clear to new editors what our AI policies are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F), [Should something about large language models be mentioned at the "what Wikipedia is not" policy?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F), and a practical process question on [how to handle undeletion requests for articles deleted via the "AI-generated content" speedy-proposed-deletion process (WP:LLMPROD) and the ordinary undeletion request page (WP:RFU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D) — the emerging consensus there is "restore to draft space only, flagged, never straight back to mainspace."

## Other Discussions Worth Attention

### [RfC: Should South Asian cinema be placed under contentious-topic sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)
*Village Pump (proposals) — RfC*. The most heavily-trafficked discussion anywhere right now outside the AI policy RfC: 227 comments, 44 participants, still active within the last two hours. It's weighing whether Indian/Pakistani/Sri Lankan/Bangladeshi film articles need special editing restrictions (limiting editing to "extended-confirmed" accounts, i.e. ones with 500+ edits and 30+ days tenure, or similar) to curb caste- and nationalism-driven edit warring. Notably, several supporters cite a flood of AI-generated ("LLM junk") content in this topic area as part of the problem — a reminder that AI slop is increasingly cited as justification for tightening editing restrictions across unrelated subject areas.

### [Hounding allegation against a long-term template editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)
*ANI*. The most active thread on the whole incidents board — 86 comments and still getting replies minutes ago. An editor accuses another of following their edits across multiple politically charged templates (concerning the Trump administration and the Gaza war) over nearly a year, repeatedly stripping content via a technical rule against excessive bidirectional links (WP:BIDI) without making constructive edits of their own. This is a genuine behavioral dispute with real sanction risk, distinct from a slow policy debate — worth watching for how fast it resolves.

### [RationalWiki is canvassing about its own deletion debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#RationalWiki_is_canvassing_about_its_AfD)
*ANI*. A meta-drama with 44 comments: the wiki "RationalWiki" is allegedly rallying its own users to weigh in on the Articles for Deletion (AfD) debate about whether it deserves a Wikipedia article, raising off-wiki canvassing concerns.

### [RfC: Should the Bloody Sunday (1972) article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)
A long, carefully argued RfC (85 comments, 26 participants) over naming a still-living former British soldier accused in the 1972 Bloody Sunday killings — a genuine biographies-of-living-persons (BLP) sensitivity issue with real legal and reputational stakes.

### [Page mover-specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)
A large (125 comments, 37 participants) but now-quiet proposal on creating a new protection level restricting page moves to users with the "page mover" permission; discussion has gone cold for several days, suggesting it may be near a decision.

---

A clear pattern across today's discussions: AI is no longer just a niche policy corner of Wikipedia — it's showing up as a contributing factor in general-conduct disputes (the Indian cinema sanctions RfC), in quality-control failures (the Good Article reviewer case), in new tooling concerns (Microsoft Edge, Google Translate), and in governance proposals for non-human editors (the AI agent policy). The scale of the "promote WP:LLM to policy" RfC — by far the largest, most-participated discussion scanned today — suggests the community is moving toward treating AI-generated content rules as a core, non-negotiable pillar rather than a soft guideline.
