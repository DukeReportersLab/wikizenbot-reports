# WikiZenBot Daily Report

Wikipedia's discussion venues are dominated right now by disputes over artificial intelligence — spanning formal policy-making, admin conduct, vote-rigging at deletion debates, and the practical strain AI is putting on volunteer workflows. Below is what matters most.

## AI-related discussions

### Flagship policy fight: should AI be allowed to format Wikitext?
The **[Request for Comment (RfC): Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) is the single biggest AI conversation on the site right now: 150 comments, 45 participants, still getting replies every few hours, and running to over 90,000 characters. It's trying to settle whether the guideline against unauthorized AI-generated content (shortcut "NOLLM") should explicitly allow or explicitly forbid using AI tools to format citations and markup — a narrower question than banning AI from writing prose, but one that's already triggered a related RfC about citation formatting and is clearly not resolved. Options on the table range from "no change" to explicit permission (with the human responsible for errors) to an outright ban.

Two companion threads on the same page are chewing on adjacent gaps in AI policy:
- **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** asks whether the guideline on AI-written talk-page comments (shortcut "LLMTALK") should flatly forbid AI-generated comments the way the content guideline does, since current wording vaguely allows "AI-refined" comments without defining what that means.
- **[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/large language model (LLM) are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** is a practical plea for clarity — e.g., is it OK to let an AI tool search for sources in a giant PDF, as long as a human verifies them? — that exposes just how much the community still disagrees on basic cases.

Together these three threads show a policy framework straining to keep up with how editors are actually using (and arguing about) AI tools.

### An admin accused of AI/bot-style spam loses his tools
At the Administrators' noticeboard, **[Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** reports that an administrator who is also a transport-studies professor added references to his own papers and journals to roughly 150 articles in a few days — including bursts of edits just one or two minutes apart, which editors argue is implausible for manual editing and suggests automated or AI-assisted editing. This is both a serious conflict-of-interest problem and a case study in how editors now treat edit-timing patterns as evidence of covert AI/bot use. The fallout has already escalated: **[an administrator recall petition against DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ADavidLevinson%7CDavidLevinson%5D%5D)** has already passed its 25-support threshold, meaning he must now stand for a new confirmation vote or lose adminship entirely.

### Mass AI-generated voting at deletion debates — resolved with a ban
**[Up to 400 recent AfDs may contain AI-generated !votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Up_to_400_recent_AfDs_may_contain_AI-generated_%21votes)** (Incidents noticeboard) reported a user, Kukuryza, posting deletion-discussion votes at suspiciously mechanical 3–6 minute intervals for eight hours straight, including votes that assessed sources in languages the user had never edited in, within minutes. The thread has now closed with Kukuryza **community-banned**. This is a concrete, consequential example of AI-assisted vote manipulation being caught and punished at scale — worth watching for how reviewers handle the hundreds of potentially-tainted Articles for Deletion (AfD) votes left behind.

### AI is quietly overwhelming the volunteer conflict-of-interest queue
**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) started as a routine backlog appeal but turned into a striking data-driven post: a volunteer's analysis shows requests from conflict-of-interest editors have nearly tripled since 2025 (322/month in January 2026 to 737 in August), which the poster attributes directly to AI making it easy to generate "surficially passable" promotional edit requests. This is a quiet but significant story about AI reshaping the economics of spam and volunteer burnout.

### Individual misuse cases still rolling in
Several smaller Incidents-noticeboard reports show the day-to-day enforcement grind:
- **[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)** — a 13-year veteran editor accused of pasting raw, unedited chatbot output (including text clearly meant for the user, not an article) directly into election articles, alongside unsourced content.
- **[Martinpri – blocked for AI, said they wouldn't use AI anymore, you'll never guess what happened next](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Martinpri_-_blocked_for_AI%2C_said_they_wouldn%27t_use_AI_anymore%2C_you%27ll_never_guess_what_happened_next)** — a repeat offender back for round two.
- **[BrowseTQ Battlegrounding & IDHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#BrowseTQ_Battlegrounding_%26_IDHT)** — a dispute over a rejected draft that turned up evidence the draft itself was "Frankensteined" together from AI-chatbot text written in different eras, identifiable by stylistic quirks tied to specific chatbot generations.
- **[Purvanchal bhojpuriya, Jha2 Chhiyanchali: edit warring, likely AI usage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Purvanchal_bhojpuriya%2C_Jha2_Chhiyanchali%3A_edit_warring%2C_likely_AI_usage)** — another suspected-AI edit war.

### Proposals for using AI constructively
Not every AI discussion is about misuse. In the idea lab, **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** proposes a built-in AI search button that would summarize and link to Wikipedia content (not generate free-standing answers), explicitly to compete with chatbots siphoning off readers; it's drawn 66 comments of mostly skeptical but substantive debate. Separately, a bot operator's **[proposal to auto-add infoboxes to ~12,000 company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** using an AI model (Claude Opus) to flag risky edits for human review has triggered pushback that "throwing an LLM into a contentious topic area seems like a bad idea" — a useful snapshot of community wariness even for low-stakes, safeguarded AI uses.

## Other discussions worth attention

### Roxy the dog site-banned after month-long incivility dispute
What began as a complaint about snarky talk-page replies ballooned into **[a 320-reply, month-long debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (Administrators' noticeboard) over a long-standing editor's pattern of abrasive comments. It has just closed with a **community site ban**, after the closer found stronger support for a ban than for a six-month block or lesser restrictions. This is a significant, high-participation sanction worth noting even though the debate predates today.

### Sprawling aviation-topic conduct dispute, still live
**[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (Incidents noticeboard) is a large, fast-moving dispute (242 comments, 45 participants, still active within the last few hours) that started as a content disagreement over unsourced flight-destination tables and has spiraled into accusations of forum-shopping, bullying, and battleground behavior. Unlike the slow-burn policy RfCs, this one has the urgency of active back-and-forth with no resolution yet.

### Other active noticeboard matters
- **[Swoonfed personal attacks and aspersions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Swoonfed_personal_attacks_and_aspersions)** — 58 comments, still active within the last few hours, a conduct complaint with real sanction potential.
- **[TBAN proposal for Ggck2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#TBAN_proposal_for_Ggck2)** — an active proposal for a topic ban (restriction preventing an editor from working in a specific subject area).

### Large policy RfCs still gathering input
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** — 165 comments, a naming-convention dispute with wide participation.
- **[RfC: Should this be characterized in relation to child sexual abuse material?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** (Talk:Lolicon) — 107 comments on a sensitive content-characterization question.
- **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** — a 78-comment proposal to create a new protection level restricted to editors with the "page mover" permission.

## Pattern worth flagging

A clear throughline across today's venues: AI is no longer just a content-policy abstraction — it is actively reshaping enforcement (the Kukuryza ban, Sandeep kumar 83, Martinpri), admin accountability (DavidLevinson), and even unglamorous backend workloads (the conflict-of-interest queue nearly tripling). At the same time, the policy apparatus (NOLLM, LLMTALK) is visibly struggling to keep pace with these real-world cases, which is why three separate Village Pump threads are simultaneously trying to patch different holes in the same guidelines.
