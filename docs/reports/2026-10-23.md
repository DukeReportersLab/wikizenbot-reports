# Wikipedia Discussion Report

AI-related conversations dominate the venues right now — a major Request for Comment (RfC, a formal community discussion on English Wikipedia) just closed, a new AI-focused noticeboard has appeared, and several admin-noticeboard cases turn on whether editors are secretly using AI. I've led with those, then covered the busiest non-AI discussions, grouped by theme.

## AI-related discussions

**[RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — Just closed after 153 comments from 47 editors. The question was whether the guideline against unsourced or undisclosed AI-generated content (known by its shortcut, WP:NOLLM) should be explicitly amended to allow or explicitly forbid using AI to format the underlying markup of articles ("wikitext"). The closer found **no consensus to change anything** — the existing cautious, ambiguous status quo stands, with the closing note that "most editors still don't trust AI for the most basic tasks." This is the clearest signal yet of where community sentiment sits on AI tooling.

**[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — A related, still-open discussion asking whether the guideline on AI-written talk-page comments (WP:LLMTALK) should be tightened to match the stricter rule against AI-generated article content. Editors are debating fuzzy terms like "AI-refined" versus "AI-generated," with general agreement that translation and grammar-check uses are fine but everything else is contested.

**[Is there any write-up of what AI/large language model (LLM) uses are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — A practical companion thread: can an editor use AI for "deep research" style source-hunting (e.g., scanning a 1000-page PDF for a phrase) without running afoul of policy? Consensus forming is that AI-assisted *search* for sources is fine if a human verifies everything before it reaches an article, but opinions diverge sharply once the output touches a talk page or draft.

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — A proposal (69 comments, 20 participants) for the Wikimedia Foundation to build an AI-powered natural-language search button that only draws on Wikimedia content, partly as a defense against readers abandoning Wikipedia for chatbots. Early responses are skeptical about cost and necessity, but it's a notable instance of editors proposing *embracing* AI rather than just restricting it.

**[Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** and the related **[administrator recall petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ADavidLevinson%7CDavidLevinson%5D%5D)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — This is the week's biggest AI-adjacent governance story. A sitting administrator, also a university professor, was found to have added roughly 150 citations to his own academic journals across Wikipedia articles in a few days, at a pace and pattern (bursts of edits minutes apart) that raised suspicion of bot or AI-assisted automation on top of an undisclosed conflict of interest. The recall petition against him has since **passed its 25-support threshold**, meaning he must now stand for a new confirmation vote or lose administrator status — a concrete consequence, not just talk.

**[User:FavCourtier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3AFavCourtier)** (ANI — Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — Closed with a **community ban**. An editor was found submitting a stream of AI-generated or AI-assisted draft biographies (framed around Holocaust-related subjects) through the "Articles for Creation" review process, evading scrutiny. Notably, this thread is the first to reference a newly created **"AI noticeboard"** — a dedicated venue, separate from ANI, specifically for reporting suspected undisclosed AI use, suggesting the community is now routing AI-misuse complaints into their own pipeline.

**[Smokinmans – sock / AI-use / undisclosed paid editing (UPE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Smokinmans_-_sock_/_AI-use_/_UPE.)** (ANI) — A vivid illustration of how contentious "AI detection" has become among editors: a new account with unusually polished wikitext formatting is accused of either being a sockpuppet (an account evading a block) or an AI-assisted undisclosed paid editor, with editors citing informal "AI tell" essays and arguing over whether a glossy user page template is itself proof of AI authorship.

**[Nearly 1000 outstanding COI edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Less a dispute than a data point worth knowing: a volunteer who processes conflict-of-interest (COI) edit requests ran the numbers and found the queue has exploded in 2026 (737 new requests in August alone, versus a historical monthly average under 150), attributing the surge directly to AI making it easier for paid editors to generate requests — a quantified look at how AI is reshaping the volume, not just the content, of problematic editing.

**[Bot proposal: automatic addition of infobox for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A bot operator proposes using a commercial large language model (Claude Opus) as a safety filter inside an automated bot that would add standard infoboxes to ~9,000–12,000 company articles. Interesting as a test case for how much trust the community extends to AI used *inside* bot infrastructure rather than for content generation — reaction so far is mixed, with concern about infoboxes as a historically contentious topic.

**Smaller but telling ANI cases** also cluster around suspected AI misuse this week: [Purvanchal bhojpuriya, Jha2 Chhiyanchali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Purvanchal_bhojpuriya%2C_Jha2_Chhiyanchali%3A_edit_warring%2C_likely_AI_usage) (edit warring with suspected AI text), [Tran tuan 2511](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Tran_tuan_2511%3A_LLM_use_and_NOENGAGE) (large language model use combined with refusal to engage in discussion), and [Creation of articles using AI which are not properly sourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Creation_of_articles_using_AI_which_are_not_properly_sourced%2C_linguistic_CIR%2C_inability_to_recognise_reliable_sources_and_deliberate_avoidance_of_AfC) (AI-assisted article creation paired with poor English competency and avoidance of the normal review process). Together with the cases above, these suggest ANI is now routinely handling "suspected AI use" as a distinct disruption category, much as sockpuppetry or promotional editing have long been.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RFC - Indian cinema contentious-topic designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals; very active, last comment under an hour ago, 87 comments) — A live vote on whether South Asian cinema should get special editing restrictions similar to those on caste and India's military history, driven partly by a wave of promotional and "LLM junk" (AI-generated spam) submissions. Worth watching for a swift resolution given the pace of comments.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals; 108 comments, 35 participants, last comment under 2 hours ago) — A technical but consequential proposal to create a new page-protection tier so trusted "page mover" editors, not just administrators, can move protected pages. Support looks strong and it's close to being implemented as a new wiki-wide feature.

**[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — The largest RfC in the system right now by volume: 207 comments from 28 editors over naming conventions for American school articles. Slow-burn rather than urgent, but a significant policy-setting exercise once closed.

**[Brainstorming ways to resolve the gap with "Today's additional featured image" on the Main Page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#Brainstorming_ways_to_resolve_the_gap_with_TWAFI_on_the_Main_Page)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — Extremely active right now (305 comments, 66 participants, last comment under an hour ago) over a cosmetic layout problem created by a new main-page feature. High engagement but low real-world stakes — a good example of the "many comments, little consequence" pattern worth distinguishing from genuinely contentious disputes.

**[Newcomer tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** (Village Pump, miscellaneous; 363 comments, 32 participants) — A sprawling, slow-moving complaint thread arguing that the automated "suggested edits" system for new editors recommends tasks far beyond their skill level, driving newcomers away rather than helping them. Long-running rather than urgent, but notable for its sheer size.

**[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (ANI; 274 comments, 51 participants, active within the last few hours) — An escalating civility dispute over flight-destination tables on airport articles, folded into a wider pattern of recurring conduct problems in the aviation topic area.

**[Swoonfed personal attacks and aspersions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Swoonfed_personal_attacks_and_aspersions)** (ANI; 105 comments, 26 participants) — A large, still-unresolved conduct dispute, one of several sizeable ANI threads this cycle, reflecting a generally contentious few days at that noticeboard.

---

**Pattern to flag for the book:** across nearly every venue, the community's AI anxieties now show up in two distinct flavors — *policy-level* debates (what should NOLLM/LLMTALK formally say) that move slowly and cautiously toward "no change," and *enforcement-level* disputes (is this specific editor using AI, and is that why they're disruptive) that are numerous, fast-moving, and increasingly handled through specialized tooling like the new AI noticeboard and informal "AI tell" essays. The DavidLevinson case is the sharpest example of the two levels colliding: an admin's suspected use of automation/AI for self-promotion triggered a real accountability mechanism (recall petition) within days.
