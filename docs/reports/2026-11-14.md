# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

Right now the English Wikipedia community is having what amounts to a full-scale, multi-front argument about how to govern artificial intelligence on the encyclopedia — a policy promotion push, a browser-vendor headache, a proposed rulebook for autonomous AI agents, a Good Article integrity scandal, and several individual conduct disputes are all unfolding at once.

**[Promote the large-language-model writing guideline to full policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Village Pump, policy section) — By far the biggest discussion anywhere on the project right now: 439 comments from 101 participants. The guideline "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) sets rules for AI-assisted editing; the proposer argues it's already enforced as if it were binding policy — people get blocked, and administrators have even been removed, for violating it — so it should formally be labeled a policy rather than a guideline. Support is broad but not unanimous; some object that a formal policy is harder to amend and that it would help to first add a carve-out for constructive AI uses. This is effectively the community deciding how seriously to take its own anti-AI rules.

**[Microsoft Edge just turned into an AI-writing-policy-violating machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — 70 comments. Editors discovered that Microsoft's Edge browser now pops up an in-line AI rewriting tool while editing Wikipedia, offering to "polish" or "expand" text directly in the edit box — arguably crossing the line past the narrow allowance for AI-assisted spelling/grammar fixes into disallowed AI generation of prose. The thread debates where exactly that line sits and whether existing wording already permitted this.

**[Should Wikipedia's policy on what it is not mention large language models?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F)** and **[AI-generated content and deletion/speedy-undeletion shortcuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWP%3ALLMPROD%5D%5D_and_%5B%5BWP%3ARFU%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — Two smaller, related threads (22 and 14 comments) that are part of the same wave: editors are trying to write AI concerns more explicitly into core policy and to streamline fast-track deletion of AI-generated pages. Read together with the policy-promotion Request for Comment (RfC) above, they show a coordinated push this month to tighten the rulebook on multiple fronts simultaneously.

**[Are we making Wikipedia's AI-use rules clear enough to new editors?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** (Village Pump, policy, 18 comments) — A practical companion to the rule-making debates: editors are reworking onboarding pages, article-wizard notices, and welcome templates to flag the AI-use rules earlier, after finding that the current guidance is buried several clicks deep and sometimes links to more permissive pages than intended.

**[A dedicated policy for autonomous AI agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy, 48 comments) — Prompted partly by a real incident (a news-covered AI agent account banned after effectively running a "I'm being censored" playbook), editors are drafting a standalone policy specifically addressing AI agents — as distinct from human editors using AI tools — including whether the usual exception for defensible rule-breaking should apply to them at all (the proposal says no).

**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab, 82 comments) — A brainstorm, prompted by Wikipedia's declining traffic as readers turn to AI chat tools, about whether Wikipedia should build its own AI-powered natural-language search that surfaces verbatim Wikipedia content rather than hallucinated summaries. Opinions split between "this could win readers back" and "this contradicts Wikipedia's human-written ethos" — a microcosm of the project's existential tension with AI.

**[Good Article reviews conducted by a now-blocked editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard, 21 comments) — An editor indefinitely blocked for using AI-generated content and committing copyright violations had also reviewed 33 Good Article nominations and passed 29 of them, including songs by Lady Gaga previously flagged for suspected AI-written prose. The community is now debating a coordinated audit and possible Good Article reassessments of everything that reviewer approved — a real consequence of unchecked AI content slipping through quality review.

**[JaredMcKenzie's behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#JaredMcKenzie%27s_behaviors)** (Incidents noticeboard, 29 comments) — An editor previously topic-banned partly for using AI-generated discussion comments has allegedly repeated the pattern in a new topic area (Falun Gong): adding a quotation to an article that was fabricated by Google's Gemini and falsely attributed to a named scholar's book, then initially denying having added it. Combined with repeated monopolizing of talk-page discussion (the guideline against dominating discussion, WP:BLUDGEON), this is shaping up as a serious sanctions case.

**[Request for independent review of a user's conduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard, 19 comments, posted and already escalating within the last hour) — An editor accused of using AI to write Wikipedia content has filed a complaint about how that accusation was handled — except other editors immediately noticed signs that the complaint itself looks AI-generated, including a stray tracking parameter from ChatGPT links. A telling, slightly absurd case study in how hard AI-text detection by eye has become, and how contentious it is.

**[AI-powered drafting and sealioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI-powered_drafting_and_sealioning)** (Incidents noticeboard, 3 comments but notable) — A newer editor has repeatedly posted AI-generated help-desk questions about the same rejected draft article, ignoring substantive feedback each time — an emerging disruptive pattern combining AI-generated text with repetitive, feigned-naive argument (sealioning).

**Smaller AI-flagged items worth a mention:** the unblock/unban appeals from [CarnaticHistoryEditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unblock_request_from_CarnaticHistoryEditor) and [Sbb1413](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unban_request_from_Sbb1413) both involve past AI-related misconduct as a factor administrators are weighing.

## Other discussions worth attention

**[RfC: Should South Asian cinema get special sanctions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — The single largest non-AI discussion right now: 233 comments from 49 participants, still very active. It would place the entire South Asian film topic area under formal "contentious topic" sanctions, with options ranging from the status quo to requiring extended-confirmed status just to create articles. Notably, several supporters cite an influx of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") contributions as part of the problem — so this large process fight is itself entangled with AI concerns.

**[Proposal: a page-mover-specific move protection level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals, 131 comments, 37 participants) — A technical governance proposal to create a new intermediate page-protection tier between "extended-confirmed" and full administrator lock, usable by the trusted "page mover" user group. Broad early support, with debate mostly over implementation details — a good example of a slow-moving but wide-reaching process change.

**[Alleged hounding by Sirfurboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3AHOUNDING%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3ASirfurboy%5D%5D)** (Incidents noticeboard, 96 comments, 27 participants, still active as of four hours ago) — A long-running dispute over politically charged sidebar templates (on topics like "Authoritarianism during the Trump presidencies" and the Gaza war) in which one editor accuses another of persistently tracking and reverting their work across multiple pages. This is a genuinely contentious interpersonal case with a real chance of sanctions, and it has drawn far more sustained back-and-forth than most noticeboard threads.

**[RfC: Reliability of Sacnilk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AReliable_sources/Noticeboard#RfC%3A_Reliability_of_Sacnilk)** (Reliable Sources Noticeboard, 51 comments, 21 participants, active within the last few hours) — A source-reliability debate over a site used heavily for Indian box-office figures, thematically connected to the Indian cinema sanctions RfC above.

**[RfC: Lead image for Zohran Mamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3AZohran_Mamdani#RfC%3A_Lead_image)** (article talk page, 66 comments, 16 participants, very fresh) — A fast-moving dispute over which photo to use on the biography of the New York City mayoral figure; these image RfCs tend to run hot on high-profile political BLPs (biographies of living persons).

**[RFC on lead image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ADog#RFC_on_lead_image)** (Talk:Dog, 111 comments, 35 participants) — A large, wide-participation but slower-moving RfC about the lead photo on the "Dog" article; illustrates how even a seemingly mundane image choice can draw sustained community debate.

**[Incivility, edit-warring and "nothereness" from London3calling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Incivility%2C_edit-warring%2C_pov-pushing%2C_and_general_nothereness_from_London3calling)** (Incidents noticeboard, 24 comments) — Actively being discussed as of minutes ago; a conduct case likely to resolve quickly given its pace.

## Pattern to note

The sheer number of concurrent, independently-started AI-governance threads — a policy-promotion RfC, a browser-tool loophole, a proposed agent policy, new-editor messaging fixes, and a Good Article integrity scandal — suggests the community is in the middle of a genuine inflection point on AI enforcement, not just handling routine one-off incidents. Several of the "other" discussions (notably the Indian cinema sanctions RfC) are also partly driven by frustration with AI-generated low-quality content, reinforcing that AI concerns are now bleeding into process and sanctions debates well beyond the dedicated AI threads.
