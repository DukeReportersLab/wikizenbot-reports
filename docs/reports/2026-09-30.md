# Wikipedia Discussion Radar — Current State

I scanned all major Village Pump sections, both admin noticeboards, open RfCs, and today's AfD logs. Below are the discussions most worth attention right now, grouped by urgency type as requested.

## 🔥 Active right now (rapid back-and-forth, decision may be imminent)

**1. [Onus for maintenance templates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Village_pump_(policy)#Onus_for_maintenance_templates) — VP (Policy)**
80 comments, 20 participants, **last comment <1 hour ago**. A dispute over an `{{AI-generated}}` tag on *Deep State* has ballooned into a genuine policy question: who bears the burden of substantiating a maintenance tag, and can an AI-cleanup tagger refuse to explain their reasoning? This is quietly precedent-setting for how the AI-cleanup movement interacts with ordinary editors — worth watching for a real guideline outcome.

**2. [Deacon Vorbis on requested redirects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User:Deacon_Vorbis_on_requested_redirects) — ANI**
114 comments, 32 participants, **last comment <1 hour ago**. Started as a complaint from a frightened new/anonymous editor, it's turned into a broader referendum on one AfC reviewer's redirect-decline pattern, with multiple editors presenting counter-examples. High participant count + still moving = a conduct case trending toward some kind of restriction or clarification.

**3. [RfC: Name of the "Discussion" button](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Village_pump_(proposals)#RfC:_Name_of_the_%22Discussion%22_button) — VP (Proposals)**
101 comments, **46 participants** (highest participant count of anything I found), still active (2.6h). Superficially a UI-naming bikeshed, but it's attracting huge participation because of a real usability concern — readers apparently mistake the "Talk" tab for an AI chatbot or text-to-speech button. Broad, fast-growing consensus process worth watching for close.

**4. [RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Village_pump_(policy)#RfC:_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext) — VP (Policy)**
98 comments, 37 participants, active (2.8h). Direct attempt to codify (or explicitly ban) LLM use for wikitext/citation formatting. Ties into the same AI-governance wave as #1 above — this is shaping up to be one of several converging fights over how far AI tooling is allowed to go, and it could produce an actual policy change to WP:NOLLM.

**5. ["Lazarbeem's CT issues continue after avoiding sanctions"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Administrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Lazarbeem's_CT_issues_continue_after_avoiding_sanctions) — ANI**
Only 23 comments/3 participants, but **last comment 0.2 hours ago** — this is the single freshest thread on the whole noticeboard and alleges a user evading a contentious-topic sanction. Small right now but worth a look before it's acted on.

## Pattern worth noting
A clear theme this cycle: **AI-content governance is the dominant live issue**, cutting across three separate venues simultaneously — the maintenance-tag onus dispute, the NOLLM wikitext RfC, and multiple ANI threads (The-Pope removing LLMPRODs, "Escalating disruption + LLM misuse," "Paid editor continuing to use AI") all revolve around how aggressively AI-generated content should be tagged, removed, or punished. Anyone wanting to influence policy in this area should look at the two VPP AI threads now, while they're still open.

Separately, ANI is unusually loaded with **conduct disputes involving established/veteran editors** (Roxy the dog, Andrew Lancaster, Deacon Vorbis) rather than the usual vandalism/sockpuppet fare — several of these look likely to end in topic bans or restrictions rather than simple warnings.

Routine AfDs today are low-drama (mostly small-consensus non-notable subjects, schools, minor sportspeople) — nothing there rises to the urgency of the above.
