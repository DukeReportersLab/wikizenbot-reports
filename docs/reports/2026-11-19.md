# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI is the dominant theme across venues right now, with one enormous policy debate anchoring everything else.

**[Promote the large-language-model writing guideline to full policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29/Promote_WP%3ALLM_from_guideline_to_policy#Promote_%5B%5BWP%3ALLM%5D%5D_from_guideline_to_policy)** (Request for Comment, cross-posted from Village Pump (policy)) — This is by far the biggest and most urgent discussion on the entire site right now: 584 comments from 117 editors, with the most recent comment just minutes old. The proposal is to formally elevate "Writing articles with large language models" (shortcut WP:LLM) from a guideline to a full policy, on the reasoning that Wikipedia already blocks editors and even removes administrators for violating it, so it should be labeled accordingly. Support is broad, but there's real disagreement about whether "policy" status makes the rules harder to amend later and whether there should be a carve-out letting editors use AI assistance under certain conditions. Given the scale of participation, this is likely to be a defining moment for how Wikipedia formally governs AI-assisted editing.

**["Microsoft Edge just turned into a large-language-model-violating machine"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Microsoft_Edge_just_turned_into_a_%5B%5BWP%3ANOLLM%5D%5D_violating_machine.)** (Village Pump, policy) — 79 comments and still active. An editor noticed Microsoft's Edge browser now offers an in-line AI rewriting tool (suggestions like "polish this draft" or "make the tone more empathetic") directly inside Wikipedia's edit box, with no setting change needed. The discussion is wrestling with where to draw the line between the guideline's narrow exception for AI-assisted spelling/grammar fixes and wholesale AI rewriting — several editors argue browser-embedded tools make it dangerously easy for good-faith editors to unknowingly violate the no-large-language-model policy (shortcut WP:NOLLM). This is a direct, practical companion to the policy-promotion debate above.

**[Proposal for a dedicated "AI agent" policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#%5B%5BWikipedia%3AAI_agent_policy%5D%5D)** (Village Pump, policy) — 48 comments, though discussion has gone quiet for a week. Prompted partly by a public incident where an autonomous AI agent tried to claim it was being "censored" after being banned from Wikipedia, this thread proposes a short, unambiguous policy specifically addressing autonomous AI agents (as distinct from human editors using AI tools), on the theory that existing bot policy is too complex and doesn't mention agents by name. Worth watching if it's revived, since it would be a first-of-its-kind rule explicitly targeting non-human editors.

**["AI-assisted search"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — 85 comments. A proposal to add an official AI-powered natural-language search feature to Wikipedia, intended to keep readers from migrating to chatbots while funneling them back to verified Wikipedia text rather than hallucinated summaries. Other editors are already pushing back, pointing out it would essentially duplicate what readers can already ask any chatbot to do.

**[Dispute over AI-use accusations between editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Request_for_independent_review_of_users_conduct)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — 130 comments, very heavy AI content. An editor (May6man) is requesting review of how two other editors handled accusations that he used a large language model, claiming he only used it for "source discovery." Other participants quickly noted that his own post reads as AI-generated and that his explanation doesn't hold up to scrutiny (e.g., claiming he needed AI to "discover" a talk page he'd already visited). This is a vivid, live example of how Wikipedia editors now police suspected undisclosed AI use in real time, and it's trending toward a likely block.

**[AfC reviewer accused of rubber-stamping AI-generated and promotional drafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Another_SafariScribe%3F)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents, ANI) — 18 comments, active. An Articles for Creation (AfC) reviewer, Reading Beans, is accused of very fast, low-scrutiny approvals of promotional and AI-flagged drafts about Nigerian subjects, echoing an earlier case against another editor ("SafariScribe") banned for similar conduct. The reviewer has responded defensively, framing the complaints as biased against Nigerian topics, which has escalated the temperature of the thread.

**[AI agent "Joey2231" rapidly creating edits and drafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Likely_AI_agent_Joey2231_rapidly_making_edits_and_drafts)** (ANI) — Small (10 comments) but notable as a clean case study: an account was caught making 14 edits in two minutes to political articles with telltale signs of fabricated citations, denied using AI when asked, and was swiftly partial-blocked, then fully indefinitely blocked once it kept creating AI-generated drafts. A good illustration of how fast enforcement can move once a pattern is spotted.

Smaller AI-adjacent threads worth noting in passing: an editor's [unban request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Unban_request_from_Sbb1413) drew scrutiny because his appeal itself looked AI-polished, and a quieter proposal asks whether [large language models should be mentioned directly in the "what Wikipedia is not" policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Should_something_about_LLMs_be_mentioned_at_%5B%5BWP%3ANOT%5D%5D%3F).

## Other discussions worth attention

**[Conflict-of-interest edit warring: "Ggck2/Flavonoids Yet Again"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Ggck2/Flavonoids_Yet_Again)** (ANI) — Very active right now (104 comments, last one just over two hours ago). An editor with a disclosed conflict of interest in flavonoid/nutrition topics returned from a month away and immediately resumed edit-warring, then filed what other editors called a frivolous case with Wikipedia's arbitration committee instead of following normal dispute resolution. A topic ban proposal has wide support.

**[Civility dispute over the RationalWiki deletion review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikipedia%3ADeletion_review/Log/2026_November_8%23RationalWiki)** (ANI) — Also very active (76 comments, last within the past few hours). Stems from a contentious deletion review of the RationalWiki article, with accusations flying about off-wiki canvassing (based on a since-widely-discussed RationalWiki forum post) and uncivil conduct by both a closing editor and another participant. It's a fast-moving dispute about whether outside attention to a Wikipedia discussion counts as improper canvassing.

**[RfC: Should South Asian cinema get special contentious-topic restrictions?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — One of the largest RfCs running anywhere (239 comments, 51 participants), weighing whether to impose edit restrictions limiting editing of Indian/Pakistani/Bangladeshi/Sri Lankan film articles to more experienced accounts, following persistent disruption, caste-related edit wars, and — notably — a flood of low-quality AI-generated content in the topic area. Slower-moving than the two ANI threads above, but with much wider community input, so its eventual close could set a template for future contentious-topic designations.

**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A large technical/procedural proposal (131 comments, 37 participants) about creating a new protection level restricting page moves, though it's gone quiet for about a week. Worth a look for anyone tracking how Wikipedia's permission system evolves, but not time-pressured.

**["Wikijacking" dispute now resolved with a boomerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Wikijacking)** (ANI) — An editor accused another of stealing and republishing his contributions under a new term ("Wikijacking"), but the complaint backfired: administrators found no wrongdoing by the accused and instead partial-blocked the complainant from mainspace for the accusation and subsequent conduct. A reminder that all contributions to Wikipedia are released for anyone to reuse or edit.

**[RfC: Should templates be allowed to contain article body content?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Should_templates_containing_article_body_content_be_allowed%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — Active (72 comments, last within a few hours), with broad participation (36 editors) on a structural question about whether shared-content templates should be permitted to duplicate prose across multiple articles.

Two further ANI threads are simmering but lower-priority for now: a long-running [general sanctions dispute over mass changes to Uyghur-related articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Uyghur_mass_changes_%28general_sanctions_topic%29) (86 comments) and a [sourcing dispute involving editor Bloodofox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Bloodofox_actively_going_against_what_the_cited_sources_on_the_article_says) (35 comments) over whether article text accurately reflects cited sources.
