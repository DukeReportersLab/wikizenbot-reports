# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

Artificial intelligence threads are unusually dense across venues today, spanning big-picture policy questions, concrete enforcement actions, and one very ironic case of an AI-detection tool being turned on the person accusing someone else of using AI.

### Should Wikipedia build its own AI search tool?
**[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Village Pump, idea lab) — 69 comments, 20 participants, by far the highest concentration of AI discussion anywhere right now (194 mentions of AI-related terms). A proposal that Wikipedia add a large-language-model-powered "advanced search" button to compete with chatbots that are siphoning off readers, while keeping it strictly confined to surfacing (not generating) sourced Wikipedia content. Replies range from "this is just outsourcing to a chatbot" to serious questions about cost versus the failed Abstract Wikipedia project. This is a foundational debate about whether the encyclopedia should meet AI tools head-on or stay apart from them.

### How clear are Wikipedia's AI rules, really?
Two linked threads on the policy Village Pump are quietly producing real changes:
- **[Is there any write-up of what AI/large language model (LLM) uses are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** asks whether using an AI tool to hunt for sources or search enormous scanned documents is acceptable. The consensus emerging is that AI-assisted *source-finding* is broadly fine as long as a human verifies every source before it's used — but opinions diverge sharply beyond that.
- **[Are we making it clear to new editors what our policies surrounding AI use are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Are_we_making_it_clear_to_new_editors_what_our_policies_surrounding_AI_use_are%3F)** grew out of frustration that new editors at deletion discussions and the help desk "Teahouse" genuinely don't know the guideline against adding AI/large-language-model-generated text to articles (shortcut: NOLLM). Editors reported they've already started adding AI warnings to welcome templates and the new-user tutorial, with measurable click-through data cited — a rare case of a Village Pump chat translating into same-week action.

### AI is measurably driving a backlog
**[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict-of-interest edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — 77 comments. A volunteer compiled year-by-year statistics showing conflict-of-interest edit requests (submitted by editors with a personal or financial stake in a topic, who must ask others to make changes for them) nearly tripling in 2026 compared to historical trends, and argues this is being driven by AI tools making it easy to generate "surficially passable" requests. The data table is a genuinely striking artifact for anyone tracking AI's real-world effect on Wikipedia's workload.

### A Good Article reviewer's AI problem ripples outward
**[Concerns about Good Article reviews conducted by Camilasdandelions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Concerns_about_Good_Article_reviews_conducted_by_Camilasdandelions)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — an editor previously indefinitely blocked for using AI-generated content is now found to have personally reviewed and promoted 29 articles to "Good Article" status while that was going on. Editors are now finding AI telltale signs (repetitive phrasing, even a hallucinated quote) in the promoted articles and are opening formal reassessments. This is a clear example of unresolved AI-content problems cascading through Wikipedia's quality-control system.

### Enforcement cases at the incident noticeboard
Several smaller but concrete cases show AI-related enforcement in action:
- **[Masterchief6865 potentially posting faked citations in an Articles for Deletion discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BUser%3AMasterchief6865%5D%5D_potentially_posting_faked_citations_in_an_AfD)** — an editor facing deletion of their article added citations to nonexistent journal articles and a nonexistent arms-trade database entry, apparently generated by an AI tool and never checked. The editor has admitted to it. This is a vivid, concrete illustration of AI hallucination directly corrupting a Wikipedia deletion debate.
- **[Tran tuan 2511: LLM use and "no engagement"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Tran_tuan_2511%3A_LLM_use_and_NOENGAGE)** — a long pattern of apparently AI-translated edits with fabricated or mismatched citations, combined with the editor's refusal to respond to any talk-page concerns; a block is being proposed.
- **["AI agent"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#AI_agent)** — an account whose own user page stated it was "operated by Muse, an AI assistant" was reported and has already been blocked, prompting a side discussion about where Wikipedia's bot-approval policy ends and its ban on AI-written content begins.
- **[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)** — a 13-year veteran editor was found pasting raw AI chatbot output (including text clearly meant for the editor, not the article) directly into election articles; the thread has since been closed with socking accounts blocked.
- **[Concerns about unsupported allegations and final rejection of a draft article about Mamoun Abu Nowar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Concerns_about_unsupported_allegations_and_final_rejection_by_Helpful_Raccoon_of_Draft%3A_Mamoun_Abu_Nowar)** — a would-be article author complained that a reviewer unfairly accused their draft of being AI-written. Other editors then ran the complainant's own Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents (ANI) post through AI-detection tools (GPTZero, Pangram), got high-confidence "AI-written" results, and closed the case as having no merit, warning of a possible boomerang sanction against the complainant. A small but telling moment showing how normalized AI-detection tools have become in routine Wikipedia conduct disputes.

## Other discussions worth attention

### Request for Comment (RfC) on sanctions for South Asian cinema
**[RFC - Indian cinema contentious-topic designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#RFC_-_Indian_cinema_CTOP)** (Village Pump, proposals) — the single most active discussion right now: 148 comments, 36 participants, still receiving comments within the last 90 minutes. It would place South Asian cinema under formal "contentious topic" sanctions (extra restrictions like requiring confirmed or extended-confirmed account status to edit), largely because of caste-based edit wars — and notably, several supporters explicitly cite a flood of low-quality AI-generated ("LLM junk") edits in the topic area as a driving reason. Worth watching for how it closes given the size and live pace of the debate.

### Technical permissions dispute
**[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — 124 comments, 36 participants. A proposal to create a new protection level restricting page moves to editors holding the "page mover" permission, to combat disruptive page-move vandalism; still active with comments in the last 24 hours.

### Yasuke samurai dispute closure, upheld
**[Closure Review Request](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Closure_Review_Request)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — a challenge to the closure of a long-running dispute over whether the historical figure Yasuke should be described as a samurai. The review was closed decisively ("no way... this is going to overturn anything"), with the challenger warned they may face a formal sanction for their conduct in reopening the matter. Useful context: this topic has a history of being one of Wikipedia's most contentious.

### Bludgeoning and synthesis dispute on a politically charged article
**[Conduct complaint against User:Alexandraaaacs1989 on "Third wave of autocratization"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#%5B%5BWP%3ABLUDGEON%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ASYNTH%5D%5D%2C_%5B%5BWP%3ANPA%5D%5D%2C_and_%5B%5BWP%3ATE%5D%5D_by_%5B%5BUser%3AAlexandraaaacs1989%5D%5D_on_%5B%5BThird_wave_of_autocratization%5D%5D)** — the article's main author is accused of dominating its talk page with repeated posts (the guideline against this is called WP:BLUDGEON), combining sources to imply conclusions not stated by either source (original synthesis, WP:SYNTH), and launching a cascade of new Requests for Comment faster than the community can process them. A topic ban is being proposed. Two related, still-active Requests for Comment on the same article (one of them updated within the last hour) are feeding directly into this dispute.

### Cross-wiki sockpuppetry spillover
**[Alofi Niue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Alofi_Niue)** (Administrators' noticeboard/Incidents) — a Portuguese Wikipedia administrator is asking English Wikipedia to intervene against an account they blocked as part of a suspected multi-year sockpuppet (fake alternate account) campaign that has now also surfaced on French Wikipedia, reportedly using AI-generated appeal messages there. Fast-moving (last comment under an hour ago) with real disagreement among English Wikipedia editors about whether any rule-breaking has actually happened *here*.

### Large, slower-moving Requests for Comment
Two very large RfCs remain open but have slowed in the last day or two and may be approaching closure:
- **[Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** — 213 comments, 28 participants, a naming-convention fight over how US school articles should be disambiguated.
- **[Should the Bloody Sunday article name "Soldier F"?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ABloody_Sunday_%281972%29#RfC%3A_Should_the_Bloody_Sunday_article_name_Soldier_F%3F)** — 74 comments, 25 participants, still active within the last half day, over naming a living/recently-deceased individual in connection with the 1972 Bloody Sunday killings in Northern Ireland — a biography-of-living-persons-sensitive question with real reputational stakes.
