# Wikipedia Discussion Report

## AI-related discussions

AI is the dominant theme across venues right now — a major policy Request for Comment (RfC), a guideline clarification debate, an admin accountability case tangled up with suspected automated editing, a wave of individual enforcement cases routed through a newly reorganized noticeboard, and hard data showing AI-generated content is swamping volunteer reviewers.

**Policy: what counts as acceptable AI use**

- **[RfC: Permitting or prohibiting AI in creating/formatting Wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Permitting_or_prohibiting_AI_in_creating/formatting_Wikitext)** (Village Pump, policy section) — The single biggest AI discussion right now: 150 comments from 45 editors, asking whether the guideline against machine-generated text (shortcut "NOLLM") should be explicitly changed to *permit* using AI/large language models (LLMs) to format wiki markup (such as citations), explicitly *prohibit* it, or stay silent. This follows a related RfC on AI-formatted citations that recently closed with no consensus, so this is effectively round two of a recurring fight over where to draw the line. Last comment was about two days ago, so it's cooling but unresolved.
- **[The status of LLMTALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#The_status_of_LLMTALK)** (Village Pump, policy) — A companion debate over whether the guideline on AI use in talk-page comments ("LLMTALK") should flatly ban AI-assisted comments the way article content is banned, or continue allowing looser "AI-refined" text. Editors are split between wanting a bright-line rule and wanting to preserve allowances for translation and basic copyediting.
- **[Is there any write-up of what uses of AI/LLM are unambiguously allowed, or are ungovernable?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#Is_there_any_write_up_of_what_uses_of_AI/LLM_are_unambiguously_allowed%2C_or_are_ungovernable%3F)** (Village Pump, policy) — Fresh thread (last comment within the hour) asking for clarity on edge cases like using AI to do "deep research" source-hunting or to search enormous scanned PDFs for a phrase. The practical upshot from experienced editors: AI-found source lists are fine to post for others to vet, but nothing should go straight into an article without full human verification — though there's no written guidance yet, which is exactly the problem being raised.

**Admin accountability: the DavidLevinson case**

- **[Spam and possible AI Bot use by DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Spam_and_possible_AI_Bot_use_by_DavidLevinson)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — An administrator who is also a named professor added citations to his own journal articles across roughly 150 unrelated pages, including bursts of dozens of edits within minutes — behavior other editors say looks automated or AI-assisted. This is a serious case: 61 comments, 32 participants, and still being actively discussed (last comment under 10 minutes ago at time of writing). It's compounded by a years-long pattern of the user barely engaging with talk pages at all.
- **[Administrator recall petition for DavidLevinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#An_administrator_recall_petition_has_been_initiated_for_%5B%5BUser%3ADavidLevinson%7CDavidLevinson%5D%5D)** — Directly downstream of the above: the recall petition has already passed its 25-support threshold, meaning DavidLevinson must now stand for a formal re-request for adminship or run in an administrator election to keep his tools. This is a concrete, time-pressured consequence, not just talk.

**Enforcement: a new hub and a string of individual cases**

- **[AI noticeboard overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#AI_noticeboard_overhaul)** — A brief announcement that the dedicated "AI noticeboard" has been restructured as the community's central venue for tracking individual AI-misuse cases. Several current Incidents-noticeboard threads now explicitly reference open cases there, showing the new system already in use.
- A cluster of enforcement threads at the Incidents noticeboard follows a recognizable pattern: editors — often non-native English speakers — leaning on LLMs for translation, sourcing, or article drafting, then being caught out when AI artifacts (broken formatting, chatbot links, literal "as an AI language model" text) surface in articles. Examples: **[Tran tuan 2511: LLM use and the guideline against disengaging from scrutiny ("NOENGAGE")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Tran_tuan_2511%3A_LLM_use_and_NOENGAGE)**, **["Creation of articles using AI which are not properly sourced"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Creation_of_articles_using_AI_which_are_not_properly_sourced%2C_linguistic_CIR%2C_inability_to_recognise_reliable_sources_and_deliberate_avoidance_of_AfC)** (mass-created, AI-sourced village articles bypassing the Articles for Creation review process), and **["Martinpri – blocked for AI, said they wouldn't use AI anymore..."](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#Martinpri_-_blocked_for_AI%2C_said_they_wouldn%27t_use_AI_anymore%2C_you%27ll_never_guess_what_happened_next)**, where an editor who promised to stop using AI for sourcing was caught doing exactly that a week later (a ChatGPT referral link embedded in a citation URL) and was indefinitely blocked. Also related: **[User:Sandeep kumar 83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#User%3ASandeep_kumar_83)**, where a 13-year veteran editor has reportedly pasted raw chatbot output — including the chatbot's own meta-commentary — directly into election articles.

**The scale of the problem: data from the conflict-of-interest queue**

- **[Nearly 1000 outstanding conflict of interest (COI) edit requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Nearly_1000_outstanding_COI_edit_requests)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Started as a routine "please help clear the backlog" post, but a reviewer's data breakdown is the most striking AI-related evidence this week: monthly conflict-of-interest edit requests have exploded from roughly 150/month historically to 737 in August 2026 alone, which reviewers attribute largely to AI making it trivially easy to generate "surficially passable" requests. Volunteer reviewer capacity hasn't kept pace, and the thread discusses this as a structural, not temporary, problem.

**Tooling and platform proposals**

- **[AI-assisted search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28idea_lab%29#AI-assisted_search)** (Idea Lab) — A substantial proposal (66 comments, 188 AI mentions) to build an AI-powered natural-language search assistant into Wikipedia's own search box, explicitly framed as a response to Wikipedia "losing visits" to AI chatbots. It's a slow-moving idea-lab discussion (no comment in over four days) rather than a live decision, but it's a notable example of the community debating whether to compete with AI by embracing it.
- **[Bot proposal: automatic addition of infobox for company articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Bot_proposal%3A_automatic_addition_of_infobox_for_company_articles)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A bot proposal to add infoboxes to ~12,000 company articles, which would use a large language model (Claude) as a safety filter to flag uncertain cases for human review. Even with heavy safeguards described, some editors are wary of mixing an LLM into any automated bot task given how contentious AI use has become.
- **[Newcomer tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28miscellaneous%29#Newcomer_tasks)** (Village Pump, miscellaneous) — By far the most active thread scanned (348 comments, 31 participants, still going), complaining that the automated system suggesting edits to new editors produces vague, sometimes nonsensical suggestions that create cleanup work rather than reducing it. Worth watching given the high count of AI-related terms in the discussion, though much of the visible debate concerns the recommendation algorithm generally rather than LLMs specifically.

## Other discussions worth attention

- **[Talk: Fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard#Talk%3A_Fiber)** (Administrators' noticeboard) — Despite the innocuous heading, this was a month-long, 320-comment conduct case against a long-term editor, "Roxy the dog," over abrasive and dismissive behavior toward other editors. It has just been closed with a full site ban — a significant sanction, now resolved.
- **[More airport disruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AAdministrators%27_noticeboard/Incidents#More_airport_disruption)** (Incidents noticeboard) — The largest live thread scanned (245 comments, 47 participants, last comment under 2 hours ago), a sprawling conduct dispute in the aviation topic area over sourcing standards for airport destination lists that has pulled in accusations of battleground behavior and forum-shopping on both sides. Still active and unresolved.
- **[RfC: Geographic disambiguation of school article titles in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28policy%29#RfC%3A_Geographic_disambiguation_of_school_article_titles_in_the_United_States)** (Village Pump, policy) — A large, currently very active naming-convention RfC (191 comments, last comment 7.5 hours ago), illustrating how much energy still goes into titling conventions even for non-controversial-seeming topics.
- **[RfC: Should the Lolicon article be characterized in relation to child sexual abuse material?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk%3ALolicon#RfC_Should_this_be_characterized_in_relation_to_CSAM%3F)** — A contentious, still-active RfC (109 comments) over how to frame a legally and ethically fraught topic in Wikipedia's voice.
- **[Page mover–specific move protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia%3AVillage_pump_%28proposals%29#Page_mover%E2%80%93specific_move_protection)** (Village Pump, proposals) — A substantial technical/process proposal (79 comments, 31 participants) about a new protection level tied to the "page mover" user right, relevant to anyone tracking how Wikipedia manages permissions creep.

**Pattern to flag:** across nearly every venue, AI shows up not as a single contained topic but as a thread running through policy-making (what should the rules say), enforcement (catching individual misuse), platform strategy (should Wikipedia build its own AI tools), and sheer workload (AI-generated content/requests overwhelming volunteers). The DavidLevinson case is unusual in combining several of these at once — a senior volunteer, suspected automated/AI-assisted spam, and a real governance consequence (the recall petition) — making it the single most consequential AI-adjacent story active right now.
